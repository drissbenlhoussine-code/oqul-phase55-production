**EDUCATIONAL #2**
The difference between a skin fade and a bald fade:

Skin fade → tapers down to your skin color gradually
Bald fade → goes completely down to zero, leaving no visible hair

Both are clean. The bald fade is sharper, more dramatic, and requires more maintenance. The skin fade grows out more naturally.

**EDUCATIONAL #3**
Stop washing your hair every day.

Here's why: Your scalp produces natural oils (sebum) that protect and nourish your hair. Daily shampoo strips those oils completely — then your scalp overcompensates by producing even more oil.

Result: Your hair gets oilier faster, your scalp dries out, and your hair becomes brittle.

Try washing 2–3 times per week max. Your hair will thank you.

**EDUCATIONAL #14**
Dandruff ≠ dry scalp. Here's the difference:

Dry scalp → small, white, powdery flakes. Caused by dehydration.
Dandruff → larger, oilier flakes. Caused by a yeast-like fungus (Malassezia).

Treatment is completely different. Dry scalp needs moisture. Dandruff needs an antifungal shampoo (look for zinc pyrithione or selenium sulfide).

Using the wrong one makes both conditions worse.

Beard growth myth: shaving makes your beard grow thicker and faster.

The science: FALSE.

Razor bumps vs. razor burn: the difference matters.

Razor burn → redness and irritation immediately after shaving → caused by dull blade, wrong technique, dry shaving

Bumps → exfoliate, use chemical exfoliant (salicylic acid), don't pick

**EDUCATIONAL #50**
How heat affects your hair:

Hair is made of protein (keratin). Heat above 450°F begins to break down the protein bonds — permanently.

This is why:
→ Blow dryers on high heat cause long-term damage
→ Flat irons without heat protectant fry the hair
→ Consistent heat styling leads to breakage and thinning over time

Use heat at medium settings. Always use protectant. Let your hair air dry when possible.

**EDUCATIONAL #62**
Straight razor vs. safety razor: a practical comparison.

Straight razor (at the shop):
→ Closest shave possible
→ Requires professional technique
→ Best for detail work and edges

Safety razor (at home):
→ Single blade, much less irritation than multi-blade
→ Close shave, less aggressive
→ Learning curve, but worth it

Multi-blade cartridge razors (what most men use):
→ Convenient but pulls and cuts multiple times per pass → more irritation

4. First pass: with the grain → removes bulk

7. Cold towel → closes pores
8. Aftershave balm → soothes and seals

This is not what a disposable razor does. This is a ritual.

**EDUCATIONAL #79**
The chemistry of hair color:

Natural hair color comes from melanin:
→ Eumelanin → dark pigment (black/brown)
→ Pheomelanin → light pigment (red/blonde)

When you color hair, developer (hydrogen peroxide) opens the cuticle and either removes existing pigment or deposits new pigment.

The percentage of developer determines how much the cuticle is opened:
10 vol → deposits, minimal lift
20 vol → 1–2 levels of lift
30 vol → 2–3 levels of lift
40 vol → maximum lift (also maximum damage)

Washing your beard with regular soap:
→ Strips natural oils
→ Causes dryness and irritation
→ Breaks down the cuticle
→ Leads to beard itch and flaking

Use a dedicated beard wash with balanced pH. It's not a luxury — it's maintenance.
