## SECTION 1 — KPI DEFINITIONS

### Revenue KPIs

**ADR — Average Daily Rate**
Total revenue from accommodation (excluding cleaning fees and other charges) ÷ Total nights booked.
*Example: €7,200 revenue from 60 booked nights = €120 ADR*

**RevPAN — Revenue Per Available Night**
Total accommodation revenue ÷ Total available nights (including empty nights).
*Example: €7,200 over 90 available nights = €80 RevPAN*
RevPAN is more meaningful than ADR because it accounts for vacancy. A property with €150 ADR at 40% occupancy earns less than a property with €100 ADR at 80% occupancy.

**Occupancy Rate**
Booked nights ÷ Available nights × 100.
*Example: 60 booked nights ÷ 90 available nights = 66.7% occupancy*
Block-off nights (maintenance, personal use) reduce available nights and therefore improve the occupancy rate metric without changing actual revenue. Count them honestly or exclude them from the denominator to track true demand.

**ALOS — Average Length of Stay**
Total booked nights ÷ Number of bookings.
*Example: 60 booked nights from 22 bookings = 2.73 ALOS*
Higher ALOS reduces turnover frequency and cleaning costs. A single 7-night booking at €90/night generates the same revenue as 3.5 bookings at the same rate, with one cleaning cost instead of 3.5.

**Booking Lead Time**
Average number of days between booking date and check-in date, across all bookings.
*Example: Guest books 24 days before arrival on average = 24-day lead time*
Lead time below 7 days signals last-minute demand — price these periods at -20% to -30% for vacancy management. Lead time above 60 days means guests plan ahead — you can apply seasonal premiums early.

### Cost KPIs

**CPON — Cost Per Occupied Night**
Total operating costs (fixed + variable, excluding one-time setup) ÷ Total booked nights.
*Example: €6,672 annual costs ÷ 277 booked nights = €24.08 CPON*
Reducing CPON comes from either increasing booked nights (fixed costs spread thinner) or reducing variable costs per turnover (negotiate cleaning rates, reduce consumable spend).

**Cleaning Cost Recovery Rate**
Cleaning fee collected from guests ÷ Actual cleaning costs paid.
*Example: €7,800 collected ÷ €8,760 paid = 89% recovery*
At 100%+ recovery rate, guests are fully subsidising your cleaning operation. At below 80%, cleaning is eating into margin. Adjust your cleaning fee or negotiate lower cleaning rates.

### Profitability KPIs

**Gross Profit Margin**
(Gross Revenue − Airbnb Fee − Variable Costs) ÷ Gross Revenue × 100.

**Net Operating Profit**
Gross Revenue − Airbnb Fees − Fixed Costs − Variable Costs − One-Time Setup Costs.
This is what your property earns before mortgage, rent, or financing costs.

**Monthly Average Net**
Net Operating Profit ÷ 12. The headline number most hosts care about. For the projected model in `01-HOST-BUSINESS-PLAN.md`, this is €1,899/month in Year 1 and €2,095/month in Year 2+.

**Payback Period**
One-time setup costs ÷ Monthly Net Operating Profit.
*Example: €1,150 setup ÷ €1,899/month = 0.6 months to payback*

|---|---|---|---|
| Annual Occupancy | Below 65% | 65–80% | Above 80% |

## SECTION 4 — TAX GUIDE BY COUNTRY

*This section is for general information only. Tax rules change annually. Consult a local accountant or tax adviser before filing.*

### Portugal

**Tax regime:** Rental income is taxed under Category F (property income) on the IRS (personal income tax return).

**Rate:** 28% flat rate OR you can opt to be taxed at your progressive rate (25–48% depending on total income). For most hosts, the 28% flat rate is more favourable.

**Expense deductions permitted:** Condominium fees, maintenance, insurance, depreciation (2% per year of the purchase price of the building, excluding land), utilities if paid by you, Airbnb commission (3%), platform fees.

**Alojamento Local (AL) licence:** Required for short-term rental in Portugal. Register at the local Câmara Municipal or via Balcão do Empreendedor. Mandatory for listing on Airbnb in Portugal.

**VAT:** AL properties with turnover under €15,000/year are VAT exempt. Above €15,000, VAT registration may be required — consult an accountant.

**IRS filing deadline:** April 30 of the following year for Category F income.

---

### Spain

**Tax regime:** Rental income taxed as capital income (rendimientos del capital inmobiliario) on the IRPF declaration. If the host provides hotel-like services (daily cleaning, reception, meals), it is reclassified as economic activity income.

**Rate:** 19% for EU residents on net income. Non-EU/non-EEA residents: 24% flat on gross income.

**Expense deductions (EU residents):** Mortgage interest, depreciation (3% per year on the construction value), repairs, insurance, management fees, Airbnb commission, utilities paid by you.

**Licencia de Apartamento Turístico:** Required in most autonomous communities. Rules vary significantly by region (Barcelona, Madrid, and Valencia have strict quotas and requirements). Confirm your local rules before listing.

**Filing deadline:** May 30 of the following year.

---

### France

**Tax regime:** Meublé de Tourisme classification applies to furnished tourist rentals. Two regimes available:
- **Micro-BIC:** Applies if turnover below €72,600/year. Standard abatement of 50% (only 50% of revenue is taxable). Simplest option for most hosts.
- **Régime Réel:** Deduct actual expenses if they exceed 50% of revenue. Requires accounting records.

**Rate:** Standard income tax rates apply (marginal rate from 0% to 45% depending on total household income) after the abatement.

**Déclaration en mairie:** Furnished tourist rentals must be registered with the local town hall (mairie). Some cities (Paris, Lyon, Bordeaux) require a changement d'usage permit to rent a primary or secondary home short-term.

**TVA:** Exempt for meublé de tourisme without hotel-like services and turnover below €91,900.

**Filing deadline:** Variable; typically April or May for the preceding year. Check impots.gouv.fr annually.

---

### Germany

**Tax regime:** Rental income is classified as Einkünfte aus Vermietung und Verpachtung (income from renting and leasing) and added to other income, taxed at the progressive income tax rate (14%–45%).

**Expense deductions:** Depreciation (Absetzung für Abnutzung — AfA) at 2% per year on the building value, maintenance, interest on loans, insurance, Airbnb commission.

**Short-term rental permit:** Many German cities have introduced Zweckentfremdungsverbot (prohibition on converting residential property to tourist use). Berlin, Munich, Hamburg, and Frankfurt require permits for entire-apartment rentals. Fines for non-compliance are substantial (up to €500,000 in Berlin). Confirm your local rules.

**Umsatzsteuer (VAT):** If annual turnover exceeds €22,000 (Kleinunternehmerregelung threshold), VAT registration required. Hosts under this threshold are exempt.

**Filing deadline:** July 31 of the following year (or February 28 with a tax adviser — Steuerberater).

---

### Netherlands

**Tax regime:** Short-term rental income is taxed in Box 3 (savings and investment income) if the property is an investment property, or Box 1 (income from work and residence) if providing hotel-like services. Box 3 taxes the deemed return on property value (not actual income), at a flat 36% on the deemed return.

**Short-term rental permit:** Amsterdam, Utrecht, and other cities require a verhuisvergunning (rental permit) and cap the number of days per year a primary residence can be rented (Amsterdam: 30 days/year). Consult your local gemeente.

**Filing deadline:** April 30 of the following year (May 1 deadline online).

---

### United Kingdom

**Tax regime:** Rental income is taxed as property income (Schedule A). The government's Rent a Room scheme allows up to £7,500/year tax-free for hosts renting a room in their primary home; this does NOT apply to entire property lets.

**Furnished Holiday Lettings (FHL) regime:** Historically provided capital gains and income tax advantages. As of April 2025, the FHL regime was abolished; rental income is now taxed under standard property income rules.

**Expense deductions:** Mortgage interest relief is now limited to a 20% tax credit (standard rate). Allowable: maintenance, repairs, insurance, agent fees, Airbnb commission, utilities if paid by you.

**Rate:** Income tax at your marginal rate (20%, 40%, or 45% depending on total income). National Insurance may apply if letting is treated as a trade.

**Permitted Development Rights / Planning Permission:** In London, entire properties can be let for no more than 90 days per calendar year without planning permission. Other UK cities have different rules — confirm with your local planning authority.

**HMRC self-assessment filing deadline:** January 31 online for the previous tax year (April 5 year-end).

---

### Czech Republic

**Tax regime:** Rental income taxed under §9 of the Income Tax Act (příjmy z pronájmu). 15% flat rate for individuals. Hosts can deduct either actual expenses or a flat 30% expense lump sum (výdajový paušál).

**Registration:** Short-term rentals may require a živnostenský list (trade licence) if the activity is systematic. Confirm with a local accountant. Prague requires registration via the Prague City Hall portal.

**Filing deadline:** April 1 of the following year (June 30 with a tax adviser).

---

### Belgium

**Tax regime:** For furnished rentals to private individuals, income is taxed under précompte immobilier (cadastral income regime). The taxable base is the cadastral income (indexed by a factor of 1.7 for furnished rentals), not the actual rent received. This is often significantly lower than actual income.

**Short-term rental registration:** Brussels, Flanders, and Wallonia each have their own short-term rental regulations. Flanders requires registration and compliance with safety norms. Confirm your regional rules.

**Filing deadline:** June 30 of the following year.

---

### General Tax Principles Across All Markets

1. **Separate bank account:** Keep all rental income and expenses in a dedicated bank account. This makes P&L tracking accurate and simplifies tax filing.
2. **Invoice retention:** Keep all supplier invoices (cleaning, maintenance, supplies) for a minimum of 7 years. Tax authorities can audit any year within this window.
3. **Airbnb income documentation:** Download your Airbnb Transaction History CSV for every calendar year by January 15 of the following year. This is the authoritative income record.
4. **Local accountant:** A local accountant familiar with STR taxation typically costs €300–€600/year and saves 2–3× that in correctly claimed deductions, especially in Year 1.
