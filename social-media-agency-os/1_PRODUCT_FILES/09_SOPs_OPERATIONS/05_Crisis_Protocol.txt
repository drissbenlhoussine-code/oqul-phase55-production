PURPOSE
───────
A step-by-step crisis response plan for 6 common social media emergencies.
When a crisis hits, you don't have time to think — you execute this protocol.

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 1: CRISIS SEVERITY LEVELS
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

LEVEL 1 — MINOR INCIDENT (Handle yourself, notify client)
  Examples:
  - Typo or factual error in a published post
  - Single negative comment that's gaining traction
  - Wrong photo or caption published accidentally
  - Scheduling error (post went live at wrong time)
  
  Response time: Within 2 hours
  Action: Fix, apologize if needed, inform client same day

LEVEL 2 — MODERATE CRISIS (Escalate to client within 1 hour)
  Examples:
  - Multiple negative comments on one post
  - A customer service complaint going viral in comments
  - Offensive or insensitive post published by mistake
  - Competitor or third party publicly calling out the brand
  - A post or campaign misunderstood by audience at scale

  Response time: Within 1 hour
  Action: Pause all scheduled content, assess, client call, respond

LEVEL 3 — FULL CRISIS (Emergency — drop everything)
  Examples:
  - Brand mentioned in major news story (negative)
  - Hate campaign or coordinated pile-on
  - Data breach or security incident announced publicly
  - Executive/spokesperson involved in scandal
  - Product recall or safety issue trending on social

  Response time: Within 30 minutes
  Action: STOP ALL CONTENT. Crisis team assembled. See Section 3.

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 2: 30-MINUTE CRISIS RESPONSE CHECKLIST
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

THE FIRST 30 MINUTES — CRITICAL WINDOW

  MINUTE 0–5: ASSESS
  □ Screenshot everything (evidence before deletion)
  □ Identify: What is the crisis? Where is it happening?
  □ Determine severity level (1, 2, or 3)
  □ Do NOT post anything yet
  □ Do NOT delete original post yet (may escalate "why did they delete?")
  □ Notify client/brand contact immediately via phone or Slack

  MINUTE 5–10: PAUSE
  □ Log in to scheduling tool
  □ PAUSE ALL scheduled posts immediately
  □ Put all campaigns on hold
  □ Do NOT run any paid ads during crisis
  □ Notify any team members working on this account

  MINUTE 10–20: EVALUATE
  □ How many people have seen/engaged with the crisis content?
  □ Is it spreading to other platforms?
  □ Is media or influencers involved?
  □ Is there a legal risk? (If yes → client contacts lawyer ASAP)
  □ What is the root cause?
  □ What does the audience expect the brand to do?
  □ Who should the official response come from? (Brand, CEO, department?)

  MINUTE 20–30: DRAFT RESPONSE
  □ Write initial holding statement (see templates in Section 4)
  □ Client reviews and approves statement
  □ Post to affected platform(s)
  □ Determine if the post should be deleted or left up with a response
  □ Prepare FAQ for common questions coming in DMs/comments
  □ Set up monitoring: check every 15 minutes for next 4 hours

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 3: SIX CRISIS TYPES — RESPONSE PLANS
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

CRISIS TYPE 1: OFFENSIVE/INSENSITIVE POST PUBLISHED BY MISTAKE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This could be a tone-deaf caption, wrong post sent to wrong account,
or content that reads badly in current cultural context.

Response Plan:
  Step 1: Take immediate screenshot
  Step 2: Delete post (in this case, delete quickly before it spreads)
  Step 3: Post a genuine apology (not a "sorry you were offended" — a real one)
  Step 4: In your apology: acknowledge what happened, own it, commit to better
  Step 5: Turn off comments if the ratio is extreme (only if necessary)
  Step 6: Don't over-explain or argue in comments
  Step 7: Internal debrief: How did this pass QC? Update content approval process.

CRISIS TYPE 2: NEGATIVE REVIEW/COMMENT GOING VIRAL
────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A customer complaint or critical post gaining shares, likes, and secondary commentary.

Response Plan:
  Step 1: DO NOT ignore or delete the comment
  Step 2: Respond publicly with empathy + action:
          "We're so sorry to hear this, [Name]. This isn't the experience
          we want for our customers. Please DM us [or email X] so we can
          make this right immediately."
  Step 3: Take the conversation to private (DM/email) — resolve it there
  Step 4: If you resolve it: ask the customer to update their comment
  Step 5: Do not engage with pile-on commenters — respond once, calmly
  Step 6: If it's a verified bad experience: acknowledge it openly
  Step 7: Follow-up post (if warranted): "Here's what we changed."

CRISIS TYPE 3: HACKED ACCOUNT
───────────────────────────────

Signs: Posts you didn't make, password change notification, locked out.

Response Plan:
  MINUTE 0:   Alert client immediately. This is Level 3.
  Step 1: Attempt to recover account via platform's official recovery flow
  Step 2: Post to OTHER platforms: "Our [Platform] account has been compromised.
           We are working to recover it. Do not engage with recent posts."
  Step 3: Contact platform support (Meta, TikTok, LinkedIn)
  Step 4: Change passwords on ALL connected accounts + email
  Step 5: Enable 2FA on recovery email before re-accessing socials
  Step 6: Revoke access for all third-party apps on compromised account
  Step 7: Conduct full security audit of all connected tools
  Step 8: Post account recovery announcement with context

CRISIS TYPE 4: PRODUCT/SERVICE COMPLAINT CAMPAIGN
────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Multiple customers posting the same complaint simultaneously — could
indicate a real product/service issue or coordinated campaign.

Response Plan:
  Step 1: Determine if complaints are legitimate (real customers) or bots
  Step 2: If legitimate → pause all marketing content immediately
  Step 3: Post acknowledgment: "We've seen your feedback about [X].
           We take this seriously and are looking into it right now."
  Step 4: Privately reach out to each complainant
  Step 5: Coordinate with client's customer service team for resolution
  Step 6: Post resolution/update within 24–48 hours
  Step 7: If issue is large → consider proactive press release/statement

CRISIS TYPE 5: PLATFORM OUTAGE CAUSING MISSED POSTS/CAMPAIGNS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Instagram/Facebook/TikTok goes down during a major campaign launch.

Response Plan:
  Step 1: Check DownDetector.com to confirm platform outage
  Step 2: Do NOT republish immediately when platform comes back (algorithm chaos)
  Step 3: Wait 2–4 hours after platform recovery before posting
  Step 4: Inform client: "Platform outage confirmed. Campaign delayed, not canceled."
  Step 5: Adjust scheduled content timeline
  Step 6: If time-sensitive: post to alternative platforms first
  Step 7: Document for client report (this is NOT your fault)

CRISIS TYPE 6: VIRAL BACKLASH ON A CAMPAIGN
─────────────────────────────────────────────

A campaign you carefully planned lands badly with the audience at scale.

Response Plan:
  Step 1: Stop all campaign promotion immediately
  Step 2: Assess whether to keep content up or remove:
          Keep up if: Backlash is from a small vocal minority
          Remove if: Widespread negative reaction + media attention
  Step 3: Post a genuine, specific response (not PR-speak)
  Step 4: Engage with feedback constructively
  Step 5: Brief client on what happened and what you're doing
  Step 6: Redesign or pause the campaign
  Step 7: Post a "what we heard + what we're changing" update if warranted

SECTION 4: FIVE CRISIS RESPONSE SCRIPTS
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SCRIPT 1 — GENERAL HOLDING STATEMENT
(Use when you need to say something fast but don't have full information yet)

  "We've seen the conversation happening around [topic/post/situation] and
  want you to know we're taking this seriously. We're looking into it right
  now and will have a full response within [timeframe]. Thank you for bringing
  this to our attention — it matters to us."

SCRIPT 2 — APOLOGY FOR INSENSITIVE POST

  "We made a mistake. [Post/image/caption] that we published today was [tone-deaf /
  insensitive / factually wrong], and we've removed it. We sincerely apologize
  to anyone who was hurt or offended. This does not reflect who we are or what
  we stand for. We're reviewing our content approval process to make sure this
  doesn't happen again. Thank you for holding us accountable."

SCRIPT 3 — CUSTOMER COMPLAINT RESPONSE

  "We're really sorry to hear about your experience, [Name]. This isn't the
  level of service we're known for, and we want to make it right. Could you
  please DM us or email [address] with your order/account details? Someone
  from our team will reach out to you directly today."

SCRIPT 4 — HACKED ACCOUNT ANNOUNCEMENT

  "Important notice: Our [platform] account was temporarily compromised
  earlier today. We have since regained control and secured the account.
  Any posts made between [time] and [time] were not from us. We apologize
  for any confusion and have reported the incident to [platform]. Your
  security is our priority."

SCRIPT 5 — POST-CRISIS WRAP-UP STATEMENT

  "Over the past [timeframe], many of you shared concerns about [topic].
  We heard you. Here's what we've done: [action 1], [action 2], [action 3].
  We're committed to doing better. Thank you to everyone who spoke up —
  your feedback makes us a better [company/brand]."

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 5: POST-CRISIS DEBRIEF (24–48 Hours Later)
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Complete this with your client within 48 hours of crisis resolution:

  □ What was the root cause of the crisis?
  □ How long did it take us to respond? (Target: < 1 hour)
  □ Was our response effective? (Audience reaction positive/negative?)
  □ What would we do differently?
  □ What processes need to change to prevent recurrence?
  □ Are there any ongoing reputation concerns to monitor?
  □ Do we need to continue monitoring for the next 30 days?
  □ Any legal follow-up required?

POLICY UPDATES TO MAKE AFTER A CRISIS:
  → Update content approval checklist to catch similar issues
  → Add new trigger words to the content review process
  → Brief any team members or contractors on what happened
  → Update the "sensitive topics to avoid" section of content brief
  → Strengthen social listening (set up alerts for brand name mentions)

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SECTION 6: PREVENTIVE MEASURES
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Avoid crises before they happen:

  □ Social listening tool set up (Mention, Brand24, or Google Alerts)
  □ Content review checklist followed for every post
  □ Sensitive dates and news events checked before posting
  □ Two-person review for major campaigns
  □ All team/contractor accounts secured with 2FA
  □ Shared passwords stored in a password manager (LastPass, 1Password)
  □ Crisis plan shared with client before one is needed
  □ Emergency contact list maintained:
      → Client's direct phone number
      → Client's PR contact (if they have one)
      → Client's lawyer (for legal crises)
      → Platform support contacts bookmarked
