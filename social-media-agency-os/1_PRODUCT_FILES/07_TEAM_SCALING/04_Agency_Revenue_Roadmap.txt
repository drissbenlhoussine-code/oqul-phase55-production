OVERVIEW
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Every agency owner starts alone and overworked. The ones who break through
to $30K/month aren't necessarily smarter — they systematize, delegate, and
price confidently at each phase. This roadmap shows you what each phase looks
like, what changes, and what mistakes keep people stuck.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
PHASE 1 — THE FREELANCER PHASE: $5K–$8K/MONTH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Client Count:      3-5 clients
Package Price:     $1,000-$1,800/month
Doing yourself:    Everything (strategy, content, scheduling, reporting)
Hours/week:        40-55 hours
Tools needed:      Canva, scheduling tool, Google Drive, basic invoicing

Revenue math: 5 clients × $1,200/month = $6,000/month

WHAT PHASE 1 LOOKS LIKE:
You're doing everything. Monday: content planning. Tuesday-Thursday: content creation.
Friday: scheduling, client comms. Every day: community management.
It's unsustainable at this level and that's normal — this is the apprenticeship.

WHAT TO FOCUS ON IN PHASE 1:
• Land 3-5 clients at $1,000-$1,500/month
• Systematize your workflow so every client gets the same process
• Build 3 strong case studies with real data
• Stop undercharging — raise rates by $200-$300 every 3-4 clients

DANGER SIGNS YOU'RE STUCK IN PHASE 1:
• Still at $5-8K/month after 12+ months
• Taking on more clients instead of raising rates
• Working 50+ hours consistently with no team
• Saying yes to every client regardless of fit

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
PHASE 2 — THE SOLO AGENCY PHASE: $8K–$15K/MONTH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Client Count:      5-8 clients
Package Price:     $1,500-$2,500/month
First hire:        Copywriter OR Designer (part-time contractor)
Hours/week (you):  35-45 hours (freeing 10-15 from Phase 1 via delegation)

Revenue math: 7 clients × $1,800/month = $12,600/month
Contractor cost: ~$1,500-$2,500/month
Net income: ~$10,000-$11,000/month

WHO TO HIRE FIRST AND WHY:
Hire a copywriter first if: Your biggest time drain is caption writing
Hire a designer first if: You're spending more time in Canva than anywhere else

When you should NOT hire yet: If you don't have consistent clients — you need
the revenue stability first.

TRIGGER TO MOVE TO PHASE 3:
You've handed off 30-40% of production to a contractor, you're at 7+ clients,
and you're back to feeling fully booked again despite having help.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
PHASE 3 — THE GROWING AGENCY: $15K–$22K/MONTH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Client Count:      8-12 clients
Package Price:     $2,000-$3,500/month
Team:              2-3 contractors (copywriter + designer + possibly community manager)
Hours/week (you):  30-40 hours (more on strategy, sales, and client relationships)

Revenue math: 10 clients × $2,200/month = $22,000/month
Team cost: ~$4,000-$6,000/month
Net income: ~$16,000-$18,000/month

WHAT CHANGES IN PHASE 3:
• You're reviewing work, not always creating it
• SOPs and quality checklists become non-negotiable
• Client experience is your biggest differentiator — invest in it
• You need a project management system (Notion, Asana, ClickUp) now, not later
• Start positioning the business as an agency, not "you" personally

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
PHASE 4 — THE SCALABLE AGENCY: $22K–$30K/MONTH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Client Count:      12-18 clients
Package Price:     $2,500-$5,000/month
Team:              Account Manager + Content Creator + Copywriter + Community Manager
Hours/week (you):  20-30 hours (CEO role: sales, strategy, team management)

Revenue math: 15 clients × $2,500/month = $37,500/month
Team cost: ~$8,000-$12,000/month
Net income: ~$25,000-$30,000/month

THE KEY TO PHASE 4:
An Account Manager who owns client relationships. This is the hire that
unlocks the ceiling. Without it, you're the bottleneck.
Your Account Manager runs: client calls, monthly reports, revision reviews.
You focus on: sales, strategy, team leadership, product development.

Profit margins to target:
• Phase 1: 80-90% (doing everything yourself)
• Phase 2: 65-75% (first contractor)
• Phase 3: 55-65% (small team)
• Phase 4: 45-55% (full team — but much higher total income)

5 MISTAKES THAT KEEP AGENCIES STUCK
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

MISTAKE 1: Undercharging and overdelivering
Fix: Know your cost structure. If your hourly effective rate is under $75, raise prices.

MISTAKE 2: Taking any client at any price
Fix: Qualify hard. Low-budget clients always demand more time and generate less revenue per hour.

MISTAKE 3: Not systemizing until it's too late
Fix: Document every process from Day 1 — even when you're doing it alone. Your future team needs it.

MISTAKE 4: Staying in production too long
Fix: Hire before you feel ready. If you wait until you're drowning, you'll hire wrong in a rush.

MISTAKE 5: Building a job, not a business
Fix: Your agency should be able to operate for one week without you in Phase 4. If it can't, you don't own a business — you own a job with more stress.
