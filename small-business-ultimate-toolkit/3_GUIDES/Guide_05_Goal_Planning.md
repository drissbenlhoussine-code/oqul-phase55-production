# Guide 05: Business Goal Planning
## How to Use Your Goal Planner and Monthly Business Review for Real Growth

Most small business owners are so focused on the day-to-day that they rarely stop to think strategically about where their business is actually headed. This guide changes that. The tools in this section — your Business Goal Planner and Monthly Business Review — are designed to help you set clear goals, track meaningful progress, and make smarter decisions over time.

---

## Part 1: The 90-Day Planning Framework

### Why 90-Day Goals Outperform Yearly Resolutions

Annual goals are too far away to feel urgent and too vague to act on. Most people set them in January and forget them by March. Ninety days, on the other hand, is long enough to make meaningful progress but short enough to stay motivated and focused.

Working in 90-day cycles also forces you to prioritize. You cannot do everything at once, so this framework helps you identify what will move the needle most in the next three months.

### How to Set SMART Business Goals

SMART is a well-established framework for goal setting. Every goal you set should meet these five criteria:

- **Specific:** Clearly define what you want to achieve. "Grow my business" is not specific. "Add 50 new email subscribers" is.
- **Measurable:** Include a number or metric so you can track progress. How will you know when you've succeeded?
- **Achievable:** Ambitious goals are good; unrealistic ones are discouraging. Be honest about what's possible in 90 days given your current resources and time.
- **Relevant:** Does this goal connect to your bigger business vision? Will achieving it matter six months from now?
- **Time-bound:** Set a deadline. The 90-day framework handles this for you — your goal must be achieved within the quarter.

**Example of a SMART business goal:**
"Increase monthly revenue from $1,200 to $2,000 by the end of this 90-day period by launching one new service offering and running one promotional campaign."

### Breaking Big Goals Into Action Steps

Once your 90-day goal is set, work backwards to create a roadmap:

1. Identify three to four major milestones that lead to the goal
2. Assign each milestone to a specific month within the quarter
3. Break each milestone into weekly tasks
4. Identify any resources, skills, or support you'll need

This top-down approach prevents overwhelm and keeps you moving forward.

### How to Stay Accountable

- Share your goals with someone you trust — a business partner, coach, or peer group
- Review your goal every Monday morning before you start work
- Use the weekly priorities section of your planner to stay on track
- Schedule a mid-quarter check-in at day 45 to assess progress and adjust if needed

## Part 2: Business Goal Planner (12_Business_Goal_Planner.csv)

### Overview of All Sections

The Business Goal Planner is organized into five interconnected sections:
1. **90-Day Goals** — your big-picture quarterly targets
2. **Monthly Goals** — the milestones that build toward each 90-day goal
3. **Weekly Priorities** — the specific actions you'll focus on each week
4. **KPI Tracker** — key performance indicators to monitor regularly
5. **Action Plan** — detailed task lists with owners, due dates, and status

### How to Fill In the 90-Day Goals Section

Open the file and navigate to the 90-Day Goals tab or section. For each goal:
- Write a clear, SMART goal statement
- Note the start and end date of your quarter
- Identify why this goal matters (your "why" keeps you motivated)
- Write down one potential obstacle and how you'll overcome it

Limit yourself to one to three 90-day goals. Focus is more powerful than volume.

### How to Break Goals Into Monthly Milestones

Each 90-day goal should have three monthly milestones — one per month. These are the checkpoints that tell you whether you're on track.

Example:
- **90-Day Goal:** Launch an online store with 20 products
- **Month 1 Milestone:** Choose platform, design store, set up payment processing
- **Month 2 Milestone:** Create and photograph all 20 products, write descriptions
- **Month 3 Milestone:** Test, launch, and promote the store

### Using the Weekly Priorities Section

Each week, identify your top three priorities — the tasks that will most directly advance your monthly milestone. Write them at the top of the weekly section before anything else. These are your non-negotiables for the week.

### Setting Up Your KPIs

KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) are the numbers that tell you how your business is performing. Choose three to six KPIs to track each month. See Part 3 below for examples by business type.

Enter your KPI targets at the start of the quarter, then update actual results monthly.

### Using the Action Plan Effectively

The Action Plan section is your task management area. For each goal, list every task required, along with:
- Who is responsible (even if it's just you)
- The due date
- Status: Not started / In progress / Complete

Review and update this list weekly.

## Part 3: KPI Examples by Business Type

Choosing the right KPIs depends on what kind of business you run. Here are practical examples to get you started.

### Product-Based Business KPIs
- Monthly revenue
- Number of orders
- Average order value
- Return/refund rate
- Cost of goods sold (COGS) percentage
- Inventory turnover rate

### Service-Based Business KPIs
- Number of active clients
- Monthly recurring revenue
- Client retention rate
- New inquiries per month
- Proposal-to-client conversion rate
- Hours billed vs. hours worked

### Digital Product Seller KPIs
- Number of units sold
- Revenue per product
- Email list growth rate
- Conversion rate (visitors to buyers)
- Traffic sources
- Customer lifetime value

### Freelancer KPIs
- Monthly billable hours
- Effective hourly rate (total revenue divided by total hours worked)
- Number of active projects
- New client inquiries
- Client satisfaction / testimonials received
- Months of revenue in reserve

## Part 4: Monthly Business Review (08_Monthly_Business_Review.csv)

### Why Reviewing Your Month Matters

Without a regular review, it's easy to keep repeating the same patterns — working hard without seeing what's actually working and what isn't. The Monthly Business Review gives you a structured way to look back before you look forward.

Think of it less like a performance review and more like a navigation check. You're not judging yourself; you're adjusting course.

### How to Schedule Your Monthly Review

Set a recurring appointment on the last day of each month (or the first working day of the new month). Block at least 60 minutes and treat it like a client meeting — don't cancel it.

Create a ritual around it if that helps. Some business owners do their monthly review with a coffee at a quiet café; others prefer a dedicated workspace at home.

### What to Cover in Your Monthly Review

Work through each section of the Monthly Business Review spreadsheet:

1. **Revenue and Expenses:** Compare actual numbers to your goals. Note any surprises.
2. **KPI Review:** Update your KPI tracker with last month's results.
3. **Wins:** List at least three things that went well this month. This is not optional.
4. **Challenges:** What didn't go as planned? What held you back?
5. **Lessons Learned:** What will you do differently next month?
6. **Next Month Focus:** Based on your review, what are your top three priorities for next month?

### How to Use Review Insights to Adjust Next Month's Goals

Your monthly review informs your monthly goals — and those monthly goals should always connect back to your 90-day plan. After each review, ask yourself:

- Am I on track to hit my 90-day goal?
- Do I need to accelerate or adjust my approach?
- Are there tasks I should deprioritize to focus on what's working?

This creates a feedback loop that continuously improves your planning.

### Connecting Your Review to Your 90-Day Plan

At the end of Month 1 and Month 2, use your review to assess your quarterly progress. At the end of Month 3, conduct a full quarter review:
- Did you achieve your 90-day goals?
- What contributed most to your progress?
- What will you carry forward into next quarter?
- What new goals will you set for the next 90 days?

## Part 5: Creating a Growth Mindset

### Celebrating Wins (Even Small Ones)

Progress is not always dramatic. Your first sale, your first repeat customer, completing a financial system, finishing a week of consistent posting — these all count. Celebrating small wins trains your brain to associate effort with reward, which sustains motivation over time.

Write your wins down. Keep a "wins journal" or simply use the Wins column in your monthly review. Revisit it when things feel hard.

### Learning From What Didn't Work

A goal that wasn't reached is not a failure — it's information. When something doesn't go as planned, get curious rather than critical:
- What specifically didn't work?
- Was the goal unrealistic, or did execution fall short?
- What would you do differently?
- What did you learn that will help you next quarter?

The most successful business owners fail often and learn faster than everyone else.

### Adjusting Without Quitting

There is a difference between quitting and pivoting. Quitting means abandoning a goal because it feels hard. Pivoting means adjusting your approach based on real evidence. Always be willing to adjust your tactics while staying committed to your goals.

If a goal no longer makes sense — because circumstances changed, not because it's difficult — it is perfectly acceptable to revise it. The planner is a tool, not a contract.

### Building Momentum Quarter by Quarter

Ninety days from now, you will have completed one full planning cycle. Use that experience to set better goals in the next cycle. The process compounds:
- Quarter 1: You learn how to plan and track
- Quarter 2: You set more realistic, focused goals
- Quarter 3: You start to see patterns in what works
- Quarter 4: You're operating with significantly more clarity and confidence than when you started

Trust the process. Show up consistently. Adjust as needed. That is how real business growth happens.
