Why? Because brushing only cleans about 60% of your tooth surface.

The other 40% is between your teeth — where only floss or a water flosser can reach.

One minute of flossing per day prevents years of dental problems. It really is that simple.

**#2**
The bacteria in your mouth don't sleep.

They're most active while you're sleeping — which is exactly why brushing before bed matters more than brushing in the morning.

If you skip one brush: skip the morning one. Never skip the night one.

---

**#3**
You've probably heard you should replace your toothbrush every 3 months.

Here's why: after 3 months, the bristles are frayed enough that they're cleaning at 50% efficiency. You're still brushing — but not really cleaning.

Watch for: splayed bristles, any color change, or after any illness. Replace immediately.

---

**#4**
Myth: Whitening damages your enamel.

Fact: Professional whitening, done correctly, does not damage enamel.

The sensitivity you sometimes feel is temporary and comes from the whitening agent affecting nerve endings — not from enamel erosion.

The key words: done correctly. Over-the-counter products used too often? Different story.

Questions about safe whitening? DM us or give us a call.

---

**#5**
The link between gum disease and heart disease is not a coincidence.

Research shows that the bacteria that cause gum inflammation can enter your bloodstream and contribute to inflammation in blood vessels.

Oral health is whole-body health. Your mouth is not separate from the rest of you.

---

**#6**
Why does coffee stain teeth but tea stains MORE?

Tea has higher levels of tannins — compounds that attach to the protein film on your enamel and cause discoloration.

Black tea is the biggest culprit. Green and white teas are gentler.

Drinking through a straw or rinsing with water after either one helps more than most people realize.

---

**#7**
Your toothbrush is not a power tool.

Pressing harder does NOT clean better. It actually causes:
• Enamel erosion over time
• Gum recession
• Sensitivity that gets worse with age

The right pressure: if your bristles are splaying out sideways, you're pressing too hard.

---

**#8**
A dental cleaning isn't just about clean teeth.

During every hygiene appointment, we're also checking:
▸ Signs of oral cancer
▸ Gum health and bone loss
▸ Early cavity detection (before it needs a filling)
▸ Signs of grinding or clenching
▸ Changes since your last visit

It's a health screening. Not a cleaning service.

---

**#9**
Dry mouth isn't just uncomfortable — it's damaging.

Saliva neutralizes acids, washes away bacteria, and strengthens enamel. Without enough of it, cavities form faster, bad breath worsens, and gum disease accelerates.

Common causes: certain medications, dehydration, mouth breathing, caffeine.

If you have persistent dry mouth, mention it at your next visit. We can help.

---

**#10**
Fluoride isn't just for kids.

Adults benefit from fluoride just as much — especially adults who:
• Take medications that cause dry mouth
• Have a history of frequent cavities
• Drink mostly bottled water (no fluoride)
• Are undergoing chemotherapy

It's not outdated. It's one of the most evidence-backed preventive tools in dentistry.

---

**#11**
The right order: floss THEN brush.

Flossing first loosens the plaque and debris between teeth. Brushing then sweeps it away.

Most people do it backward. The difference in effectiveness is measurable.

---

**#12**
Tooth sensitivity is your tooth trying to tell you something.

Common causes:
• Enamel erosion from acidic foods/drinks
• Gum recession exposing the root
• A cracked tooth
• Grinding (even if you don't know you're doing it)
• A cavity near the nerve

Sensitivity is not something to just tolerate. It's information. Come let us read it.

---

**#13**
Why we take X-rays even when nothing hurts:

About 60% of dental problems form where you can't see them — between teeth, under old fillings, at the root level.

By the time something hurts, the problem is often much bigger (and more expensive) than it would have been caught early.

Early detection isn't a nice-to-have. It's how we save you from major procedures.

---

**#14**
Chewing ice is one of the worst things you can do to your teeth.

The combination of cold temperature and extreme pressure creates micro-fractures in enamel — and eventually leads to cracks or chips.

If you crave ice frequently (called pagophagia), it can also be a sign of iron deficiency anemia. Worth mentioning to your doctor.

---

**#15**
Mouthwash doesn't replace brushing or flossing.

It's a supplement, not a substitute.

Antibacterial mouthwash is great for reducing bacteria overall. Fluoride rinse is helpful for added enamel protection. But neither one removes the sticky plaque that causes cavities and gum disease.

Think of it as the finishing touch, not the main event.

---

**#16**
Electric vs. manual toothbrush — which is better?

The honest answer: the best toothbrush is the one you'll use correctly, every day.

That said: studies consistently show electric toothbrushes remove 21% more plaque than manual. For most people, the oscillating-rotating type (Oral-B, Sonicare) outperforms manual — especially if you tend to brush too hard or rush.

Ask us at your next appointment which we'd recommend for you specifically.

---

**#17**
Your enamel doesn't grow back.

Once it's gone, it's gone. Which is why protecting it matters so much:

• Avoid brushing immediately after acidic foods/drinks (wait 30 min)
• Limit soda, sports drinks, and citrus
• Use a remineralizing toothpaste
• Don't ignore signs of grinding

Prevention is always cheaper and less painful than repair.

---

**#18**
The real reason your gums bleed when you floss:

Healthy gums don't bleed.

If your gums bleed consistently when you floss, that's a sign of gum inflammation — usually from plaque buildup at the gumline.

The solution is NOT to stop flossing. The solution is to floss MORE consistently until the inflammation goes away (usually within 1-2 weeks).

---

**#19**
Baby teeth matter even though they fall out.

They hold space for permanent teeth. They help with speech development. They're necessary for proper chewing.

When baby teeth are lost too early (from cavities or trauma), it can shift spacing and create alignment problems that require orthodontic treatment later.

Kids should see a dentist by age 1 or when their first tooth appears — whichever comes first.

---

**#20**
What's in your water matters for your teeth.

Fluoridated tap water has been one of the most impactful public health interventions in dental history — reducing cavities by up to 25% in communities that fluoridate.

Most bottled water contains little to no fluoride. If you exclusively drink bottled water, mention it at your next visit. Especially if you have kids.

---

**#21**
Pregnancy and your teeth: what changes.

During pregnancy, hormonal changes make gums more sensitive and prone to inflammation — sometimes called "pregnancy gingivitis."

What to do:
✓ Keep all dental appointments (safe at any trimester)
✓ Brush and floss more carefully
✓ Tell us if you're pregnant so we can adjust treatment timing

Skipping dental care during pregnancy is not safer. It's riskier.

---

**#22**
Grinding your teeth (bruxism) happens mostly while you sleep.

Signs you might be grinding:
• Worn-down tooth edges
• Morning jaw soreness or headaches
• Chipped or cracked teeth
• Your partner can hear it

A custom night guard is the most effective protection. Store-bought versions can help short-term but often don't fit correctly enough to truly protect your teeth.

---

**#23**
Soda — even diet soda — damages teeth.

It's not just the sugar. It's the phosphoric and citric acid in both regular and diet versions.

Both are acidic enough to soften enamel with repeated exposure. Over time, this leads to erosion, sensitivity, and increased cavity risk.

If soda is a daily habit, drinking through a straw and rinsing with water after helps reduce contact with tooth surfaces.

---

**#24**
The connection between stress and your oral health:

High stress → increased cortisol → compromised immune response → harder to fight gum infections

High stress also increases:
• Grinding and clenching
• Dry mouth (from breathing through the mouth when anxious)
• Neglect of brushing/flossing routines

Taking care of your mental health is taking care of your teeth too.

---

**#25**
At what age should kids start using fluoride toothpaste?

As soon as the first tooth appears.

Under age 3: use a grain-of-rice-sized amount.
Ages 3–6: a pea-sized amount, with supervision.
Age 6+: standard amount with instruction on spitting.

The concern about swallowing is real but manageable with age-appropriate amounts. The benefit of fluoride protection starts from that very first tooth.

---

**#26**
Teeth whitening works differently than most people think.

Whitening agents (hydrogen peroxide or carbamide peroxide) penetrate the enamel and break apart the molecular chains that cause staining.

This is why:
• Whitening only works on natural tooth structure — not veneers, crowns, or fillings
• Surface stains respond faster than deep stains
• Results vary widely based on what caused the staining

Come see us before whitening to make sure you're a good candidate.

---

**#27**
Is sparkling water bad for your teeth?

The honest answer: it's better than soda, worse than still water.

The carbonic acid in sparkling water is mildly acidic — but not nearly as erosive as the phosphoric acid in soda.

Unflavored sparkling water is a low-risk substitute. Citrus-flavored sparkling water (with added acids) is more erosive and should be consumed in moderation.

---

**#28**
The right technique for brushing:

Angle: 45 degrees to the gumline
Motion: small, circular strokes — NOT back and forth
Time: 2 minutes minimum, divided between all 4 quadrants
Pressure: light (let the bristles do the work)
Surface: outer, inner, and chewing surfaces — plus the tongue

Most people rush and press too hard. The technique matters as much as the frequency.

---

**#29**
Why do teeth shift as we age?

Several factors:
• Bone density loss (especially in women post-menopause)
• Gum recession exposing root surfaces
• Wisdom tooth pressure
• Grinding wearing down contact points
• Old dental work that no longer fits perfectly

Minor shifting is normal. Rapid or significant shifting should be evaluated — it can signal bone loss from advanced gum disease.

---

**#30**
The foods that actually help your teeth:

🧀 Cheese: raises pH in mouth, reduces cavity risk
🥦 Crunchy vegetables: natural scrubbing + saliva stimulation
🥛 Dairy: calcium + phosphate for enamel remineralization
🍎 Apples: water content + stimulates saliva
🫚 Nuts: calcium + phosphorus without high sugar
💧 Water: the single best drink for oral health

Your diet shapes your dental health. Every single day.
