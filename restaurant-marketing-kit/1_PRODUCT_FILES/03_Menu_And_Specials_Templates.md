# Menu Launch & Specials Announcement Templates

## How to Use These Templates

Every template in this file is written to be used immediately with minimal customization. The goal is to remove the blank-page problem from your marketing workflow so that whenever a new dish, seasonal menu, or special goes live, you have a complete, professional announcement ready within minutes. Go through each section and identify the templates that match your restaurant's voice. Some will fit exactly as written; others will need a dish name, a price, or a seasonal detail dropped in. Either way, you are editing — not creating from scratch — and that distinction matters when you are running a restaurant and time is the scarcest resource you have.

The principle behind every template in this file is that food should be irresistible in text before it is even visible on a plate. Too many restaurants announce specials with captions that read like a receipt: "Grilled salmon, lemon butter, asparagus, $28." That tells the guest what they will get. It does not make them want it. The templates here lead with feeling, with story, with sensory language, with the question that makes someone tap "reserve" or walk through your door. Use them as written, adapt them to your voice, and then make it a habit to describe food the way you would describe it to someone who couldn't yet see it but already wanted it.

**Staff Talking Points for New Item**

When a guest asks about the new [dish name], here is what every server should know and be able to say naturally:

- The story: This dish was developed by our chef over [X weeks/months]. It started as an idea around [the inspiration — a seasonal ingredient, a cooking technique, a cuisine influence] and evolved into what it is now. Feel free to share that story at the table — guests love knowing that what they are eating came from a genuine creative process.

- The key ingredients: [Ingredient 1] is sourced from [supplier or region] and is at its peak right now. [Ingredient 2] is made in-house every morning. [Ingredient 3] is a seasonal element that will not be on the menu year-round.

- The preparation: This dish is [preparation method — wood-fired, slow-braised, hand-rolled, etc.], which [explain what this does for the flavor or texture — e.g., "creates a caramelized crust that adds depth to the dish" or "gives it a tenderness that you can't achieve with a faster cooking method"].

- What it pairs well with: [Wine pairing suggestion], [cocktail pairing if applicable], or for non-drinkers, [non-alcoholic pairing]. Let guests know if there is a pairing that the chef particularly recommends.

- How to describe it to hesitant guests: If someone is on the fence, lead with the most approachable element. If it is a complex or unusual dish, anchor it to something familiar: "It has the richness of a good braise but the lightness of a spring preparation." Make it sound like something they already love, elevated.

- Allergen information: [List any relevant allergens — gluten, dairy, nuts, shellfish, etc.]. Always confirm with the kitchen if a guest has a specific allergy before confirming the dish is safe.

## SECTION 5 — MENU PRICE INCREASE COMMUNICATION

### How to Frame a Price Increase: Language Guide

A price increase is a communication challenge before it is a financial one. How you announce it — and what language you use — determines how your regulars receive it. The foundational principle is this: lead with what you are committed to, not with what is changing. Guests who love your restaurant are not paying for a price point. They are paying for an experience, a standard, and a relationship with your kitchen. Communicate that your commitment to that standard is the reason for the adjustment, and most guests who genuinely value what you do will understand.

Do not apologize excessively. A brief acknowledgment of the inconvenience is appropriate. A paragraph of hand-wringing apologies signals anxiety and actually draws more attention to the increase than a confident, transparent statement would. Say what is true, say it with warmth, and say it once. What you should never do: blame it on forces outside your control without also explaining what you are doing to protect quality in spite of those forces. Guests want to know that you are in control, that you are making deliberate decisions, and that you still have their experience as the priority.

The language that works: "Our commitment to the quality you expect has not changed." "We work hard to absorb costs wherever we can before they reach your bill." "Every adjustment we make is designed to protect what you love about eating here." The language that does not work: "We hate to do this," "Due to inflation," "Unfortunately we have no choice." The first set sounds like a chef who is in control. The second sounds like a business that is overwhelmed. Be the chef.

**FAQs to Prepare Staff**

Q: Why are the prices going up?

A: The cost of the ingredients and labor that go into everything we make has increased significantly over the past year. We held off making any changes to the menu as long as we could because we did not want to pass that on to our guests. But to maintain the quality and sourcing that define what we do here, this adjustment became necessary. The short answer is: it costs more to make what we make to the standard we make it, and we chose the standard.

---

Q: Is everything more expensive or just some things?

A: Most items on the menu have been adjusted, though the changes vary. Some items have changed more than others depending on the ingredients involved. The best thing to do if a guest has a specific concern is to acknowledge it genuinely and let them know that the quality behind every price is something we stand behind completely.

---

Q: Will you be adding new things to the menu?

A: We are always developing new dishes and refining the menu. There are some exciting additions coming up that the kitchen has been working on, and we think guests are going to love what is coming. A price adjustment on existing items does not mean we are pulling back — if anything, we are doubling down on what makes this menu exceptional.

---

Q: This is too expensive now, I'm not coming back.

A: I completely understand, and I am sorry to hear that. We genuinely value your business and it is never easy to hear that. If there is anything I can do to make sure your experience here today is exceptional, I would love to do that. I would also encourage you to look at our [lunch menu / happy hour menu / specific lower price-point items] as options that might fit better. And if you are a member of our loyalty program, there may be some additional benefits available to you. We hope we will see you again.
