## SECTION 5 — MENU PRICE INCREASE COMMUNICATION

### Language Guide — Framing a Price Increase

**What to Say (and How to Say It)**

A price increase is never fun to communicate — but how you frame it determines how customers receive it. The goal is honesty, warmth, and a forward-looking tone that emphasizes value. Here's a guide to the language choices that work and the ones that backfire.

**Words and phrases to USE:**

- "Adjusting our prices" — softer and more accurate than "raising" or "increasing"
- "To continue offering the quality you expect" — connects the change to your commitment to them
- "Rising costs of [ingredients / labor / sourcing]" — specific reasons build trust and feel honest
- "We don't take this decision lightly" — acknowledges it's a meaningful choice, not a casual one
- "Our commitment to [locally sourced / made-from-scratch / quality ingredients] hasn't changed" — reassures on values
- "We're grateful for your continued support" — positions the relationship as mutual
- "We've held our prices as long as we could" — shows restraint and consideration
- "We believe you'll still find exceptional value here" — ends on confidence, not apology

**Words and phrases to AVOID:**

- "Unfortunately" used repeatedly — over-apologizing feels weak and draws more attention to the negative
- "We have no choice" — sounds passive and corporate; you do have a choice, and you're making a thoughtful one
- "Due to inflation" as the only explanation — too vague; be specific about what's driving costs
- "We hope you understand" — slightly passive-aggressive; trust that they will
- "We hate to do this" — undermines confidence in your business decisions
- "Same great food, higher prices" — tone-deaf; acknowledge that price changes matter to guests
- Burying the announcement in unrelated content — be direct and lead with it
- Over-explaining or listing every cost in detail — one or two honest reasons is enough; a laundry list feels defensive

**Tone to strike:**

Confident, warm, honest. You are a business making a necessary and considered decision. You respect your guests enough to tell them directly and explain briefly why. You're not asking for forgiveness — you're communicating a change and affirming your continued commitment to the experience they love.

### Staff FAQs — Price Increase Preparation

**Template: Staff FAQ Guide for Price Adjustments**

**Q1: A guest says, "Did your prices go up? Things seem more expensive."**

**A:** "Yes, we did make some pricing adjustments recently. The cost of the ingredients we use — we source [locally / from small farms / with a focus on quality] — has increased quite a bit, and we wanted to be upfront about it. We've held our prices as long as we could, and this was a considered decision. We still feel we offer really strong value for the experience, and we hope you agree."

---

**Q2: A guest is visibly upset or says, "This is too expensive now. I used to come here all the time."**

**A:** "I completely understand — any change in price is worth acknowledging, and I'm sorry if it's caught you off guard. Our ownership made the decision to be transparent about it rather than quietly raise prices, and they've tried to keep increases as minimal as possible. We really do value your loyalty. If you'd like, I can point out some of our best-value items on the menu, or let you know about any specials we have tonight."

*If the guest remains upset, offer to get a manager. Do not argue or over-explain.*

---

**Q3: A guest asks, "Why did the price of [specific dish] go up so much?"**

**A:** "That's a fair question. [Specific dish] uses [key ingredient], which has gotten significantly more expensive to source at the quality we use. Rather than switch to a lower-quality ingredient or reduce the portion, we adjusted the price. We think you'd rather have the same dish you love than a compromised version."

---

**Q4: A guest asks, "Are you going to lower prices again if costs come down?"**

**A:** "That's something our ownership takes seriously. We've been honest with guests about why prices have changed, and if the economics shift, they'd want to reflect that fairly. I can't make a specific promise, but it's not something they take lightly."

---

**Q5: A guest mentions they saw backlash or complaints about the price increase online.**

**A:** "I understand people have strong feelings, and we respect that. Our team has tried to be as transparent as possible about why this was a necessary change. We're proud of what we make here and the people who make it, and we believe the experience is worth it. We hope you'll be able to judge that for yourself tonight."

---

**Q6: A guest asks whether they should have been notified about the price change.**

**A:** "We did our best to communicate it — we sent an email to our subscriber list and posted on social media before the change went into effect. We're sorry if that didn't reach you. Going forward, signing up for our email list is the best way to hear news like this directly: [sign-up link or method]."

---

**Q7: A guest says, "The food better be worth it at these prices."**

**A (with a warm smile):** "That's exactly the right standard to hold us to. I think it will be. Let me take great care of you tonight and I hope you'll leave feeling that way."
