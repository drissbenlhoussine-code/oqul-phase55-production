# Reservation, Booking & Guest Communication Templates

## How to Use These Templates

Every touchpoint in the reservation process is a chance to make a guest feel welcome before they ever walk through your door. These templates cover the full guest communication journey — from the first confirmation email to the day-of reminder to the no-show follow-up — and they're designed to sound warm and human, not automated. Customize the bracketed placeholders with your restaurant's name, address, hours, and any specific policies before deploying. We recommend loading the email versions into your email marketing platform or POS system's automated communication tools, and saving the text templates as quick-reply shortcuts in your phone or team messaging app.

The phone scripts in Section 5 are designed to be printed and kept at the host stand for easy reference. Staff should read them through, internalize the tone and structure, then speak naturally — not robotically. The goal isn't word-for-word recitation; it's consistent, warm, professional communication that reflects your brand. Role-play each script with your front-of-house team at least once before putting it into practice. Guests remember how you made them feel, and these scripts give your team the language to make every guest feel expected, valued, and genuinely looked forward to.

## SECTION 5 — PHONE SCRIPTS

---

**Incoming Reservation Script**

*[Phone rings. Answer within 3 rings.]*

"Good [morning / afternoon / evening], thank you for calling [Restaurant Name]. This is [Your Name] — how can I help you today?"

*[Guest says they'd like to make a reservation.]*

"Absolutely, I'd be happy to help with that. Let me grab a few details from you."

*[Collect the following, in this order:]*

1. "What date were you thinking?"
   *Confirm availability immediately. If the requested date is unavailable, offer the closest alternatives before asking anything else: "I'm sorry, we're fully booked that evening — but we do have availability on [Day] and [Day]. Would either of those work for you?"*

2. "And what time works best for your party?"
   *Offer two options if they're flexible: "We have availability at 6:30 or 7:45 — does either of those work for you?"*

3. "How many guests will be joining you?"

4. "Can I get a name for the reservation?"
   *Spell it back to confirm: "That's [spelling] — did I get that right?"*

5. "And a best phone number in case we need to reach you?"
   *Repeat the number back: "I have [number] — is that correct?"*

6. "Is there any special occasion we should know about — birthday, anniversary, anything like that?"
   *If yes: "Wonderful — we'll make a note and do something to make it extra special."*

7. "Any dietary restrictions or allergies I should flag for the kitchen?"
   *If yes, note each one specifically and read them back.*

*[Confirm everything before closing:]*

"Perfect — so I have you down for [Name], party of [Number], on [Day, Date] at [Time]. You'll receive a confirmation text / email shortly. Is there anything else I can help you with?"

*[If no:]*

"Wonderful — we look forward to seeing you. Have a great [day / evening]!"

*[Key principle: Never rush. The guest should feel the conversation was warm and unhurried — not processed. A great phone call is the first impression.]*

---

**Handling "Do You Have a Table for X Tonight?"**

*[If YES — you have availability:]*

"We do! What time were you thinking? We have something available around [Time Option 1] or a bit later at [Time Option 2] — which works better for you?"

*[Take the booking using the standard reservation script above.]*

---

*[If NO — fully booked:]*

"I'm so sorry — we're fully committed for tonight. I know that's disappointing."

*[Pause briefly. Then offer options:]*

"A couple of things I can do for you — first, I can add you to our cancellation list. If anything opens up, you'd be the first call we make. Would that be helpful?"

*[If yes: take their name and number and add them to the list immediately.]*

"Also, our bar is open for walk-ins — we almost always have bar seating available, and you can order from the full menu. It's a great option if you'd like to come in tonight."

*[If neither option works:]*

"Can I help you find a time later this week? We have [availability on Thursday at 7 and Friday at 6:30] — I'd hate for you to miss us."

*[Always end with:]*

"I'm sorry we can't make tonight work, but we'd genuinely love to have you in soon. What's the best way to reach you if something opens up?"

---

**Taking Dietary Restriction Notes**

*[Once you've confirmed the core reservation details, ask:]*

"One more quick thing before I let you go — do any of your guests have dietary restrictions or food allergies we should know about?"

*[If yes:]*

"Of course — could you walk me through those?"

*[Listen carefully. As they share each one, confirm it back:]*

"So we have [Guest or 'one guest'] who is [gluten-free / allergic to shellfish / vegan / avoiding dairy / etc.] — is that right?"

*[Confirm each restriction individually. Then:]*

"I'm going to note this directly on your reservation right now, and I'll make sure our kitchen is aware so they can prepare accordingly."

*[For severe allergies, add:]*

"For severe allergies — especially anything like tree nuts, shellfish, or gluten — I'd also recommend mentioning it again to your server when you arrive, just so the kitchen can take every precaution on the night itself. We take this very seriously."

*[If no restrictions:]*

"Perfect — and if anything changes before your visit, please don't hesitate to call back and we'll add it to the notes."

---

**Upselling the Experience (Special Occasion Upgrade)**

*[When a guest mentions a birthday, anniversary, proposal, or any celebration during the reservation call:]*

"That's so exciting — congratulations! We'd love to help make the evening really special. Can I mention a few things we can arrange ahead of time?"

*[Pause for a yes.]*

"We can put together a few different touches — I'll run through them and you can just let me know what sounds appealing:

- We can arrange a **personalized dessert** with a congratulatory message from our pastry team — that's [$X] and it's always a wonderful surprise
- We can have a **bottle of champagne** waiting at the table when you arrive — we have a few options starting at [$X]
- We can flag a **preferred table** for you — our team will do their best to seat you somewhere a bit more intimate or with a nice view, depending on what's available that evening
- And if you'd really like to let us take care of you for the whole evening, our **chef's tasting menu** is available for the full table — it's a multi-course experience, and for a celebration it's something quite special

Absolutely no pressure — even just having the occasion noted means our team will know to give you a little extra attention throughout the evening."

*[If they choose an upgrade:]*

"Wonderful — I've noted everything on your reservation. You'll see it reflected in your confirmation. Feel free to reply to that email if you think of anything else between now and then. We're looking forward to making it a memorable evening."

*[If they pass:]*

"Of course — we'll make sure it's a lovely evening regardless. See you on [Date]!"

*[Key principle: Offer once, warmly, with specifics. Give them enough to visualize the experience, then let them decide. A gentle mention is far more effective than repeated upselling — and it leaves the guest feeling considered, not sold to.]*
