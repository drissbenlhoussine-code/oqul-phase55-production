# Customer Review & Reputation Management System

---

## SECTION 1 — TIMING STRATEGY

The single biggest mistake restaurants make when trying to collect reviews is focusing entirely on how to ask and almost nothing on when to ask. Timing determines whether your ask lands as a warm, natural moment or an awkward transaction. Getting the timing right is the foundation of this entire system.

The best moment to ask for a review is during the natural emotional peak of the dining experience. For most guests, that's not the middle of the meal — it's right after dessert, when the last course has been cleared, people are relaxed, conversation is winding down, and the overall experience is fresh and positive in their minds. That's the ideal window. The check arrives, the server takes payment, and if the experience has been good, that final moment of human contact is the right place for a genuine, low-pressure mention of your review profiles.

There are a few caveats. If a table had a problem during the meal — a long wait, a wrong order, a noise complaint — and it was resolved, wait to see the emotional temperature before asking. A table that left upset and came back to okay is not a table that will leave a five-star review tonight. Better to handle that follow-up by email the next day, after the friction has faded and only the resolution remains. This is discussed more in the digital follow-up section below.

Timing also varies by dining format. At a casual counter-service or fast-casual restaurant, the best moment is the transaction itself — a small card with the register receipt, a small sign near the pickup window, or a QR code on the tray. At a sit-down casual restaurant, the server moment at checkout is ideal. At a fine dining establishment, the manager or maître d' walking the guest to the door and thanking them personally is the highest-value moment, because the personal relationship and attentiveness are already established.

Digital follow-up timing matters just as much. A same-day text message sent within two to four hours of the meal performs significantly better than one sent the next morning — the food is still fresh in memory, the guest is still in the glow of the experience. For reservation-based guests who've provided email addresses, a next-day email strikes the right balance: far enough removed that it doesn't feel desperate, close enough that the meal is still vivid. For special occasions like birthdays, anniversaries, or private events, a follow-up email sent the day after the event tends to generate the warmest and most enthusiastic reviews, because the guest is still basking in the occasion itself.

Finally, frequency matters. Don't train your regulars to expect a review ask every single visit. For loyal customers who come in weekly or monthly, a periodic request — perhaps once a season, or when something genuinely new has been added that you want feedback on — keeps the ask feeling authentic rather than mechanical. The occasional loyal customer review is worth ten times the drive-by first-timer review, because regulars write with specificity, warmth, and credibility.

## SECTION 2 — IN-PERSON SCRIPTS

### Server Table-Side Ask (Casual Dining)

Use this after the meal, when the bill is presented or when payment is being processed. The tone is warm and conversational — not scripted and robotic.

**The Script:**

"I'm really glad you came in tonight — it looked like you were having a great time, and that always makes our job feel worth it. If you get a chance and you enjoyed the meal, it would mean a lot to us if you left us a quick review on Google. It honestly makes a huge difference for a place like ours. We put the link right on the receipt — it takes about two minutes, and we read every single one. No pressure at all, but we'd love to hear what you thought."

---

If the guest responds positively or asks how to find the review page, follow with:

"The easiest way is just to search for us on Google — our name pops right up, and the review link is right there. Or the QR code on the receipt takes you straight to it. We really appreciate it."

---

### Server Table-Side Ask (Fine/Formal Dining)

At a fine dining establishment, the approach should feel elevated, personal, and gracious rather than transactional. The server or maître d' should never say the word "review" in a way that sounds like a metric being chased.

**The Script:**

"It's been a pleasure taking care of you this evening. I hope everything was exactly as you'd hoped. If you have a moment later this week, we'd love for you to share your experience online — our team puts a great deal of care into every detail of an evening like this, and hearing from guests who truly appreciate it is something that means a great deal to all of us. We're on Google and TripAdvisor, and any words from you would be genuinely valued."

---

If the guest responds warmly, the server can add:

"Thank you so much. We hope to see you again before long."

---

This script works because it frames the review as an act of appreciation toward the team, not a favor to the business. That framing resonates with guests at fine dining establishments who are already emotionally invested in the experience.

---

### Manager Check-In Script

Use this when the manager or owner stops by the table mid-meal or at the end of service. This works both for a proactive check-in and as a natural follow-up to a compliment the guest offers.

**The Script:**

"Thank you so much for saying that — I'll absolutely pass that along to the kitchen. That kind of feedback genuinely makes their night. We're always working to make sure every table has an experience worth talking about, and when guests take a moment to share what worked for them online, it helps us more than I can explain. If you get a chance, we're on Google — it would mean a great deal to us. And please come back soon."

---

If the guest raises a concern during the check-in:

"I'm really sorry to hear that — let me make this right for you tonight. And I'd love to follow up with you personally after your visit to make sure we've fully addressed this. Can I ask your server to grab your email?"

---

Note: Never ask for a review during a conversation where a complaint has just been raised. Address the issue first. The review ask, if it comes at all, comes later via email after resolution.

---

### Post-Meal Send-Off Script

Use this at the door, when the host or manager is saying goodbye to a table as they leave. This is one of the most underutilized moments in the entire guest experience.

**The Script:**

"Thank you so much for being with us tonight — it was really wonderful to have you. I hope we'll see you again soon. If you enjoyed everything, a Google review would make our team's week. We're easy to find, and it only takes a minute. Safe travels home — thank you again."

---

This script works best when delivered with genuine warmth and eye contact. It is brief, sincere, and specific. The guest should leave feeling seen, not sold to.

## SECTION 3 — DIGITAL FOLLOW-UP TEMPLATES

### Template 1 — Same-Day Text Follow-Up

Send within 2–4 hours of the meal. Keep it under 160 characters. Use your restaurant's name in the first line.

**Complete Text Message:**

"Thanks for dining with us tonight! If you enjoyed your experience, a quick Google review would mean so much to our team. [link] Thanks again — hope to see you soon!"

---

### Template 2 — Next-Day Email for Reservation Guests

Send the morning after a reservation visit. This is for guests who provided an email at booking.

**Subject Line:** We loved having you with us last night

**Email Body:**

Hi there,

Thank you so much for joining us for dinner last night. We hope it was everything you were hoping for — and then some.

Our team works hard to make sure every detail of your experience feels worth it, from the first bite to the final course. When guests take a few minutes to share their thoughts online, it genuinely helps us reach more people who we think would love what we do here.

If you have a moment, we'd be so grateful if you could leave us a quick review on Google. It takes about two minutes and means more to our small team than you might realize.

Leave a Review: [Your Google review link here]

Thank you again for your support. We hope to see you back soon.

Warmly,
The Team

---

### Template 3 — Post-Event Thank You and Review Ask

Send the day after a private dining event, corporate dinner, or large group celebration.

**Subject Line:** Thank you for celebrating with us

**Email Body:**

Hi [First Name or "there"],

We wanted to take a moment to thank you for choosing us to host your event. It was truly a pleasure to be part of your celebration, and we hope every detail of the evening was exactly what you were hoping for.

Our team puts an enormous amount of care into making sure events like yours go smoothly — from the menu planning to the service to the final details of the evening. Hearing that it came together for you makes all of that effort worthwhile.

If you'd like to share a few words about your experience, we'd be incredibly grateful for a review on Google or TripAdvisor. A review from an event host helps other couples, families, and organizations find us when they're planning something special.

Google Review: [link]
TripAdvisor: [link]

We'd love to host you again. Please don't hesitate to reach out if there's anything we can do better or anything we should know for next time.

With gratitude,
The Team

---

### Template 4 — Birthday and Anniversary Follow-Up

Send the day after a birthday or anniversary dinner. This is a warm, relationship-first message.

**Subject Line:** We hope your celebration was everything you deserved

**Email Body:**

Hi there,

We are so glad you chose to celebrate with us. Whether it was a birthday, an anniversary, or just a milestone worth marking — we feel honored that you spent part of it in our dining room.

Our team loves being part of those kinds of nights. The little moments — the dessert, the toast, the laughter at the table — those are the reasons we do what we do.

If you have a moment, leaving us a quick Google review would make our team's week. Knowing that we got the details right for your special occasion is something everyone in our kitchen and dining room genuinely cares about.

Leave a Review Here: [Your Google review link]

Thank you again. We hope whatever you were celebrating continues to bring you joy — and that we get to be part of the next one too.

All the best,
The Team

---

### Template 5 — Loyalty Program Review Request

Send periodically to active loyalty members. Can be sent as a text or email.

**As a Text Message:**
"Hi! As one of our most valued regulars, your opinion matters most. Would you take 2 min to leave us a Google review? [link] Thank you so much — see you soon!"

**As an Email:**

**Subject Line:** Your opinion means more than most

**Email Body:**

Hi there,

You've been a part of what we do here for a while now, and that genuinely means the world to our team. Regulars like you are the reason this place works — you've supported us through menu changes, busy seasons, and everything in between.

We'd love to ask a favor. If you haven't yet left us a review on Google, would you consider taking two minutes to share your experience? Your perspective as someone who truly knows this place carries more weight than almost anyone else's. A detailed review from a longtime guest can make a real difference for a small restaurant.

Our Google review link: [link]

If there's anything you think we should be doing better, we want to hear that too. You can reply to this email directly — we read every response.

Thank you for being a part of this place. We're grateful for you more than you know.

The Team

## SECTION 4 — PLATFORM GUIDE

### Google Reviews

Google is the single most important review platform for a local restaurant. When someone searches for your restaurant by name, your Google Business Profile appears first — with your star rating and recent reviews immediately visible. Google reviews also directly influence local search rankings. A restaurant with 200 positive Google reviews will consistently rank higher in local search results than one with 50, all other things being equal.

To set up your Google Business Profile, go to business.google.com and claim or create your listing. Make sure your address, phone number, hours, and website are accurate — consistency in this information across the web improves search ranking. Upload professional photos of your food, your interior, and your team. Add your menu if the feature is available in your region.

To get your review link, go to your Google Business Profile dashboard, click on "Ask for Reviews," and copy the direct link. This is the URL that goes in your text messages, your emails, and your QR codes. Shorten it with a service like bit.ly for use in printed materials.

Share the link on your website, in your email signature, on a small card that comes with the bill, and as a QR code posted near the exit or printed on your menu. The easier you make it to find the review page, the more reviews you receive.

### Yelp

Yelp has a passionate and loyal user base, particularly in urban markets. It functions differently from Google in one critical way: Yelp's algorithm actively filters reviews from accounts it deems unreliable (new accounts, single-review accounts, accounts with limited activity). This means that many genuine reviews from first-time Yelp users can be filtered, which is frustrating but worth understanding.

Claim your business at biz.yelp.com and ensure all information is complete and accurate. Add photos, update your hours for holidays, and respond to all reviews — both positive and negative. Yelp is an active community and businesses that engage consistently tend to maintain stronger standing.

The most important thing to know about Yelp: do not actively solicit Yelp reviews. Their terms of service prohibit directly asking for Yelp reviews, and doing so can trigger a consumer alert on your profile. Instead, use passive approaches: a "Find us on Yelp" sign near the register, your Yelp badge on your website, and a mention that you're on the platform without a direct ask.

### TripAdvisor

TripAdvisor remains the dominant platform for tourist-heavy markets and destination dining. If your restaurant is located in an area that draws visitors, or if you serve guests who are traveling, TripAdvisor is essential. For neighborhood restaurants with a primarily local clientele, it is secondary to Google.

Claim your listing at tripadvisor.com/owners. Complete your profile fully — adding photos, your menu, a description, and your price range all improve your positioning in TripAdvisor's ranking system. The platform rewards businesses that respond to reviews regularly, so build response time into your weekly routine.

For restaurants that receive significant tourist traffic, TripAdvisor cards at the table and a note on your takeaway bag are effective low-cost tools for generating reviews from travelers who are actively browsing the platform for their next destination.

**Priority Recommendation:** Spend 80% of your review generation effort on Google. If you have bandwidth left, add TripAdvisor for your tourist-facing visibility. Manage Yelp passively — claim, respond, and let your reputation build organically.

## SECTION 5 — RESPONDING TO REVIEWS

Responding to reviews is one of the highest-leverage activities in restaurant reputation management. It signals to future guests that you are attentive, that you care, and that real people run this place. Every response you write is read not just by the person who left the review, but by every potential new customer who's evaluating whether to book with you. Write every response with that broader audience in mind.

### 5-Star Review — Brief Response

**Template:**

"Thank you so much for the kind words! We're thrilled you had a great experience, and we hope to see you back soon."

---

### 5-Star Review — Warm Response

**Template:**

"This made our whole team's day — thank you! We work hard to make sure every detail of your visit feels worth it, and knowing that it came through for you is exactly the kind of feedback that keeps us going. We can't wait to have you back."

---

### 5-Star Review — Specific Mention Response

Use when the reviewer mentions a specific dish, staff member, or experience element.

**Template:**

"Thank you so much for taking the time to write this — and especially for mentioning the [specific dish]. It's one our kitchen is truly proud of, and it means so much to hear that it stood out. We'll make sure to pass your kind words along to the team. We hope to see you again very soon!"

---

### 4-Star Review — Response Version A

**Template:**

"Thank you for this thoughtful review and for the four stars! We're glad the overall experience was positive and we'd love the chance to make your next visit a five-star one. If there's anything specific we can do better, we'd love to hear it — feel free to reach out directly."

---

### 4-Star Review — Response Version B

**Template:**

"We really appreciate you taking the time to share your thoughts! We're happy you enjoyed your experience and we're always working to get better. We'd love to have you back and show you what we've been working on."

---

### 3-Star Review — Acknowledge and Invite Back

**Template:**

"Thank you for being honest with us — it genuinely helps. We're sorry your experience didn't quite reach the level we strive for, and we'd love the opportunity to do better. If you're willing to give us another try, please reach out to us directly and we'll make sure your next visit is a meaningful improvement. We take every piece of feedback seriously and we appreciate you giving us the chance to hear it."

---

### 2-Star Review — Apologize and Follow-Up Offer

**Template:**

"We sincerely apologize for falling short during your visit. What you described is not the experience we want any of our guests to have, and we're taking your feedback seriously. We'd genuinely like the opportunity to make this right. Please reach out to us directly at [email address] and we'll do everything we can to turn this around for you. Thank you for letting us know."

---

### 1-Star Review — Legitimate Complaint

**Template:**

"We are truly sorry to hear about your experience and we sincerely apologize for what you went through. This is not the standard we hold ourselves to, and we want to understand exactly what happened so we can address it properly. Please contact us directly at [email address] — we take this very seriously and we would like to make it right. Thank you for bringing this to our attention."

---

### 1-Star Review — Unreasonable or Emotional Review

Use when the review contains exaggerations, inaccuracies, or appears to be emotionally driven rather than factually grounded. Remain calm, professional, and brief. Never argue.

**Template:**

"Thank you for taking the time to share your feedback. We're sorry to hear that your experience didn't meet your expectations. We strive to provide every guest with a positive experience and we take all feedback seriously. If you'd like to discuss your visit further, please reach out to us directly — we're always willing to listen and work toward a resolution."

---

### Fake Review Response

Use when you believe the reviewer has never actually visited your restaurant. Be measured, professional, and fact-based. Never accuse directly — simply note the discrepancy.

**Template:**

"Thank you for your review. We've looked into your experience and we're unable to find a record of your visit in our reservations or transaction history. We take all feedback seriously and want every guest experience to be positive. If there's been any confusion or if you did visit us and we've missed something, please reach out to us directly at [email address] — we'd love the chance to connect and clarify."

---

Note: You can also report suspected fake reviews to the platform directly using their flagging or reporting tools. On Google, go to the review, click the three-dot menu, and select "Report review." This does not guarantee removal, but it initiates a review by the platform.

---

### Response to Review Mentioning Specific Staff Member Positively

**Template:**

"Thank you so much for this wonderful review — and for specifically mentioning [staff member's name]. We'll absolutely pass your kind words along to them. Our team works incredibly hard to make sure every guest feels taken care of, and hearing that it made an impression means a great deal to all of us. We hope to see you back soon!"

---

### Bonus — Response to Review Mentioning Wait Time Complaint

**Template:**

"Thank you for sharing your experience and for your patience during your visit. We're sorry the wait time was longer than expected — on busy evenings, we know that can be frustrating, and we appreciate you bearing with us. We're constantly working on improving our flow during peak hours, and your feedback helps inform those decisions. We'd love the chance to have you back during a calmer moment so you can experience the visit we intended to give you. Thank you again for understanding."

## SECTION 6 — REVIEW TRACKING SHEET

### Monthly Review Tracker — Column Headers and Setup

Use this tracker to monitor your review performance monthly across all platforms. Set it up as a spreadsheet (Google Sheets or Excel) with the following columns:

| Month | Platform | Total Reviews (Cumulative) | New Reviews This Month | Average Star Rating | 5-Star Count | 4-Star Count | 3-Star Count | 2-Star Count | 1-Star Count | Reviews Responded To | Response Rate % | Themes Mentioned (Positive) | Themes Mentioned (Negative) | Action Taken |

---

### How to Use This Tracker

At the beginning of each month, log in to each review platform and record your cumulative review count and current star rating. At the end of the month, return to each platform and note the number of new reviews that came in during the period. Count the distribution across star ratings and calculate your response rate by dividing the number of reviews you responded to by the total number of new reviews and multiplying by 100.

The "Themes Mentioned" columns are the most valuable part of this tracker. Read through all new reviews and note recurring words or topics that appear in positive reviews (fast service, great atmosphere, pasta, friendly staff) and in negative reviews (wait time, noise level, portion size). These themes become your operational roadmap — what to protect and what to fix.

The "Action Taken" column is where accountability lives. If three reviews mentioned slow service during Saturday dinner, the action might be "added one additional server to Saturday night shift starting Week 3." If five reviews praised the seafood special, the action might be "moved seasonal seafood to permanent menu consideration." Review the tracker monthly in a team meeting so front-of-house and kitchen staff understand both where things are going well and where there's room to improve.

Aim for a monthly response rate of 100% on all reviews, a minimum of 80%. Prioritize responding to 1-star and 2-star reviews within 24 hours, 3-star reviews within 48 hours, and all positive reviews within one week. A consistent, timely response practice is one of the most visible indicators of a well-managed restaurant to potential guests browsing your profile.
