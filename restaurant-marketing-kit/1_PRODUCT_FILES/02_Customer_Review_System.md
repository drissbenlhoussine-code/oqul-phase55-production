# Customer Review & Reputation Management System

## How to Use This System

A restaurant's online reputation is not built overnight, and it is not maintained by accident. This system gives you the tools to consistently generate more reviews, respond to every type of feedback with professionalism, and track your reputation over time so you can see what is working and what needs attention. The key mindset shift is this: you are not pestering guests to leave reviews. You are making it easy for happy guests to share what they already want to share. The friction between a satisfied meal and a published review is usually nothing more than a prompt at the right moment.

Work through each section of this system in order and implement one piece at a time. Start with your in-person scripts — train your team, practice the language, make it natural. Then layer in your digital follow-up templates. Then optimize your platform profiles. Then build the habit of responding to every review within 24 hours. Consistency across all of these pieces creates compounding results. A restaurant that has 200 reviews and responds to all of them will outrank and outperform a restaurant that has 500 reviews and never responds. Engagement signals matter. This system is designed to build all of them.

## SECTION 1 — TIMING STRATEGY

**The Window of Maximum Enthusiasm**

The best time to ask for a review is when the guest's experience is at its peak. For most guests, that peak is the last five minutes of the meal — after dessert, after the bill, while the warmth of the evening is still present. That is the window. Miss it and you are asking a guest to reconstruct a memory later, which is always less vivid and always less likely to result in action.

**Three Touchpoints That Work**

*During check presentation:* This is the most natural moment. The bill arrives, the server makes a brief, warm comment about the meal, and the review platform or QR code is visible. Nothing forced. Just a gentle signal that a review is welcome.

*Post-meal send-off:* As guests stand to leave and exchange goodbyes at the door, a well-trained host or server can say something brief and genuine about how much it means when guests share their experience online. The guest is still warm. The impression is fresh.

*Same-day or next-day digital follow-up:* For guests who made reservations, you have contact information. A text sent within two hours of the meal or an email the next morning catches the guest while the experience is still top of mind. These digital touches are the most scalable piece of your review strategy because they do not require any in-person judgment about timing.

**Why Happy Guests Forget**

Satisfied guests leave your restaurant with every intention of leaving a review. Then they go home. Life intervenes. The intention fades. Research consistently shows that most satisfied diners who do not leave a review cite "I forgot" as the reason — not dissatisfaction. Your job is not to convince them. It is to remind them before the moment passes. That is what this system is built to do.

**Which Channels Work Best**

Google Business Profile is the priority for every restaurant. It directly affects local search ranking and is the first thing potential guests see when they search your name. Yelp is secondary but important for discovery. TripAdvisor matters primarily for restaurants that draw tourists, travelers, or destination diners. OpenTable and Resy generate reviews through their own automated flows — optimize your profiles and the platform handles the ask. Focus 80% of your energy on Google.

## SECTION 2 — IN-PERSON SCRIPTS

**Script 1 — Server Casual Ask (Table-Side)**

*Context: This is a relaxed, conversational ask during the check presentation. Works in any casual to mid-range setting.*

"I'm so glad you enjoyed tonight — that honestly makes our night too. We're a small team and we really do read every review that comes in. If you have a minute when you get home, leaving us one on Google goes a long way for us. I can send you the link if that's easier?"

*If they say yes to the link:* "Perfect — can I grab your number? I'll text it to you right now so it's easy to find."

*If they seem hesitant:* "No pressure at all — we're just grateful you came in. We hope to see you again soon."

---

**Script 2 — Server Fine Dining / Elevated Ask**

*Context: A more refined, less direct approach for upscale or tasting menu environments where aggressive asks feel out of place.*

"It's been such a pleasure having you tonight. We put everything into each service and it genuinely means the world to us when guests take a moment to share their experience. If you find yourself thinking of us after tonight, a review on Google is the best way to help us continue doing what we love. Thank you again for being here."

*Note: Do not follow up with a request for their number. In fine dining, plant the seed and trust the experience did the work.*

---

**Script 3 — Manager Check-In Script**

*Context: The manager visits the table mid-meal or at the end of the meal to check in. This is a natural moment to mention reviews without it feeling like a transaction.*

"Good evening — I'm [Manager Name], the manager on duty tonight. I just wanted to stop by personally and make sure everything has been exactly what you hoped for. [Pause, listen to their response.] We're so glad to hear it. Honestly, guest feedback is how we keep getting better — if you're willing to share your experience on Google, it would mean a great deal to us and helps more people find us. Enjoy the rest of your evening."

---

**Script 4 — Post-Meal Send-Off Script**

*Context: Used by the host or server as guests are putting on their coats and heading toward the door.*

"Thank you so much for coming in tonight. It was a pleasure having you. If you enjoyed yourself, we'd love it if you took a moment to leave us a review on Google — honestly, it makes a huge difference for a small restaurant like ours. We hope to see you again soon."

*If guests are regulars:* "You know how much we love seeing you here — if you ever feel like leaving us a word online, it goes a long way. But more importantly, we'll see you next time."

## SECTION 4 — PLATFORM GUIDE

### Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile is the single most important review platform for any restaurant. When someone searches your restaurant's name — or searches "restaurants near me" — your Google profile is what appears first. Your star rating, number of reviews, and recent review activity directly influence your position in local search results. More reviews and higher ratings mean more visibility. It is that direct.

To get your Google review link, go to your Google Business Profile dashboard, click "Get more reviews," and copy the direct link. This is the link you will include in all digital follow-up templates. Shorten it with a tool like Bit.ly to make it less intimidating in text messages.

Profile completeness matters for ranking. Make sure your hours are accurate, your photos are current (restaurants with 100+ photos receive significantly more requests for directions), your menu is uploaded, and your business description is fully written. A complete, active profile signals to Google that the business is engaged, which improves ranking.

Respond to every review — positive and negative. Google favors profiles where the owner actively engages. Response activity is factored into local ranking signals. Beyond the algorithm, a thoughtful response to every review communicates to prospective guests that you are present, professional, and that you care.

---

### Yelp

Yelp has strict policies around soliciting reviews. Directly asking guests to leave a Yelp review — especially with a direct link — can result in a "Consumer Alert" banner being placed on your profile, which significantly damages trust. Yelp's filter also tends to flag reviews from accounts that were created specifically to leave a single review.

The compliant approach on Yelp is to make guests aware that you are on the platform without directly asking them to review you. Use "Find us on Yelp" language on your website, in your email footer, and on any printed materials. Yelp provides compliant window stickers and table tents through their business portal — use these. Let satisfied guests who are already Yelp users find you there naturally.

Claim and fully optimize your Yelp profile regardless. Add photos, complete every section, upload your menu, and set your hours correctly. Respond to all reviews — positive and negative. A well-managed Yelp presence builds trust with a large segment of the dining public, particularly for first-time visitors who use Yelp for discovery.

---

### TripAdvisor

TripAdvisor is most valuable for restaurants that attract tourists, travelers, and destination diners. If your restaurant is in a tourism area, near a hotel corridor, or positioned as a destination experience, TripAdvisor should be a priority platform. For neighborhood restaurants with primarily local clientele, it is worth maintaining but not your primary focus.

Set up your TripAdvisor Management Center and claim your profile. Complete every field: description, cuisine type, price range, dietary options, atmosphere tags, and photos. TripAdvisor ranks restaurants within its listings based on a combination of review quantity, rating quality, and recency — the same fundamentals that apply elsewhere.

Direct travelers and first-time visitors toward TripAdvisor, particularly if you know they are from out of town. A guest who mentions they are visiting the city is the ideal TripAdvisor reviewer — they are likely already on the platform, they leave reviews as a travel habit, and their review will reach an audience of other travelers.

---

### OpenTable & Reservation Platforms

OpenTable, Resy, and similar reservation platforms send automated review prompts to guests after their reservation — this is built into the platform's functionality. Every diner who books through these systems will receive a post-meal review request without any action required from you. This makes optimizing your reservation platform profile critically important.

On OpenTable and Resy, your listing profile, photos, dining experience tags, and menu information all influence conversion from browsing to booking. Guests who discover you through the platform and book directly are then entered into the automated review flow. The quality of your listing determines how many guests enter that flow.

Respond to reviews left on OpenTable and Resy through your management dashboard. Many restaurants overlook these responses — which means a thoughtful, engaged response strategy immediately distinguishes you from competitors on the platform.

**Fake Review Response**

We invite you to contact us directly at [management email or phone number] so we can investigate further and, if there has been a genuine issue, address it properly. We remain committed to honest, responsive service for every real guest who visits us.

**How to Use This Tracker**

Fill in the tracker at the start of each month and again at the end. The "Start" column records your review count on the first of the month; the "End" column records it on the last day. The difference is your New Reviews count for the month. Over time, this data tells you whether your review generation efforts are working and whether your momentum is accelerating or stalling.

Track your average star rating monthly. A single bad week can drag down an average that took months to build, and catching a downward trend early allows you to investigate and respond before it compounds. If your average drops, cross-reference your Notable Feedback column — patterns in negative feedback usually reveal a specific issue (a new staff member, a menu change, a service gap) that can be addressed.

Use the Notable Feedback column to capture recurring themes — positive or negative. If three reviews in a single month mention the same dish, the same server, or the same atmosphere complaint, that is signal worth acting on. Share the tracker summary at your monthly team meeting. Reputation is a team metric, not a management one. When the kitchen knows how many new reviews came in and what guests said, it creates accountability and pride across the whole operation.
