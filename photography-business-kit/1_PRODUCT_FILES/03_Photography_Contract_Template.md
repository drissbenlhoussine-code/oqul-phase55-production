## 1. Scope of Services

Photographer agrees to provide professional photography services as described herein. The specific scope of the session is as follows:

**Coverage:** Photographer will provide _______ (___) hours of photography coverage on the Session Date listed above, beginning at _______ and concluding at _______. Any time beyond the agreed coverage period will be billed at $_______ per hour, invoiced after the session.

**Deliverables:** Photographer will deliver a minimum of _______ (___) professionally edited digital images selected at Photographer's sole artistic discretion. The final image count may exceed this minimum but is not guaranteed to do so. Photographer selects which images are delivered; unselected or rejected images will not be provided.

**Delivery Timeline:** Final edited images will be delivered within _______ (___) weeks of the session date via an online digital gallery, unless otherwise agreed upon in writing.

**What Is Included:**
- Professional photography coverage for the agreed number of hours
- Professional post-processing and color correction of selected images
- Online gallery delivery with download capability
- High-resolution digital files suitable for printing up to 20x30 inches

**What Is NOT Included Unless Separately Contracted or Specified in Writing:**
- Prints, albums, canvases, or physical products of any kind
- RAW (unedited) image files (see Section 8)
- Videography or video services
- Hair, makeup, or styling
- Props, permits, or location fees
- Second photographer coverage (unless noted in writing as an addition to this contract)
- Rush editing or expedited delivery
- Usage licenses beyond personal use (see Section 5)

Any services beyond what is described in this contract must be agreed upon in a signed addendum or written amendment.

## 3. Cancellation and Rescheduling Policy

**3.1 Client-Initiated Cancellation**

If Client cancels this contract for any reason, the following terms apply:

- **Cancellation 30 or more days prior to the Session Date:** Client forfeits the non-refundable retainer. Any additional amounts paid beyond the retainer will be refunded within 10 business days.
- **Cancellation 14–29 days prior to the Session Date:** Client forfeits the non-refundable retainer. Additionally, 50% of the remaining balance (if paid) will be retained by Photographer to compensate for lost bookings and preparation time. Any remaining amount paid will be refunded within 10 business days.
- **Cancellation fewer than 14 days prior to the Session Date:** The full contract amount is owed and non-refundable. If Client has paid in full, no refund will be issued. If Client has not yet paid the remaining balance, that balance remains owed.

All cancellations must be communicated in writing (email is acceptable) to Photographer. Verbal cancellation will not be recognized as official cancellation.

**3.2 Photographer-Initiated Cancellation**

In the unlikely event that Photographer must cancel this contract due to a serious emergency, illness, or circumstances beyond Photographer's control, Photographer will:

1. Notify Client as promptly as possible in writing.
2. Refund all payments received in full, including the retainer, within 10 business days.
3. Make a good-faith effort to refer Client to a qualified photographer of similar skill and style, though Photographer cannot guarantee availability of a replacement.

Photographer's liability in the event of cancellation is limited to a full refund of amounts paid. No additional compensation, damages, or reimbursement for ancillary costs (travel, attire, venue, etc.) will be owed.

**3.3 Rescheduling Policy**

If Client requests to reschedule the session, the following applies:

- **Rescheduling requests made 14 or more days in advance:** Photographer will make reasonable efforts to accommodate a new date. The retainer will transfer to the rescheduled date, provided the new date is available and booked within _______ (___) months of the original session date. A rescheduling fee of $_______ may apply for the second or subsequent reschedule.
- **Rescheduling requests made fewer than 14 days in advance:** The original retainer is forfeited. A new retainer will be required to secure the rescheduled date, as Photographer has likely turned away other clients to hold the original date.

Rescheduling is subject to Photographer's availability. If Photographer is unavailable on the requested new date, the cancellation policy in Section 3.1 will apply.

## 4. Weather and Force Majeure

**4.1 Outdoor Sessions — Weather Policy**

For outdoor sessions, Client and Photographer each bear the responsibility of monitoring weather in advance of the session date. If weather conditions are unsafe (lightning, severe storms, flooding, extreme heat advisories) or would significantly compromise the quality of images, either party may request to reschedule under the following terms:

- Requests to reschedule due to weather must be made no later than _______ hours before the scheduled session start time.
- Light rain, overcast skies, or mild weather do not constitute grounds for rescheduling. These conditions often produce beautiful, soft light, and Photographer will proceed unless conditions are unsafe.
- If both parties agree that weather makes the session inadvisable, the rescheduling provisions of Section 3.3 will apply and no additional fee will be charged for the first weather reschedule.
- If the session proceeds and weather deteriorates during the session, Photographer will use professional judgment to complete as much of the session as possible. No refund will be issued for sessions that have begun.

**4.2 Force Majeure**

Neither party will be held in breach of this contract if performance is prevented by circumstances beyond that party's reasonable control, including but not limited to: natural disasters, earthquakes, floods, fires, acts of God, war, terrorism, government-mandated shutdowns, pandemics, or other extraordinary events that make performance impossible or illegal.

In a force majeure event:
- Both parties will be excused from performance for the duration of the event.
- The parties will work in good faith to identify a mutually agreeable rescheduled date.
- The retainer will transfer to the rescheduled date at no additional cost for the first reschedule.
- If no mutually agreeable date can be found within _______ (___) months, Client may request a full refund of all amounts paid.

**4.3 COVID-19 / Illness Policy**

If Client or a primary subject of the session is ill on the session date, or has been exposed to a contagious illness within _______ days of the session:

- Client must notify Photographer as soon as possible.
- Photographer reserves the right to reschedule the session to protect the health of all parties.
- If rescheduled due to documented illness, the retainer will transfer to the new date at no penalty, subject to Photographer's availability.
- If Client simply chooses not to attend due to illness concerns without documented illness or exposure, the standard cancellation policy applies.

## 5. Model Release and Image Usage Rights

**5.1 Photographer's Right to Use Images**

Client grants Photographer a non-exclusive, perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free license to use, reproduce, display, and distribute any photographs taken under this contract for the following purposes: portfolio display (print and digital), Photographer's website, social media marketing, advertising, contest submissions, print displays at events, and any other legitimate promotional or business purpose. Photographer will handle all images with professionalism and will not use images in a manner that is defamatory, offensive, or inconsistent with the professional photography industry.

**5.2 Client Opt-Out of Model Release**

Client may opt out of the model release by initialing here: _______. If Client opts out, Photographer agrees not to publicly publish or use images from this session for promotional purposes. Note that opting out of the model release does not affect the session fee, and Photographer may still use images in private professional portfolios shared directly with prospective clients (not publicly posted).

**5.3 Client's Usage Rights**

In exchange for full payment of the contract amount, Photographer grants Client a non-exclusive, personal-use license to the delivered digital images. "Personal use" includes:
- Printing images for personal or home display
- Sharing images on Client's personal social media accounts
- Sending images to family and friends
- Creating personal photo gifts (albums, mugs, cards, etc.) for non-commercial purposes

**Client may NOT:**
- Sell, license, or sublicense the images to any third party
- Use images in advertising, commercial campaigns, or for any commercial purpose without a separate commercial use license from Photographer
- Submit images to stock photo libraries or agencies
- Remove, alter, or obscure Photographer's watermark (if used)
- Apply filters, heavy edits, or alterations that materially change the artistic nature of the images before posting publicly (light cropping is acceptable)

Commercial licensing is available upon request and is subject to a separate fee negotiated in writing.

Client agrees to respond to communications from Photographer within a reasonable timeframe (generally within _______ business days) for questions regarding session logistics, scheduling, or delivery. Delays in Client response that affect session preparation or delivery timelines are not the responsibility of Photographer.

## 7. Limitation of Liability

**7.1 Equipment and Technical Failure**

Photographer uses professional-grade equipment and takes all reasonable precautions to ensure equipment is in proper working order. However, in the unlikely event of equipment malfunction, memory card failure, file corruption, accidental damage, theft, or other technical failure beyond Photographer's reasonable control, Photographer's total liability to Client shall not exceed the total amount paid by Client under this contract.

**7.2 Data Loss**

Photographer uses redundant memory cards and/or backs up files promptly following sessions as a professional practice. However, in the event of catastrophic data loss, Photographer's liability is limited to a full refund of amounts paid. Photographer is not responsible for consequential damages, including but not limited to, the cost of re-staging a session, travel expenses, attire costs, or emotional distress.

**7.3 Cap on Liability**

Under no circumstances shall Photographer be liable for any indirect, incidental, consequential, special, or exemplary damages arising from this contract or the services provided, even if Photographer has been advised of the possibility of such damages. Photographer's maximum liability under this contract shall not exceed the total amount paid by Client to Photographer.

**7.4 Acts Outside Photographer's Control**

Photographer is not responsible for the failure to capture specific moments, expressions, or events due to circumstances outside Photographer's reasonable control, including: the actions or non-cooperation of third parties; venue restrictions on photography equipment or positioning; inadequate lighting conditions in venue-restricted environments; or interference by guests or other vendors.

Client agrees to contact Photographer directly (not through public reviews or social media posts) to raise any concerns, and Photographer agrees to respond within _______ (___) business days with a genuine effort to reach resolution.

**11.2 Mediation**

If good faith communication does not resolve the dispute within 30 days, the parties agree to participate in non-binding mediation with a mutually agreed-upon mediator before pursuing any other legal remedy. The cost of mediation will be shared equally by both parties.

**11.3 Arbitration**

If mediation is unsuccessful, the parties agree to resolve all disputes through binding arbitration in accordance with the rules of the American Arbitration Association (or a mutually agreed-upon arbitration service), rather than through litigation in court. The arbitrator's decision shall be final and binding, and judgment on the award rendered may be entered in any court having jurisdiction. Each party will bear its own attorney's fees and costs, unless the arbitrator determines that a party has acted in bad faith.

## Customization Notes for Photographers

Use these notes to tailor this contract to your specific business, jurisdiction, and shooting style. Read through each section before sending the contract to a client.

---

### Section 1: Scope of Services

**What to customize:** Fill in your standard coverage hours, minimum image count, and delivery timeline. These numbers will vary by session type — a 1-hour portrait session has very different deliverables than a 10-hour wedding. Consider creating separate versions of this contract (or a simple addendum) for each session type you offer.

**Why it matters:** The scope section is your protection against scope creep. Clients sometimes expect "all the photos" or assume prints are included. Spelling out what is and is not included prevents refund demands and awkward conversations after the fact.

**Common mistake:** Photographers often leave the minimum image count blank or vague. Be specific. If you say "a gallery of your favorite images," a client might expect 500 edited images from a 1-hour session. Set a real minimum, then routinely exceed it — clients love that.

**Practical tip:** Add your specific session types as separate addendum sheets (Portrait Session Addendum, Wedding Day Addendum) so you have one master contract with customized scope sheets attached.

---

### Section 2: Investment and Payment Terms

**What to customize:** Fill in your retainer amount (typically 25–50% of the total), your payment due date (7–14 days before the session is standard), your overtime rate, your late fee, and your accepted payment methods. Remove any payment methods you don't actually accept.

**Why it matters:** A clear payment schedule protects your income. The non-refundable retainer language is the single most important sentence in this contract — it's what compensates you for turning away other clients on that date. Make sure it's explicit, not buried.

**Common mistake:** Many photographers set the remaining balance due date too close to the session — sometimes the day of. This is stressful for everyone. Set the balance due 7–14 days out so any payment issues can be resolved before you arrive at the session.

**Practical tip:** Require the retainer to be paid at the same time as contract signing, not "within a few days." If they can sign but not pay, the date is not actually held. Most booking platforms (HoneyBook, Studio Ninja, Dubsado) can enforce simultaneous sign-and-pay workflows.

---

### Section 3: Cancellation and Rescheduling Policy

**What to customize:** Review the tiered cancellation percentages and adjust based on how quickly you can re-book a session. Photographers in high-demand markets may warrant stiffer cancellation terms. Fill in the rescheduling fee and the window for rescheduling (typically 6–12 months).

**Why it matters:** Without clear cancellation language, clients will expect full refunds even when they cancel the day before a session. The tiered structure here is fair to clients while protecting your business for the very real economic harm of a last-minute cancellation.

**Common mistake:** Photographers often say the retainer is "non-refundable" but then cave under pressure. Enforce your own terms consistently. If you make exceptions, document them in writing and understand that word travels — one refund outside policy can create a client expectation for others.

**Practical tip:** For weddings especially, consider requiring the remaining balance 30 days (not 14 days) before the event. This gives you more financial protection and aligns with what most venues and vendors require.

---

### Section 4: Weather and Force Majeure

**What to customize:** Fill in your weather communication window (most photographers use 24–48 hours) and your force majeure re-booking window. Update the COVID/illness exposure timeframe based on current health guidance in your area.

**Why it matters:** Weather disputes are among the most common sources of conflict for outdoor photographers. If you don't define what counts as "bad enough weather to reschedule," you'll face clients who want to cancel on a cloudy day. This clause sets clear expectations and prevents confusion.

**Common mistake:** Photographers often omit illness language entirely, which leaves a gap. The COVID clause was born of necessity and remains relevant for any contagious illness. Having clear language protects both you and your clients from an uncomfortable negotiation when someone is genuinely sick.

**Practical tip:** State explicitly that overcast and light rain are not automatic reschedule conditions. Clients often don't realize that soft, diffused overcast light is actually flattering for portraits. Educating clients on this before it becomes an issue builds trust and reduces last-minute anxiety.

---

### Section 5: Model Release and Image Usage Rights

**What to customize:** Review the permitted uses listed under your model release and confirm they align with how you actually use images. If you never enter contests or submit to magazines, remove those references. If you use images for paid advertising or sponsored content, that should be stated. Update the opt-out initial line location and make it visually prominent.

**Why it matters:** The model release is your legal permission to use client images in your portfolio and marketing. Without it, even posting an image to Instagram could technically constitute a privacy violation depending on your state's laws. Most clients are happy to grant this — but it needs to be explicit.

**Common mistake:** Photographers frequently post client images without any model release language in their contract at all, relying on the assumption that clients are "okay with it." This is a legal and relationship risk. Include it, make the opt-out clear, and honor it.

**Practical tip:** The opt-out initial line should be on the same page as the signature block if possible, so it's visible and intentional. If a client opts out, add a note to their client file so future social media posts are filtered accordingly.

---

### Section 6: Client Responsibilities

**What to customize:** Fill in the communication response window (2 business days is standard). For family photographers, consider adding specific language about pet behavior or client responsibility for children who are uncooperative. For wedding photographers, note who is responsible for coordinating the family formal shot list.

**Why it matters:** Setting client expectations in writing creates accountability. When a family session runs 30 minutes over because the client couldn't gather their group, this clause makes clear that the session time is still ending at the contracted time.

**Common mistake:** Photographers avoid putting responsibility on the client because they don't want to seem harsh. The opposite is true — clients appreciate knowing what's expected of them. Clear expectations actually lead to better sessions because clients come prepared.

**Practical tip:** In your pre-session email or guide (a great upsell addition to your kit), repeat the key client responsibilities in friendly, non-legal language. The contract sets the legal baseline; your client welcome guide sets the tone.

---

### Section 7: Limitation of Liability

**What to customize:** This section is intentionally conservative. Review the liability cap language with an attorney in your state — some jurisdictions have consumer protection rules that affect how much you can limit liability. Do not remove this section without legal counsel.

**Why it matters:** Equipment failure and data loss are rare but real. Without limitation of liability language, a court could theoretically hold you responsible for a client's full "damages" — including the dress, the venue, the catering, if a wedding photographer's card fails. The cap on liability is essential protection.

**Common mistake:** Photographers sometimes soften this language because it "sounds harsh." Do not. No language about liability will feel warm, and that's appropriate — this section exists for catastrophic edge cases, not routine interactions. Keep it clear and firm.

**Practical tip:** Invest in redundant memory cards, dual-slot cameras (where possible), and same-day or next-day backups. Not only does this protect your clients, it makes the limitation of liability section almost academic — you're unlikely to ever need it if your backup systems are solid.

---

### Section 8: Delivery Timeline and Format

**What to customize:** Fill in your standard turnaround time and be realistic. If your actual turnaround is 6 weeks during wedding season, put 6 weeks, not 3. Under-promising and over-delivering on timeline is far better than the reverse. Add your gallery platform name, gallery access duration, and archive policy.

**Why it matters:** Delivery timeline is the single most common source of client complaints in the photography industry. Setting it in writing — and not overpromising — protects you legally and manages expectations.

**Common mistake:** New photographers frequently promise 2-week turnaround and then book themselves into a position where it's impossible. If your busy season overlaps with typical session dates, build buffer time into your contracted timeline.

**Practical tip:** The "no RAW files" policy should be communicated at the booking stage, not discovered by clients afterward. Consider adding a sentence about it in your FAQ or pre-booking email so it's never a surprise. The brief "why" in this section (it's a working file, not a finished product) is intentionally included to educate clients without being defensive.

---

### Section 9: Second Shooter / Assistant Policy

**What to customize:** Fill in whether you regularly use a second shooter, and for which session types. If you never use a second shooter, you can simplify this section to two sentences or remove it entirely. If you do, have a separate second shooter agreement you require them to sign.

**Why it matters:** Clients book you specifically. If someone else shows up to shoot their wedding without notice, that can feel like a bait-and-switch. This clause gives you the flexibility to bring help while being transparent with clients upfront.

**Common mistake:** Photographers bring a second shooter without disclosing it — then the second shooter independently posts images on their own social media before the primary photographer delivers the gallery. This creates client confusion and potential model release issues. Have a second shooter contract template in your own files.

**Practical tip:** Even if you don't always use a second shooter, keep this clause in your contract. It gives you flexibility and doesn't create any obligation. If you do bring a second shooter, send a quick heads-up to the client 48 hours before — it's a trust builder, not a required disclosure, but it shows care.

---

### Section 10: Social Media and Portfolio Usage

**What to customize:** Fill in your typical sneak peek posting window and your embargo window. Update your social media handle. If you tag clients, confirm you're using the right platform handle — a wrong tag on a wedding post can create real friction.

**Why it matters:** Social media is one of your primary marketing channels. Protecting your right to post while respecting client preferences keeps both parties happy. The credit/tagging requirement is industry standard and most clients are glad to tag you when they know it helps your business.

**Common mistake:** Photographers post sneak peeks before clients have seen their images. This is a gift-before-the-gift situation — some clients are thrilled; others are mortified that a photo they haven't approved went live first. Establish your sneak peek window and stick to it. Many photographers send the sneak peek directly to the client first, then post it publicly.

**Practical tip:** Consider adding a line in your client welcome email that says: "I may post a sneak peek within [X days]. If you have any images you'd prefer I not post publicly, please let me know within [X hours] of receiving your gallery." This creates a collaborative relationship around posting.

---

### Section 11: Dispute Resolution

**What to customize:** Fill in your state name and county for the governing law clause. If you are not based in the U.S., replace the arbitration organization reference with the applicable body in your country. Review with your attorney whether binding arbitration is standard and enforceable in your state.

**Why it matters:** Without a dispute resolution clause, any disagreement defaults to the court system, which is expensive, slow, and unpredictable. The good faith → mediation → arbitration escalation in this contract gives both parties multiple chances to resolve things before it becomes a legal battle.

**Common mistake:** Many photographers skip mediation and go straight to arbitration or omit this clause entirely. The good faith communication requirement alone prevents a significant number of disputes from escalating — it forces the client to come to you directly before going public with a complaint.

**Practical tip:** When a dispute arises, respond with empathy first, facts second. Most unhappy clients want to feel heard. A calm, professional response to a complaint — referencing the relevant contract clause without being combative — resolves the majority of disputes without ever reaching mediation.

### Section 12: Entire Agreement and Severability

**What to customize:** Minimal customization needed here. If you have a specific platform you use for electronic signatures, you may name it. Review the electronic signature language with an attorney if you operate in a jurisdiction that has specific e-signature rules.

**Why it matters:** The entire agreement clause ensures that verbal promises ("oh don't worry, I'll include a print package") made during the sales conversation can't be enforced against you unless they're in the written contract. This protects you from selective memory on both sides.

**Common mistake:** Photographers make verbal commitments during client calls that contradict the contract — then feel obligated to honor them. Train yourself to say: "Let me add that to the contract addendum," any time you make a promise that isn't already in the contract language.

**Practical tip:** Send the contract within 24 hours of the discovery call while the conversation is still fresh. The longer the gap between booking conversation and contract signing, the more opportunity for "I thought you said..." conflicts to develop.

### Section 13: Signatures

**What to customize:** For couples or co-clients on a wedding contract, both signature blocks should be used and both parties should sign. For solo portrait or branding clients, the second signature block is optional — you may remove it or note "N/A."

**Why it matters:** A signature creates legal accountability. Both parties signing confirms that both have read and accepted the terms. For weddings especially, having both partners sign removes the "my partner signed but I didn't agree to that" defense.

**Common mistake:** Photographers send contracts and then begin working before receiving a signed copy back. Do not begin preparing for, traveling to, or shooting any session until you have a signed contract and received the retainer. Period.

**Practical tip:** Use a booking platform with contract and payment integration (HoneyBook, Dubsado, Studio Ninja, or 17hats) so signing and payment happen simultaneously. This eliminates the "I signed but forgot to pay" scenario and gives you a timestamped, legally documented record of both.
