# Client Inquiry & Booking Scripts: Complete Communication System

## How to Use These Scripts

These templates are written to sound like a real person — because you are one. Before you use them, do a quick read-through and swap in your name, your business name, your pricing, and any detail that reflects how you actually work. The bracketed fields like [YOUR NAME] are the obvious ones, but also look for places where a sentence might not match your style and rewrite it in your own voice. These scripts are a starting point, not a straitjacket.

A few practical notes:

**Response time matters more than you think.** Aim to respond to new inquiries within 2-4 hours during business hours. Photographers who respond within the hour book at significantly higher rates. If you can't respond personally that fast, set up a simple auto-reply that acknowledges receipt and sets the expectation for when they'll hear from you.

**Don't copy-paste cold.** Read the inquiry before choosing a template. If someone mentions they're planning an intimate elopement, don't send the full wedding template without acknowledging that. One or two personalized sentences at the top go a long way.

**Use a consistent sending system.** Whether that's Gmail templates, Honeybook, Dubsado, or a folder of saved drafts, have a system so you're not rewriting from scratch every time. These scripts are designed to live in that system.

**Track everything.** Use the Inquiry Tracking Log in Section 8 to stay on top of follow-ups. Leads go cold fast, and a simple spreadsheet keeps nothing falling through the cracks.

## SECTION 2: Pricing Conversation Scripts

---

### Presenting Investment Verbally (on a call)

The most common mistake photographers make when discussing pricing is apologizing for it — either directly ("I know it's a lot") or through tone (trailing off, rushing through the numbers, over-explaining). Your pricing reflects the value you deliver. Present it that way.

**The setup before you say the number:**

Before you quote a price, anchor it in value. This isn't manipulation — it's context. Say something like:

*"What I do is [describe your approach in one or two sentences]. Most of my clients tell me [specific outcome — e.g., 'they finally have photos they're proud to hang in their home' / 'they felt completely at ease on their wedding day']. My [session/collection] investment starts at [$XXX], and here's what that includes..."*

Then list what's included clearly and confidently.

**After you say the number, stop talking.**

Seriously. The impulse to fill silence after quoting a price with justification, comparisons, or softening language works against you. Say the number, share what's included, and let them respond. Silence after a price quote is normal. It's not rejection.

**If they say "Okay, how do we get started?" — great. Move forward.**

**If they pause or say "Let me think about it" — say this:**

*"Of course — that's completely fair. Is there anything about the investment or what's included that would be helpful to talk through? I'd rather you have all the information you need to make the decision that's right for you."*

This invites the real objection without pressure.

### What NOT to Say When Discussing Price

Avoid these phrases — they undermine your value before the client has a chance to respond:

- "I know it's a lot..." — You're doing their objecting for them.
- "I'm not the cheapest, but..." — Never compare yourself to cheaper options. It invites the comparison you don't want.
- "It's really worth it because..." (before they've objected) — Over-justifying before anyone pushes back signals insecurity.
- "I can probably work something out..." — Said before they've even asked. Don't offer discounts preemptively.
- "My prices are [X], but it depends..." — Vague pricing creates confusion and invites negotiation.
- Rambling after you say the number. Say it. Stop. Let them react.

## SECTION 6: Phone/Video Consultation Script

---

### Opening — Build Rapport, Set the Agenda (First 2-3 Minutes)

*[Answer warmly, use their name immediately]*

"Hi [NAME], so glad we connected! How are you doing?"

*[Let them respond — actually listen, don't just wait to talk]*

"Good, good. So I've got [20/30] minutes blocked for us today — I want to make sure we use it well. Here's what I'm thinking: I'd love to start by hearing more about [your wedding/your session/your family] — like actually hearing about it, not just the logistics. Then I can share a bit about how I work, and we can figure out together if it makes sense to take the next step. Does that work for you?"

*[This sets an agenda so they know what to expect and signals that you're organized and intentional.]*

---

### Discovery Questions — What to Ask (At Least 8)

Don't rapid-fire these. Ask one, listen fully, and let the conversation breathe. Some will naturally lead into others.

**For wedding clients:**
1. "Tell me about your wedding — what does the day look like so far? Do you have a venue, a vibe, a feel you're going for?"
2. "What's the most important part of the day for you personally — is it the ceremony, the reception, the getting-ready moments, all of it?"
3. "When you think about looking at your wedding photos ten years from now, what do you want to feel?"
4. "Have you had professional photos taken before — either personally or at events? How did that experience go?"
5. "What's made you hesitant or uncertain about booking a photographer, if anything?"
6. "How did you find me / hear about me?"
7. "Do you have a budget range in mind for photography? I ask because it helps me point you toward the right package rather than guessing."
8. "Is there anything specific — a detail, a moment, a person — that you absolutely need captured?"

**For portrait/family clients:**
1. "What's prompting this session — is there a specific occasion, or is it just time?"
2. "Have you done a professional photo session before? If so, what did you love about it, and what would you change?"
3. "If you could describe the feeling of the photos you want, what words come to mind?"
4. "Who's going to be in the session? Tell me about them."
5. "Is there a location that means something to your family, or are you open to suggestions?"
6. "What's your biggest concern going into this — anything you're nervous about?"
7. "What are you planning to do with the photos — prints, albums, digital, all of the above?"
8. "What would make you walk away from this session feeling like it was completely worth it?"

---

### Transitioning from Listening to Presenting

Once you've asked your questions and they've had a chance to share, transition like this:

*"Thank you for sharing all of that — I feel like I actually know what this means to you, which is helpful. Can I tell you a bit about how I work and then we can see if it fits?"*

Then share:
- Your approach in 2-3 sentences (how you shoot, how you interact, what you're focused on capturing)
- A brief story or example that's relevant to what they shared ("You mentioned you're worried about feeling awkward in front of the camera — I had a client recently who said the same thing, and here's what happened...")
- What the experience of working with you looks like from start to finish (timeline, what to expect, what they receive)

Keep this portion to 5-7 minutes. Don't lecture. Invite responses: "Does that resonate with what you're looking for?"

---

### Closing the Consultation Toward Booking

After you've shared your approach and walked through your packages, bring it to a close clearly:

*"So — based on everything you've shared, I think [PACKAGE/COLLECTION NAME] would be the best fit for you because [specific reason tied to what they said]. The investment for that is [$XXX], which includes [key inclusions]."*

Then, the most important move: **stop talking and let them respond.**

If they're ready to book:
*"Perfect. Here's what happens next: I'll send you a contract and invoice within [24 hours]. Once those are signed and the deposit is in, your date is locked. Does that sound good?"*

If they want time to think:
*"Totally understand — this is a real investment and it should feel right. Is there anything I haven't answered that would help you make the decision? [Pause, listen.] Great. I'll send you a follow-up email with everything we talked about today so you have it all in one place. Take a look and just reply when you're ready."*

Then do exactly that — send a short follow-up email the same day while the call is still fresh.

**Tips for using your tracking log effectively:**

- Review it every Monday morning. Look at anything with a follow-up date from the previous week and make sure it was handled.
- When you mark something "Booked," note the package and final price in the Notes column. Over time this data shows you which session types and price points convert best.

- "No Response" is different from "Lost." Don't write off a lead after one follow-up. Use the three-touch sequence in Section 3 before you close the file.
