### How to Share Your Photos on Social Media

You just got your gallery back and you're ready to share — here's how to do it in a way that shows off the photos (and your photographer's work) at their best.

---

**Step 1: Download First, Post Second**

The number-one thing I ask is this: please download your images from the gallery before posting them, rather than screenshotting them from your phone. Screenshots cut the resolution way down, and your photos will look softer and less vibrant than they actually are. The downloaded files are the real deal — sharp, color-accurate, and ready to shine on any screen.

How to get them on your phone from your download:
- Email yourself the files from your computer
- Use AirDrop (Mac to iPhone) or Bluetooth transfer (Android)
- Upload to Google Photos or iCloud from your computer and access from your phone
- Download directly to your phone from the gallery app if the platform supports it

---

**Tagging Your Photographer**

It means more than you know. When you post, please tag:
- Instagram: **[YOUR IG HANDLE]**
- Facebook: **[YOUR FACEBOOK PAGE NAME]**

Tagging helps other people find me, and it's one of the most meaningful ways you can support a small photography business. If you're posting to stories, a mention is perfect. If it's a feed post, a tag in the photo and a credit in the caption is ideal.

Example caption credit: *"Photos by [YOUR BUSINESS NAME] | [YOUR IG HANDLE]"*

---

**Hashtags You Can Use**

Feel free to include any of these in your posts:
- [YOUR BRANDED HASHTAG, e.g., #JaneSmithPhoto]
- [LOCAL COMMUNITY HASHTAG, e.g., #AustinFamilyPhotographer]
- [SESSION-TYPE HASHTAG, e.g., #WeddingPhotography / #FamilyPortraits / #BrandingPhotography]

---

**A Note on Filters and Editing**

I spend a lot of time carefully editing your images — the colors, the light, the contrast — to make them look exactly the way they do. A small, light Instagram filter is totally fine. But heavy filters, extreme color shifts, or high-contrast presets can really change the feel of the work.

My request: if you're going to edit, keep it subtle. If something looks off or you want a different overall feel, reach out and let's talk — I'd rather adjust the files for you than have photos floating around that don't represent either of us well.

---

**Stories vs. Feed Posts**

- **Stories:** Grab your image, post it, and tag me. Stories are casual and perfect for sharing quickly.
- **Feed posts:** Take a minute on the caption. Something personal about the session, the moment, or what the photos mean to you will always get more engagement than just a location tag.
- **Reels/TikTok:** If you want to use your photos in a slideshow or video, go for it — just credit me in the caption.

---

**What I'd Love to Be Tagged In**

Honestly, all of it — but especially:
- Your favorite single image
- A before/after of your session (if you want to show outfit choices, etc.)
- Any printed products you order using your photos
- Holiday cards, announcements, or gifts made from your images

I repost client content regularly on my Instagram stories and sometimes on my feed (with your permission). If you'd prefer I don't repost, just let me know — completely understood.

### Your Guide to Printing These Photos the Right Way

You've got beautiful, high-resolution files. Here's how to turn them into something you'll actually hold in your hands — and love for years.

---

**Why Not CVS, Walgreens, or the Grocery Store**

I get it — it's convenient. But consumer retail printing is calibrated for snapshots, not professional photography. The color profiles are off, the paper quality is thin, and the result often looks faded, greenish, or flat compared to what you see on your screen. It's genuinely a shame when a stunning photo gets printed on paper that does it no justice.

Professional print labs calibrate their printers to match the color space your photos were edited in. The difference is immediately visible, and the longevity is significantly better — professional prints won't fade the way drugstore prints do.

---

**What to Look for in a Print Lab**

A good print lab will:
- Offer ICC profile downloads (so you can soft-proof if you're color-savvy)
- Have paper options beyond basic glossy (lustre/satin, matte, fine art, metallic)
- Provide a size chart with resolution recommendations
- Have consistent, reviewable quality (check photography forums, not just their own website)

Labs I personally trust and recommend:
- **[PRINT LAB NAME 1]** — [BRIEF DESCRIPTION, e.g., "Excellent for fine art prints and wedding albums. Consistent color. Slightly slower turnaround but worth it."]
- **[PRINT LAB NAME 2]** — [BRIEF DESCRIPTION, e.g., "Great for canvas and metal prints. Fast shipping, excellent packaging."]
- **[PRINT LAB NAME 3]** — [BRIEF DESCRIPTION, e.g., "My go-to for holiday cards and smaller prints. Affordable without sacrificing quality."]

If you'd like me to place an order on your behalf through my professional account (which often comes with better pricing), just ask. I'm happy to help. **[ADJUST THIS OFFER BASED ON YOUR WORKFLOW]**

---

**File Resolution and Print Size — In Plain English**

Your downloaded files are high-resolution, which means they contain a lot of pixel information — more than enough for large prints.

Here's a rough guide to what your files can handle:
- A file from a modern DSLR or mirrorless camera can typically print beautifully up to **16x20 inches**, and often larger
- For a **4x6 or 5x7** print, almost any digital file will look sharp
- For **canvas wraps 24 inches or larger**, you'll want to make sure you're using the full-resolution download (not a web export or screenshot)
- For **very large format** prints (30x40 or bigger), ask me first — some compositions work better than others at that scale

The rule of thumb: if you downloaded the file from the gallery (not screenshotted it), you're almost certainly in great shape for anything up to a standard large print.

---

**Recommended Products by Session Type**

**Family Portraits**
- **Canvas wraps** are classic and warm — they work in living rooms and hallways and feel like art
- **Framed fine art prints** in a series (3-5 images displayed together) are a beautiful wall arrangement
- **Photo books** are underrated — a professionally bound album of your family session is something kids will look at for their entire lives

**Wedding Photography**
- **Wedding albums** are the obvious choice, and worth every penny — a bound album is an heirloom
- **Fine art prints** of your ceremony, first look, or reception details for wall display

**Branding Photography**
- **Metal or acrylic prints** for office display — they look sleek and professional
- **Framed prints** for studio, shop, or office walls
- **Printed collateral** — some clients use branding images for business cards, brochures, or signage (check your licensing if you're unsure what's included)

**Portrait Sessions (Individual / Senior / Headshots)**
- **Framed prints** in sizes that work for a desk or bedroom wall
- **Canvas wraps** for a standout single image
- **Digital use** — headshot clients especially benefit from web-optimized versions for LinkedIn, websites, and email signatures

---

**Gifting Ideas**

Your photos make genuinely meaningful gifts. Consider:
- A framed print or canvas for grandparents after a family session
- A custom photo book as a anniversary gift using wedding or couple portraits
- A set of holiday cards using your session images
- A desk print or framed headshot for a professional in your family
- A photo blanket or pillow (many print labs offer these) — great for grandparents who want something cozy
- A gallery wall arrangement gifted as a set of coordinated prints

## TEMPLATE 7: What to Do With Your Photos — Client Usage Guide

### What to Do With Your Photos: A Complete Guide

You've got your gallery. Now what? Here's everything you're allowed to do, some ideas you might not have thought of, and a few things to be aware of.

---

**Personal Social Media Use**

Yes, please — share away. Posting your photos on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Pinterest, or anywhere else you like is completely encouraged. A few things that make a difference:

- Download the files before posting (don't screenshot)
- Tag your photographer: **[YOUR IG HANDLE]** / **[YOUR FACEBOOK PAGE]**
- Keep filters subtle so the editing holds up

---

**Printing**

Your files are high-resolution and ready to print. Here's a rough guide to what works where:

- **4x6 or 5x7:** Perfect for everyday frames, desks, and albums
- **8x10 or 11x14:** Great for a bedside table, office, or gallery wall
- **16x20 or 20x24:** A statement piece — a single hero image for a wall
- **24x30 and larger:** Works beautifully for canvas or large format prints; check your file size first

For a full printing guide with lab recommendations, see the separate printing document included with your gallery delivery.

---

**Photo Albums and Books**

A professionally printed photo book is one of the most underused options for photography clients, and one of the most rewarding. Services like **[PHOTO BOOK SERVICE PLACEHOLDER, e.g., Artifact Uprising, Chatbooks, Milk Books]** let you design and order a hardcover bound book using your downloaded files.

Weddings, family sessions, senior portraits, and maternity shoots all make stunning albums. This is how photos stop living only on phones and start living on coffee tables.

---

**Gifts**

Your photos make thoughtful, personal gifts. Ideas:

- **Framed prints** for grandparents, parents, or significant others
- **Canvas wraps** for a living room or hallway
- **Photo books** (see above) as holiday or anniversary gifts
- **Custom greeting cards** — holiday cards, thank-you cards, birth announcements
- **Photo blankets or pillows** (great for family and pet portraits)
- **Metal or acrylic prints** for modern, office-friendly display
- **Calendars** using 12 of your favorite images from the year

Many print labs ship directly to the gift recipient, which makes ordering simple.

---

**Digital Use**

- **Phone wallpaper:** Set your favorite image as your lock screen or home screen
- **Computer wallpaper:** Works beautifully on large desktop monitors, especially landscape or wide-format crops
- **Holiday cards:** Upload your files to any card-making platform
- **Email signatures:** Especially useful for branding clients — a professional headshot in your email signature makes an immediate impression
- **Website and LinkedIn:** For headshot and branding clients, your files are sized for web use. Update your profiles.

---

**What You Can't Do With Your Photos**

Your photos are licensed for personal and personal brand use. Specifically:

- **You may not sell your photos** to stock agencies, publications, or third parties
- **You may not license your photos** for commercial use by other businesses without a separate commercial licensing agreement
- **You may not remove the photographer's watermark** (if applicable) or claim the photos were taken by someone else
- **Branding clients:** Your photos are licensed for use in your own brand — website, social, marketing materials. They are not licensed for use by other companies or for sublicensing to others.

If you have a specific use case you're unsure about, just ask. Licensing is flexible, and there's usually a straightforward path to getting what you need.

---

**Archiving Your Photos — Back Them Up**

Please don't let your gallery expiration be the only place your photos live. Once you download them:

1. **Save to your computer** in a clearly labeled folder (e.g., "Smith Family Photos — June 2026")
2. **Back up to cloud storage** — Google Photos, iCloud, Dropbox, or Amazon Photos all work well
3. **Consider a second backup** — an external hard drive for photos you really want to protect (wedding photos especially)

The "3-2-1 rule" is a good standard: 3 copies of important files, on 2 different types of storage, with 1 copy offsite (cloud counts). Your memories are worth the extra 10 minutes to set this up.
