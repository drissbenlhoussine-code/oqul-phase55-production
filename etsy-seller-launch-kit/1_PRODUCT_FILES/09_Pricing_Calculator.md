## SECTION 2 — PRICING STRATEGY GUIDE

### The 4 Pricing Approaches

**1. Cost-Plus Pricing**
Add up all costs, add your desired margin.
- Best for: Physical products with clear cost structures
- Formula: `Total Cost × (1 + Profit Margin %)`
- Example: $8 in materials + $10 labor + $2 packaging = $20 cost. 50% margin = $30 price.

**2. Value-Based Pricing**
Price based on the value delivered to the buyer, not your cost.
- Best for: Digital products, templates, tools
- Ask: "How much time or money does this save the buyer?"
- Example: A proposal template saves 3 hours at $50/hr = $150 in value. Selling at $29 = huge value.

**3. Competitive Pricing**
Research competitors and price at, above, or below them deliberately.
- At: Compete on quality (photos, descriptions, brand)
- Above: Premium positioning — justify it with better photos and perceived quality
- Below: NOT recommended — buyers associate low price with low quality on Etsy

**4. Anchor Pricing (Bundles)**
Show a higher "original" value so your price feels like a deal.
- "5 templates separately: $75 — Bundle: $39" (saves $36)
- Creates perceived value even if you've never sold the items individually

## SECTION 4 — PRICING PSYCHOLOGY TIPS

**Price endings that work on Etsy:**

| Ending | Effect | Use When |
|---|---|---|
| X.99 | Classic discount feeling | Budget-conscious buyers |
| X.97 | Feels like a calculated deal | Mid-range products |
| X.00 | Premium / clean feeling | High-end products |
| X.50 | Neutral | Any price point |

**Best practices:**
- $14.97 vs. $15.00 — the former consistently outperforms in A/B tests
- $29 feels meaningfully cheaper than $30 (left-digit anchoring)
- Free shipping (baked into price) outperforms charging for shipping separately

**Sale pricing strategy:**
- Original price should be real and stable (Etsy monitors for fake markups)
- Run sales for no more than 30% of the time (buyers wait for sales if they happen too often)
- Best sale %. 20–30% off drives urgency without eroding perceived value
- Flash sales (48–72 hours) outperform permanent discounts

## SECTION 6 — WHEN TO RAISE YOUR PRICES

**Signs you should raise your price:**
- [ ] Conversion rate is above 5% (buyers are jumping at it)
- [ ] You're getting frequent "is this still available?" messages
- [ ] You've accumulated 50+ 5-star reviews (social proof justifies premium)
- [ ] Competitors have raised their prices
- [ ] You can't keep up with demand (physical products)

**How to raise prices without losing buyers:**
1. Increase by 10–15% at a time
2. Add a new photo or improve description simultaneously (reinforce value)
3. Monitor conversion rate for 14 days — if it drops below half your previous rate, revert

**Signs your price is too high:**
- Many views, very few sales (conversion under 0.5%)
- "This is expensive" in reviews
- Buyer abandons cart frequently (check Etsy Stats)
