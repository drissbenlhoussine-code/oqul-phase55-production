**#6**
Earnest money vs. down payment — these are NOT the same thing.

**Earnest money:** A deposit (typically 1–3% of purchase price) paid when your offer is accepted. It shows you're serious. If the deal closes, it applies to your down payment. If you back out for non-contingency reasons, you may lose it.

**Down payment:** The portion of the purchase price you pay at closing (3–20%+ depending on your loan type). This is separate from earnest money.

Understanding this before you make an offer prevents expensive surprises.

**#11**
Rate vs. APR: the number most buyers focus on is the wrong one.

**Interest rate:** The cost of borrowing money, expressed as a percentage.
**APR (Annual Percentage Rate):** The interest rate PLUS lender fees, expressed annually.

Two lenders can offer the same interest rate with very different total costs. The APR gives you the apples-to-apples comparison.

When shopping lenders: ask for the APR, not just the rate. And always compare loan estimates from at least 2–3 lenders.

**#12**
FHA vs. Conventional loans: which is right for you?

**FHA Loan:**
• Down payment: as low as 3.5%
• Credit score: 580+ (or 500 with 10% down)
• Requires mortgage insurance (for the life of the loan if < 10% down)
• Best for: first-time buyers with limited savings or credit building

**Conventional Loan:**
• Down payment: as low as 3–5%
• Credit score: typically 620+
• PMI removed once you reach 20% equity
• Best for: buyers with stronger credit and more savings

There's no universally "better" option — it depends on your situation.

**#20**
Title insurance: why you need it and what it actually covers.

Title insurance protects you from claims against the property's ownership history — things that happened before you bought it.

Without it, someone could come forward after closing claiming they have a right to the property: an heir, an unpaid contractor lien, a fraudulent transfer.

**Owner's title policy:** Protects you.
**Lender's title policy:** Required by your lender. Protects them.

You need both.

It's a one-time fee paid at closing — typically $500–$2,000 depending on purchase price and state. It protects you for as long as you own the home.

Worth every penny. One of the questions you should always ask your closing attorney or title company.

**#24**
What a low appraisal actually means — and your options.

Your lender won't loan more than the appraised value. If the home appraises below your agreed purchase price, you have options:

**Option 1:** Renegotiate — ask the seller to lower the price to the appraised value.
**Option 2:** Make up the difference in cash (pay the "appraisal gap" out of pocket).
**Option 3:** Walk away — if your contract includes an appraisal contingency.
**Option 4:** Challenge the appraisal — your agent and lender can request a rebuttal with additional comps.

What you should NOT do: close without understanding which option you're choosing and why.

I've navigated low appraisals for multiple clients. DM me if you're facing one.
