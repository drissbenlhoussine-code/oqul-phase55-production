# Fitness Business Operations & Marketing Systems Guide

## How to Use This Guide

Good marketing templates only work if you have a system behind them. This guide is the operational layer: how to create content consistently without burning out, how to price your services correctly, how to run reputation management as a weekly routine, and how to read your monthly numbers so you can make smart decisions. Start with the content workflow (Section 1) and the metrics tracker (Section 7) — those two alone will change how you run your week.

The goal isn't to implement everything at once. Pick one section per week and build it into your workflow. In a month, you'll have the full system running.

## SECTION 1 — CONTENT WORKFLOW SYSTEM

### The 2-Hour Sunday Batching Session

**Step 1 — Review the content calendar (10 minutes)**
Open Template 8 (30-Day Content Calendar). What pillar is scheduled for each day this week? What class or program is being promoted? Any member spotlights or challenge posts planned? Write down the 4-5 pieces of content you need to create.

**Step 2 — Identify visual assets (10 minutes)**
Which classes are happening this week that you could film or photograph? Schedule a 15-minute window before or after a class for this. You need one video (Reel) and 2-3 still images for the week at minimum.

**Step 3 — Film and photograph (30 minutes)**
Use this week's list from Step 2. Film one Reel (exercise demo, class preview, or BTS moment), capture stills for carousel or single image posts. Batch during the session if you can, or plan exactly when you'll capture during the week.

**Step 4 — Write or customize captions (20 minutes)**
Pull from Template 1 (caption bank), match each caption to a photo or video, make any customizations — swap in a class name, a member's name (with permission), or a specific detail that makes it feel like it's from you.

**Step 5 — Create graphics (20 minutes)**
Open Canva, use your studio template, create any promotional graphics needed — challenge announcement, new class promo, quote card.

**Step 6 — Schedule (10 minutes)**
Upload posts to Later or Buffer, add captions and hashtags, schedule for optimal posting times.

**Step 7 — Set a mid-week Story reminder (5 minutes)**
Stories should feel in-the-moment — don't schedule them in advance. Add a Wednesday calendar block to post a behind-the-scenes Story.

---

### Free Tools for Fitness Content Creation

**Canva (Free)** — Studio-branded templates, quote cards, promotional graphics, carousel slides. Create a brand kit once with your colors and fonts, then every graphic takes 5 minutes.

**Later (Free tier)** — Schedule Instagram and Facebook posts up to 30 per month. Visual calendar makes it easy to see your week at a glance.

**Buffer (Free tier)** — Alternative to Later. Also schedules Google Business posts, which Later doesn't.

**CapCut (Free)** — Best app for Reel editing from your phone. Easy slow-motion, music sync, text overlays, and auto-captions. Built specifically for the content formats fitness studios use most.

**Mailchimp (Free up to 500 contacts)** — Email newsletters and automated welcome sequences. Connects to most booking platforms for list import.

**Mindbody / Glofox / MindBody Online** — Your booking software. Most have built-in review request automation — activate this if you haven't.

**Google Business Profile (Free)** — Weekly posts, review management, Q&A. Your single most important local marketing channel.

**Meta Business Suite (Free)** — Manage Facebook and Instagram posts and paid ads from one place. Use this to monitor comments and messages across both platforms.

### The Posting Schedule That Works for Fitness Studios

**Minimum viable frequency:** 4 posts per week (1 Reel, 2 feed posts, 1 Story per day minimum)

**Growth frequency:** 6-7 feed posts per week, daily Stories, 3 Reels per week

**Best days for fitness content:**
- Monday: "Monday Motivation" consistently outperforms — use Pillar 5 engagement captions or Pillar 1 transformation captions
- Wednesday: Educational content performs well mid-week (form tips, recovery advice)
- Friday: Community and transformation content — people are in a reflective mood heading into the weekend
- Saturday: Events, challenges, outdoor workouts — weekend activity-driver content

**Best posting times:**
- 6:00–8:00 AM: Catches early morning workout crowd before they head to the gym
- 11:00 AM–1:00 PM: Lunch scroll — people checking their phone between commitments
- 7:00–9:00 PM: Evening wind-down — high engagement after the workday

**Content type frequency:**
- Reels: 3x per week minimum — exercise demos get saved and shared more than anything else
- Stories: Daily or near-daily — raw, unpolished, in-the-moment
- Carousel posts: 1-2 per week — educational content performs well as carousels
- Single images: 2-3 per week — member spotlights, quotes, promotional

## SECTION 2 — VIDEO & CONTENT CREATION FOR FITNESS

### The 5 Types of Video Every Fitness Studio Needs

**1. Exercise Demo Reel**
Film at a 45-degree angle or straight front. Demonstrate with perfect form. Add text overlay with the exercise name and one coaching cue. Keep it 15-30 seconds. These get saved and shared more than anything else you post — they're evergreen and will continue to drive traffic for months.

**2. Class Preview / Vibe Clip**
30-60 seconds of a real class in action: people lifting, sweating, laughing, giving each other high-fives. Show the atmosphere, not just the exercise. Add music that matches the class energy. This is your most powerful recruitment tool — people join when they can see themselves in the room.

**3. Transformation Story**
Before-and-after photos or video (always with written member consent). Narrate in caption or voiceover: where they started, the turning point, where they are now, one quote from the member. These generate the most DMs and new member inquiries of any content type.

**4. Coach / Staff Introduction**
Film the instructor in their natural element: teaching a cue, warming up, explaining a movement. Add text overlay: name, specialty, one-sentence coaching philosophy. Run these for every new hire and periodically for your existing team — members want to feel they know the people coaching them.

**5. Behind the Scenes**
The studio at 5am. Setting up equipment before the first class. Creating the playlist. Chalking the whiteboard workout. Counting inventory. Unpolished, authentic. This content builds the kind of trust that polished marketing cannot buy.

---

### Lighting Your Fitness Content

**The overhead fluorescent problem:** Most gyms use overhead fluorescent lighting, which creates unflattering downward shadows and washes out colors. Avoid shooting directly under them whenever possible.

**Best light source:** Natural window light. Position subjects near windows. The ideal time to film is before service begins when the studio is empty and you can use window light without equipment in the way.

**For evening classes with no window light:** A small LED ring light or LED panel (under $40) positioned at 45 degrees to the subject dramatically improves quality. This is the most cost-effective equipment upgrade you can make.

**Phone camera tips:**
- Tap and hold on your subject to lock focus and exposure before recording
- Turn off flash — it flattens the image and looks amateurish
- Use the rear-facing camera for quality; front camera for authenticity in Stories
- Clean your lens before filming

---

### Camera Angles for Fitness Content

**Front-facing (camera at chest height):** Best for upper body exercises, shows facial expression, works well for instructional content where you want to connect with the viewer.

**Side profile (camera at hip height):** Best for squats, deadlifts, lunges — shows depth and form clearly. Essential for any exercise where the sagittal plane matters.

**45-degree front angle:** Most versatile. Works for compound movements, functional exercises, any time you want to show both the front and the side without fully committing to either.

**Overhead (camera above looking down):** Best for floor work — yoga sequences, core exercises, ab workouts. Shows the full movement pattern clearly.

**The one angle to never use:** Looking up at the person. It distorts proportion, looks amateurish, and makes the subject look bigger than they are — rarely flattering.

---

### Filming Reels Without Disrupting Class

- Set up your phone on a gorilla tripod (under $15) before class starts
- Film 30-60 seconds of class in action without interacting with the camera during the session
- Designate 15-minute content shoots before or after a class 2-3 times per week with willing members
- Never stop class to adjust the camera or reshoot — authenticity beats production quality every time
- Get blanket consent from members when they join, or ask individually before filming anyone recognizable

---

### Editing Fitness Content in CapCut

1. Import your clip and trim to 15-30 seconds
2. Add trending audio from CapCut's library or use your own
3. Add text overlay for the exercise name or coaching cue (keep font clean and readable)
4. Tap "Auto Captions" for any spoken instruction — watch time increases significantly when captions are on
5. Export at 1080p (or 4K if your phone supports it)
6. Post directly from CapCut or save to your camera roll for scheduling in Later

## SECTION 3 — PRICING YOUR FITNESS SERVICES

### Membership vs. Drop-In vs. Package

**Memberships (recurring revenue):** The backbone of a sustainable studio. Monthly auto-pay creates predictable cash flow and encourages habit formation — members who commit financially come more often. Target: memberships should represent 70%+ of your revenue.

**Drop-In Classes:** Necessary for prospects to try before committing. Price drop-ins significantly higher than the per-class cost of a membership to make membership the obvious value choice. If your unlimited membership is $150/month and you run 20 classes, that's $7.50 per class — drop-in should be $22-28.

**Session Packages (personal training):** 5-session, 10-session, and 20-session bundles with a discount per session as quantity increases. Non-refundable but transferable. Create 90-day expiration windows to encourage consistent attendance.

---

### Pricing New Services

1. Calculate your cost to deliver: instructor time, space cost, equipment wear, admin overhead
2. Research 3 local competitors at a similar quality level
3. Set your price above your cost and at or slightly above market
4. Run at that price for 60 days before adjusting
5. If it fills consistently, you may be underpriced — test a $10-15 increase on the next launch

---

### How to Present Pricing

**Per-week framing:** "$150/month" becomes "Less than $38 per week — that's one dinner out." This reframes the number in comparison to something familiar and slightly indulgent.

**Lead with the popular tier:** Show your mid-tier option first (not the cheapest). People anchor to the first number they see — leading with your budget option sets a low anchor.

**Let them try before committing:** A free trial or first-week discount removes the biggest barrier (uncertainty). The conversion rate from trial to paid member is your most important business metric.

---

### Tiered Membership Structure

**Tier 1 — Core**
- Access to [X] classes per month
- Online booking access
- Member app / resource access
- Price: $___/month

**Tier 2 — Essential (Most Popular)**
- Unlimited group classes
- One free guest pass per month
- Member discounts on workshops (10-15%)
- Priority booking for high-demand classes
- Price: $___/month

**Tier 3 — All Access**
- Everything in Essential
- Monthly 30-minute personal training check-in
- All workshops included or heavily discounted
- First access to new programs
- Price: $___/month

*Customize tier names to reflect your studio's brand. The goal: enough difference between tiers to justify each price point, but not so much complexity that it overwhelms a new prospect.*

---

### Annual vs. Monthly Pricing Psychology

Annual memberships reduce churn, guarantee revenue, and create commitment. Price them at the equivalent of 10 months (2 months free) — e.g., if monthly is $150, annual is $1,500.

Best windows for annual sign-up pushes: January (resolution energy), September (back-to-routine), and after a successful challenge completion (members are at peak motivation and commitment).

## SECTION 4 — MANAGING ONLINE REPUTATION

### Weekly Reputation Check Routine (10 Minutes)

Every Monday morning:
1. Search your studio name on Google — check for new reviews since last Monday
2. Respond to any new reviews within 48 hours
3. Check Yelp for any new activity
4. Note any recurring themes (positive or negative) — if three reviews this month mention parking, that's a signal
5. Pass any positive staff mentions to management for team recognition

Fast response to reviews (within 24-48 hours) signals active management to Google's ranking algorithm. It also shows potential members reading your reviews that you care.

---

### Responding to Fitness-Specific Negative Reviews

**"The classes are too hard / too advanced"**
Thank you for sharing this feedback — we genuinely appreciate it. Our classes are designed to be scalable, and we always offer modifications for every fitness level. We're sorry if that wasn't communicated clearly during your visit. We'd love the opportunity to set you up with the right class for where you are right now — please reach out at [email] and we'll personally make sure your next class is a better fit.

**"The trainer was dismissive / didn't pay attention to me"**
Thank you for telling us about your experience — this isn't the standard we hold our team to, and we take it seriously. We've shared your feedback with our coaching staff. We'd like the opportunity to make this right. Please reach out to us directly at [email] and we'll personally ensure your next visit is different.

**"I didn't get results"**
We hear you, and we're genuinely sorry the results haven't come yet. Fitness outcomes depend on a combination of consistent training, recovery, sleep, and nutrition — and we want to make sure we're supporting you in all of those areas, not just what happens in class. We'd love to set up a check-in to talk about your goals and build a better plan together. Please message us at [email] — no charge, no obligation, just a conversation.

**"The studio is too crowded / I can't get a spot"**
Thank you for this — it's genuinely useful feedback and we're sorry for the frustration. We're currently looking at [adding a session / expanding our schedule / adjusting capacity limits] to address this. In the meantime, booking 48 hours in advance typically guarantees a spot in your preferred class. If you'd like help getting on a consistent schedule, reach out at [email] and we'll sort it out personally.

**"I got injured during a class"**
We're so sorry to hear this, and your wellbeing is our top priority. We'd like to speak with you directly as soon as possible — please contact us at [email/phone] so we can understand what happened and make sure you're being taken care of. We take every report like this seriously and want to do right by you.

---

### Building a Testimonial Pipeline

**Timing:** Ask at the 90-day mark (members have real results to share) and after a visible win (first pull-up, completing the challenge, reaching a goal weight).

**Method:** Google review first (highest priority for local SEO), written testimonial second (for website and inquiry emails), video third (for social media and ads).

**Guiding questions:** "What were you struggling with before you joined? What has changed since? What would you tell someone who's on the fence about joining?" These three questions produce testimonials that speak directly to new prospects' hesitations.

**Where to use them:** Website homepage, inquiry response emails, pricing guide, social media (with permission), ad creative.

## SECTION 5 — MEMBER EXPERIENCE SYSTEMS

### The First-Class Experience Checklist

1. **Door greeting by name** — Review the booking list before class. Greet new members by name when they walk in. This single action has a larger impact on retention than any other touchpoint.

2. **Brief studio tour** — Before class: where to leave bags, restrooms, water station, where to find equipment, where to stand/set up.

3. **Instructor introduction** — The instructor introduces themselves to new members before class begins. Name, a brief word on what the class involves, what modifications are available.

4. **New member flag to instructor** — Staff passes a note or verbally alerts the instructor: "Two new members today — Jamie and Sam." The instructor knows to watch for them.

5. **Form check during class** — The instructor makes at least one personal contact with each new member during the session: a correction, an encouragement, using their name.

6. **Immediate post-class check-in** — Within two minutes of class ending: "How did you feel? That was your first class! How are you doing?" Don't let them leave without a human moment.

7. **Next class recommendation before they leave** — "Based on what you did today, I think you'd love our Thursday morning strength class. Can I get you booked in before you go?"

8. **Capture email if not in the system** — Smooth, simple: "Can I grab your email so I can send you some resources for new members?"

9. **Same-day text within 4 hours** — The day-of welcome text from Template 2.

10. **Post-first-class email within 24 hours** — From Template 2.

11. **Two-week check-in** — From Template 2.

12. **30-day milestone email** — From Template 4.

---

### The 30-Day New Member Onboarding Plan

**Week 1:** First-class experience (checklist above) → same-day welcome text → day-1 email → day-3 "how to get the most out of your membership" email

**Week 2:** If they haven't returned by day 10, send the at-risk text from Template 4 → day-7 "meet the team" email if they're engaged

**Week 3:** Invite them to an upcoming challenge or event (from Template 3 or 5) → if they've attended 3+ times, a personal thank-you from the instructor

**Week 4:** 30-day milestone email from Template 4 → ask for a Google review while they're in the honeymoon phase

---

### Training Staff on Member Recognition

**Pre-class briefing:** 5-minute team huddle before each session. Manager mentions: new members expected, VIP regulars, members who haven't been in for 10+ days and are due for a check-in.

**The two-visit rule:** Learn their name by their second visit. Use the booking software notes to flag: "Learn name — second visit."

**Booking software notes:** Use them for everything — dietary restrictions, injury history, preferred class style, member's job, family details they've shared. These aren't surveillance — they're the foundation of making people feel known.

**Churn prevention signals — 5 things to watch for:**

1. Attendance drops from 3x/week to 1x/week for two consecutive weeks
2. Member stops booking in advance and only shows up on impulse
3. Member mentions a competing studio casually in conversation
4. Member asks about "pausing" without a stated reason
5. Member stops engaging with the studio on social media after previously being active

*For each signal: flag to management, trigger the appropriate at-risk template from Template 4 within 48 hours.*

## SECTION 6 — MANAGING SLOW SEASONS

### When Fitness Studios Slow Down

Most fitness studios experience predictable dips. Identifying yours allows you to plan ahead instead of reacting in a panic:

- **February:** Post-resolution drop. The members who joined in January for motivation fall off by week 4-6. Churn spikes, new acquisition slows.
- **Late June–August:** Summer vacation season. Members travel, outdoor activities replace gym time. Especially pronounced in warm-weather markets.
- **October:** Transitional month — unpredictable. Back-to-school energy has settled but holiday energy hasn't started. A soft month in many studios.
- **Late November–Christmas:** Holiday competing priorities. Evening classes suffer most. Morning classes often hold better.

---

### Counter-Programming for Slow Months

**1. Challenge Events**
A well-timed 4-6 week challenge creates artificial urgency and community energy. Members who have drifted re-engage because there's something to be part of. Time your challenges to start 2 weeks *before* a typical slow period (launch mid-January before the February drop; launch mid-August before the fall drift).

**2. Outdoor Workouts**
In summer, take a class outside. Members less likely to come to the studio will attend an outdoor pop-up event. It brings the community together, generates content, and attracts new faces.

**3. Corporate Wellness Outreach**
Summer and early fall are when HR departments plan Q4 wellness programs. Cold outreach to local businesses in August and September can bring in group contracts that provide steady revenue through the slow months.

**4. Holiday Survival Campaign**
November-December framing: "Keep moving through the holidays." Not guilt-driven ("don't eat the cookies") — realistic and low-pressure ("30 minutes three times a week keeps your energy up through the season"). Lower the bar and more members show up.

**5. Studio Anniversary Offer**
Use your studio's anniversary month as an annual event. Limited-time offer, community celebration, "come back" campaign for lapsed members. Creates a reliable calendar anchor for re-engagement.

---

### Using Slow Periods Strategically

**Content creation:** A slow Tuesday morning is the best time to shoot a month's worth of content. Empty studio, natural light, no service pressure, willing staff members to demo. A 3-hour session can produce 30+ pieces of content.

**Team training:** Schedule cross-training and certifications during slow periods. Staff get better, the studio gets better, and you're investing in the team during the time it costs you least.

**Equipment maintenance:** Deep clean, inspect, repair. Everything that gets deferred during busy months happens now.

**Menu development:** Test new programming, try new class formats, get member feedback on ideas. The slow period is when you build what drives the next busy period.

## SECTION 7 — METRICS TO TRACK MONTHLY

| Metric | How to Calculate | Target | Notes |
|--------|-----------------|--------|-------|
| Active Member Count | Members with active membership this month | Track your baseline and grow from there | Subtract paused from active total |
| New Member Sign-Ups | New memberships started this month | 10-20% of active count per month = healthy growth | Compare to prior year same month |
| Cancellations | Members who canceled this month | Under 5% of active member count | Track the reason for each cancellation |
| Monthly Churn Rate | Cancellations ÷ Active Members × 100 | Under 5% | Industry average 5-8%; under 3% is excellent |
| Avg Revenue per Member | Total Revenue ÷ Active Members | Depends on pricing — track the trend | Rising = successful upsells or upgrades |
| Class Attendance Rate | Classes attended ÷ Classes booked × 100 | Above 75% | Low rate signals scheduling or commitment barriers |
| Trial-to-Member Conversion | New members ÷ Trials started × 100 | 50%+ is strong | This is your single most important conversion metric |
| Net Promoter Score | 0-10 survey: "How likely are you to recommend us?" | Score of 50+ is excellent | Survey quarterly; Promoters (9-10) minus Detractors (0-6) |
| Social Reach | Instagram/Facebook weekly reach | Growing month over month | Reach matters more than follower count |
| Google Review Count | New reviews this month | 4+ per month minimum | Reviews older than 90 days carry less SEO weight |
| Email List Growth | New subscribers this month | Steady month-over-month growth | 20/month = 240 new contacts per year |

**How to use this tracker:** Create a Google Sheet. Copy this table. Fill in your numbers at the end of each month — it takes 15 minutes. At the end of each quarter, review the trends: what went up, what went down, what might explain the change. You're not trying to hit arbitrary benchmarks. You're trying to see your own business clearly enough to make good decisions.

## SECTION 8 — 7 SIGNS IT'S TIME TO RAISE YOUR RATES

**1. Your popular classes have a consistent waitlist.** Demand exceeds supply. That's a market signal to raise prices — either to manage demand or to fund the capacity to serve it.

**2. You haven't raised prices in 12 months or more.** Rent, utilities, software subscriptions, and instructor pay have all increased. If your rates haven't moved, your margins have quietly shrunk.

**3. Good instructors are leaving for competitors who pay more.** Instructor quality is your product. If you can't afford to retain top coaches, your rates are too low.

**4. Your most loyal long-term members have never once mentioned price.** The members who love you aren't choosing you for the deal. They're choosing you for the coaching, the community, and the results. A 10-15% increase won't drive them away.

**5. Competitors with similar or lower quality are priced higher than you.** Underpricing signals lower quality, even when your product is excellent. Guests perceive price as a proxy for value — being the cheapest option in your market works against you.

**6. Your membership capacity is straining your ability to deliver a quality experience.** More members than your schedule can comfortably hold means a worse experience for everyone. Either add capacity (new classes, staff) or raise rates to manage demand. Both are valid.

**7. You're burning out trying to maintain high volume at insufficient margins.** Running a full schedule at rates that don't sustain the business (and you) is not a long-term strategy. Price your service to sustain your quality, your team, and your own energy.

---

### How to Raise Rates Without Losing Loyal Members

Announce increases 30-60 days in advance. Give long-term members early notice — email them before you post publicly. Frame the increase around what you're investing in: "We're raising rates to bring on an additional instructor and upgrade our equipment."

Offer a rate-lock for members who pre-pay annually — this rewards loyalty and converts monthly members to annual at the same time.

**Language that works:**
"We want to be transparent with you. Effective [date], our membership rates will increase to [new rate]. We've held our rates steady for [X time] while continuing to invest in our team and programming. This increase allows us to keep delivering the studio experience you know and expect."

**Language that doesn't work:**
"We're so sorry to have to do this..." — Never apologize for pricing your service correctly. Apologetic language plants doubt. Confident language builds trust.
