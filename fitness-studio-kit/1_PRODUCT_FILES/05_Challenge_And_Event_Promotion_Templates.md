# Challenge, Workshop & Event Promotion Templates

## How to Use These Templates
Events and challenges do three things at once: they drive revenue, build community, and create content. A well-run 30-day challenge generates 30+ posts, dozens of testimonials, and a wave of new members who joined because they saw the community participation. These templates handle the promotion — you handle the delivery.

Layer your promotions: announce early, remind mid-way through registration, post daily during the event, celebrate the completion publicly. The sequence below is built to do exactly that.

### Member Spotlight / Transformation Story Post

This is a template structure rather than fixed filler text, because real member stories vary and the specific details are what make them powerful. Fill in each section with what your member actually told you.

---

OPENING — Who they are and when they started:
Write 1–2 sentences that introduce them by first name and give a sense of where they were when they walked in. Keep it general enough to protect privacy unless they've explicitly said they're comfortable with details. Example framing: "Meet [Name]. [He/She/They] first came to us about [timeframe] ago, at a point when [brief context about their starting place — no medical specifics unless they've given clear consent to share]."

THE TURNING POINT — What brought them in or made them commit:
Write 1–3 sentences about the moment or decision that got them through the door or kept them coming back when it would have been easier to stop. This is the emotional core of the story. Quote them directly if you have a quote — it will always be more powerful than your paraphrase. Example framing: "What kept [him/her/them] coming back, [he/she/they] told us, was [quote or paraphrase of their reason]."

THE PROGRESS — What they've achieved:
Be as specific as the member has given you permission to be. Concrete details — a weight, a distance, a lift, a habit streak, a milestone — are far more compelling than vague language. Examples: first unassisted pull-up, ran a 5K after years of not running, lost 30 lbs over 8 months, showed up 4 days a week for a full year, deadlifts their body weight, just feels better in their body than they have in a decade. Write 2–4 sentences.

THE CLOSE — Quote or community line:
End with either a direct quote from them (always the strongest option) or a sentence that frames what they mean to your community. Keep it honest and warm, not hyperbolic.

Example close using a quote: "[Name] put it best when [he/she/they] said: '[direct quote — something real, specific, and in their voice.]'"

Example close without a quote: "We are so proud of everything [Name] has built here — and we're grateful every single day that [he/she/they] walked through that door."

---

Note: Always get written permission from the member before posting their name and story. Let them read the draft before it goes live. Give them the option to use only their first name or initials. Never share weight, measurements, medical history, or other sensitive details without explicit consent for each specific piece of information. A simple "Hey, I'd love to feature your story — can I send you what I'm planning to post so you can approve it?" is all you need to ask.
