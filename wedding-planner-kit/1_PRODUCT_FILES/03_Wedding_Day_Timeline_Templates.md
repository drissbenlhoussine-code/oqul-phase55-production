# WEDDING DAY TIMELINE TEMPLATES

**Template 3 of 9 | Wedding Planner Business Kit**
*Professional Day-Of Timeline Templates for Wedding Coordinators and Planners*

---

## HOW TO USE THESE TEMPLATES

These timeline templates are your working foundation — not a rigid script. Every wedding is different, and the variables that affect timing are numerous: the distance between venues, the size of the bridal party, whether the couple tends to run late, how efficient the catering staff is, and a dozen other factors specific to each event.

Start with the framework that most closely matches your wedding format, then layer in the specific details: exact venue addresses and travel distances, vendor contracted arrival times, the number of people in hair and makeup, how many family formal combinations are planned, and any unique programming the couple has requested. Work backward from your ceremony time to build the getting-ready schedule, and work forward from ceremony end to build the reception flow.

Share a copy with every vendor two weeks in advance, and carry a printed copy on the wedding day. Do not rely solely on your phone.

**The single most important principle in any wedding day timeline: build in buffers at every transition point, and do not apologize for them.** A wedding day is not a factory floor where every task completes in exactly the allotted time. Hair runs fifteen minutes long because a bridesmaid arrived late. The florist needs an extra twenty minutes because the arch is more complex than anticipated. The photographer wants one more shot in the golden light. These are not failures — they are the nature of a day with this many moving parts.

A rule of thumb: add ten minutes to every task you think will take thirty minutes, add fifteen minutes to every vendor transition, and add a thirty-minute buffer before ceremony start. When everything runs smoothly, those buffers disappear silently. When something goes wrong, those buffers are why the couple never knows about it.

## SECTION 1: OUTDOOR CEREMONY TIMELINE (4 HOURS)

### Outdoor Ceremony — Vendor Arrival Through Reception Start

**Format:** Outdoor ceremony (garden, vineyard, estate, or park setting) followed by cocktail hour. 4-hour window covering vendor arrival through the close of cocktail hour and start of reception.
**Sample Ceremony Start Time:** 4:00 PM
**Bridal Party Size:** 6 people
**Adjust all times proportionally if your ceremony starts earlier or later.**

---

**12:00 PM — Coordinator Arrives, Venue Walkthrough Begins**
- Coordinator arrives at least 30 minutes before any vendor is scheduled
- Conduct full walkthrough of ceremony space, cocktail hour area, and reception area
- Confirm ceremony space is cleared and accessible for vendor load-in
- Verify restroom access, parking logistics, and any site-specific restrictions
- Locate all electrical outlets; confirm power availability for DJ and sound equipment
- Identify the on-site venue contact; exchange cell phone numbers immediately
- Note anything that needs to be corrected before guests arrive

*Coordinator tip: Walk the entire property before touching anything. You need to know where every vendor is going before they ask you. If there are issues — a muddy path, a broken light fixture, a missing table — you have time to address them now.*

---

**12:30 PM — Floral Delivery**
- Florist arrives with all ceremony florals: arch arrangements, aisle markers, petal cones, programs, and any reserved seating flowers
- Coordinator walks with the florist to confirm placement of all pieces
- Verify personal flowers are separated and clearly labeled: bridal bouquet, bridesmaid bouquets, boutonnieres, corsages, flower girl baskets
- Personal flowers go into a floral cooler or cool shaded area until needed — do not leave them in direct sun
- Confirm with florist: what time will they depart, and who do you call if a piece needs adjustment before the ceremony?

*Coordinator tip: Count every piece against the order before the florist leaves. A missing boutonniere discovered at 3:45 PM when everyone is already dressed is a crisis. A missing boutonniere discovered at 1:00 PM is a phone call.*

---

**1:00 PM — Ceremony Sound and AV Setup**
- DJ or band sound technician arrives and begins setup in the ceremony area
- Run a full sound check: officiant microphone, music playback, and any live instrument amplification
- Confirm processional song list and the exact cue order with the coordinator
- Confirm recessional song
- Test wireless lapel mic if the officiant is using one; confirm battery is fresh
- Confirm backup plan if any equipment fails (second speaker, backup device)

*Coordinator tip: An outdoor ceremony sound check must happen at approximately the same time of day as the ceremony itself — ambient noise, wind, and temperature all affect microphone performance. A sound check at noon for a 4:00 PM ceremony may not reflect afternoon wind conditions.*

---

**1:30 PM — Rental Company Final Delivery**
- Chair rentals, arch rentals, or any final furniture pieces arrive and are placed
- Coordinator verifies chair count against the confirmed guest count — always check for the exact number, not an approximation
- Confirm aisle width is adequate for the processional (minimum 5 feet for full dress with train)
- Confirm arch placement is secured; outdoor arches must be weighted or staked to prevent tipping in wind
- Any tent sidewalls or weather-related additions confirmed in place

*Coordinator tip: If the forecast has changed in the last 48 hours, this is the moment to discuss weather contingency with the venue. Know your backup plan before guests arrive.*

---

**2:00 PM — Photographer Arrives at Bridal Suite**
- Photographer begins detail shots: rings, shoes, bouquet, invitation suite, dress hanging shot, accessories
- These shots require no participation from the bridal party — they can be captured while hair and makeup are still finishing
- Coordinator briefs photographer on the full timeline, first look location (if applicable), and any must-have shot requests from the couple
- Confirm photographer has the family formal shot list and has reviewed the processional order

*Coordinator tip: Good photographers do not need to be directed shot-by-shot, but they do need your timeline and location information. Give them everything they need at the start and let them work. Step in only if they are running behind.*

---

**2:15 PM — Hair and Makeup Completion Target**
- All bridesmaids should be fully done with hair and makeup by this time
- Bride is beginning or completing final touch-ups
- Getting-into-the-dress process begins: coordinator or maid of honor assists
- Bustling instructions confirmed with the designated person (often the maid of honor) if the dress has a bustle — walk through the steps now, not later

*Coordinator tip: If hair and makeup are running more than 15 minutes behind at this point, begin managing downstream items: alert the photographer, adjust the first look time if possible, and prepare to compress the wedding party portrait schedule.*

---

**2:30 PM — Bride into Dress, Detail Portraits**
- Bride is fully dressed and ready
- Photographer captures bride stepping into gown, lacing up shoes, receiving bouquet
- Bridesmaids dressed and holding bouquets for getting-ready group shots
- Father of the bride or parent first look if planned — a beautiful moment that takes only 5–8 minutes but means everything

*Coordinator tip: Confirm with the makeup artist that they will stay nearby for a touch-up after the dress goes on. Getting into the dress can smudge makeup, loosen hair, and cause a stress response that shows on the face — a 90-second touch-up before portraits makes a significant difference.*

---

**2:45 PM — First Look (If Planned)**
- Transportation to first look location or move to the designated spot on the venue property
- Coordinator positions both partners and steps away — give them genuine privacy for the moment
- Photographer captures the first look and immediate candid reactions
- Allow 15–20 minutes for the first look and the first spontaneous couple portraits that follow
- If there is no first look, groomsmen arrive at the ceremony site and wedding party portraits begin now

*Coordinator tip: The first look is one of the highest-value moments of the day for both the couple and the photographs. Do not rush it and do not allow any other activity to interrupt it. Phones away, guests away, family away.*

---

**3:00 PM — Wedding Party Portraits**
- Full bridal party portraits: all together, bride with bridesmaids, groom with groomsmen, and any subgroups requested
- Coordinator keeps the bridal party on location and manages the energy — people need direction and encouragement, not just positioning
- Photographer works through the shot list efficiently; coordinator should have a copy of the must-have list and can help call people in and out of frame
- Target: complete all wedding party portraits by 3:25 PM

*Coordinator tip: The energy of the bridal party during portraits sets the tone for every image. Keep people laughing, keep the mood light, and move quickly between groupings. Slow transitions kill the energy.*

---

**3:15 PM — Groomsmen Confirmed at Ceremony Site**
- Groomsmen button up, jackets on, and arrive at the ceremony area
- Ushers receive final briefing on seating protocol: which side for which family, whether reserved rows are roped off, and how to escort guests (offering an arm to female guests is traditional)
- Boutonniere check: all groomsmen properly pinned, flowers facing up and outward

*Coordinator tip: Check the groom's boutonniere last, right before he takes his position. It is the one most likely to be handled, touched, or wilted from a long morning.*

---

**3:20 PM — Family Formals**
- Immediately after wedding party photos, move into family formals
- Coordinator has pre-contacted family members and told them where to be and at what time
- Work through the list in a specific order: largest groups first (all immediate family together), then smaller configurations
- Recommended order: full combined family photo → bride's family large group → groom's family large group → bride with parents → groom with parents → individual parent combinations → grandparents if requested
- Target: complete all family formals by 3:45 PM

*Coordinator tip: Your family formal shot list should be finalized with the photographer at least one week before the wedding. Knowing exactly how many combinations are planned lets you allocate time precisely. 8–10 combinations in 25 minutes is achievable. 20 combinations in 25 minutes is not.*

---

**3:45 PM — Couple Released to Private Holding Area**
- Couple moves to a private holding space — they should not be seen by arriving guests before the processional
- Final touch-up by makeup artist: lip color refreshed, hair secured
- Coordinator final check: bridal bouquet ready, groom's boutonniere intact, rings confirmed with best man, officiant in position
- Groomsmen and groom move to staging position near the ceremony entry point

*Coordinator tip: Use this window to do your own final sweep — ceremony chairs, sound check one more time, programs distributed, aisle clear. This is your last opportunity to fix anything.*

---

**3:30 PM — Guest Arrival Begins**
- Ushers in position to greet and escort guests as they arrive
- Ceremony prelude music begins softly
- Programs distributed at the entrance or placed on chairs
- Signage in place directing guests to the ceremony space from parking
- Parking attendants in position if contracted

---

**3:50 PM — Processional Staging**
- All members of the processional gathered in the staging area
- Coordinator confirms processional order with all participants one final time
- Children (flower girl, ring bearer) given their final briefing and any props
- Remind flower girl: walk slowly, scatter petals in a wide arc, stop at the end of the aisle and move to the side

---

**4:00 PM — Ceremony Begins (Hard Start)**
- Officiant takes position at the altar
- Coordinator gives the DJ or musician the signal to transition from prelude to processional music
- Seating of grandparents if planned (escorted by ushers)
- Seating of groom's mother
- Seating of bride's mother — her seating signals that the ceremony is beginning
- Groomsmen process in (if walking down the aisle)
- Bridesmaids process in
- Flower girl and ring bearer
- Coordinator signals DJ to switch to bride's entrance song
- All guests rise; bride and escort enter

*Coordinator tip: Do not allow the ceremony to start late without a specific reason. A ceremony delayed 10 minutes compresses cocktail hour, which compresses the room flip, which compresses dinner service. One delay cascades into six.*

---

**4:05 PM — Ceremony in Progress (20–30 Minutes)**
- Officiant leads the ceremony per the approved script
- Coordinator steps away once the processional is complete
- Use this time: confirm cocktail hour setup is complete, confirm caterer is ready for service, check in on room flip status if applicable
- Time the ceremony from your staging area — note when it began

*Coordinator tip: If the couple has young children as ring bearers or flower girls, position a parent near the end of the aisle during the ceremony so that the child can exit quietly if they become restless. Coordinate this placement before the ceremony begins.*

---

**4:30 PM — Recessional**
- DJ or musician receives the coordinator's cue for recessional music — this is your cue, not automatic
- Couple exits first on recessional music
- Wedding party follows in pairs
- Ushers dismiss guests row by row as soon as the wedding party has exited
- Coordinator at the exit point to direct guests immediately toward the cocktail hour space

*Coordinator tip: The transition from ceremony to cocktail hour is the most vulnerable moment for guest confusion. Have ushers and signage in place before the recessional ends so that every guest has a clear path to the next location.*

---

**4:35 PM — Cocktail Hour Begins**
- Guests directed to cocktail hour space
- Passed appetizers begin service within 5 minutes of cocktail hour opening — hungry guests who are standing around without food or drinks become restless quickly
- Bar opens
- Couple is pulled aside by the photographer for golden hour couple portraits while guests enjoy the cocktail hour — this is optimal use of that time for both the couple and the photography
- Coordinator confirms cocktail hour is fully operational, then checks in with reception setup

*Coordinator tip: The couple missing the first 30–45 minutes of cocktail hour is completely normal and should be explained to them in advance. They will arrive at the reception with stunning couple portraits and guests who are relaxed, fed, and happy to see them.*

## SECTION 2: TRADITIONAL CHURCH CEREMONY TIMELINE (5–6 HOURS)

### Church Ceremony — Bridal Party Arrival Through Reception Dinner

**Format:** Traditional church ceremony with a separate reception venue. 5–6 hour window from morning preparations through the start of reception dinner.
**Sample Ceremony Start Time:** 2:00 PM
**Reception Dinner Start Time:** 7:30 PM

**Before you begin:** Church ceremonies require additional coordination with the church's own coordinator or designated contact. Confirm all church-specific policies well in advance: candle usage, aisle runner, confetti or petal restrictions outside the church, flash photography restrictions during the ceremony, whether a sound check with the church's system is permitted, whether there is a bridal room, and whether there is a separate entrance for the groom's party.

---

**8:00 AM — Church Contact Confirmation Call**
- Coordinator places a confirmation call to the church coordinator or designated contact
- Confirm: doors open time for vendor setup, sacristy access for groom's preparations, bridal room availability and exact location, and any last-minute restrictions or changes
- Confirm the church sound system operator will be present and their arrival time
- Confirm who will be managing the church's own schedule throughout the morning

---

**9:00 AM — Church Setup Access Opens**
- Florist arrives and begins decorating: pew markers, altar arrangements, unity candle table setup if applicable, and any aisle runner
- Coordinator on-site to receive the florist and confirm placement per the approved floor plan
- Confirm kneeler placement if the couple will be kneeling during prayer
- Confirm program placement: on seats, in the narthex, or handed by ushers
- Coordinate with church contact on any restrictions for floral placement near the altar

*Coordinator tip: The church's own coordinator or sexton may have strong opinions about where florals can and cannot be placed. Build the relationship early in the morning; do not arrive as an adversary.*

---

**9:30 AM — Coordinator Check on Getting-Ready Location**
- Call or text the maid of honor or lead bridesmaid for a status update on getting-ready progress
- Confirm hair and makeup are on schedule
- Confirm breakfast or food is available — energy management is crucial through a long day
- Identify any emerging issues now while there is still time to address them

---

**10:00 AM — Groom's Party Arrives at Church**
- Groom and groomsmen arrive at the church and access the sacristy or designated preparation room
- Boutonniere pinning by coordinator or best man
- Final wardrobe check: ties straight, jacket buttons, pocket squares, shoes polished
- Coordinator confirms the groom has the rings — or confirms that the best man has them
- Marriage license review: confirm it is signed and stored correctly if it has already been completed, or confirm the plan for signing it today
- Photographer or second shooter begins documentation of the groom's preparations

*Coordinator tip: Keep the groom's room calm. This is often overlooked — everyone focuses on the bridal suite — but the groom's experience of the morning matters. Have water, snacks, and a relaxed atmosphere in the sacristy.*

---

**10:30 AM — Photographer Coverage Begins**
- Primary photographer at the getting-ready location with the bridal party
- Second shooter at the church covering the groom's preparations
- If there is only one photographer, they are with the bridal party; coordinate with a reliable groomsman to capture candid groom prep shots on a personal camera or phone

---

**11:00 AM — Hair and Makeup: Final Hour**
- All bridesmaids should be in the final stages of hair or makeup
- Bride in the makeup chair for final application
- Confirm food is available and has been eaten — hunger causes headaches by 3:00 PM
- Coordinator confirms transportation timeline to the church and builds in a 15-minute buffer

---

**12:00 PM — Bride into Dress**
- All bridesmaids fully ready and dressed
- Bride steps into gown with assistance from the maid of honor and coordinator
- Photographer captures the entire process
- Veil placement and final bouquet delivery from floral cooler
- Accessories applied: earrings, necklace, bracelet
- Something old, new, borrowed, blue — confirm each item is present if this is important to the couple

*Coordinator tip: The moment the bride steps into her dress is when the emotion of the day typically surfaces for the first time. Give the room space. Do not rush the next 15 minutes. These are photographs and memories the couple will have for a lifetime.*

---

**12:15 PM — Pre-Church Family Formals**
- If the first look is not planned, and if family formals at the church will be limited due to receiving line or photo restrictions, complete family portraits now at the getting-ready location
- If a first look IS planned, this is typically the best time — complete the first look, then transition immediately into couple portraits and family formals while everyone is fresh
- Target: all portraits at the getting-ready location complete by 12:40 PM

---

**12:20 PM — Pre-Ceremony Lunch or Snack**
- If the timeline permits, a brief seated snack or light lunch between portraits and departure is highly valuable
- The ceremony is at 2:00 PM; the next opportunity for the couple to eat may not be until 7:30 PM
- Even 15 minutes with a plate of food makes a significant difference in energy and mood

---

**12:45 PM — Transportation Departs for Church**
- Coordinator confirms all members of the bridal party and immediate family are loaded into transportation
- Final headcount before vehicles depart
- Dress and veil secured in the vehicle — one designated person holds the bridal bouquet
- Wedding dress is gathered carefully so that the train does not drag on the vehicle floor; have a garment bag or spare fabric to protect it during transit
- Estimated travel time to church confirmed; account for traffic

*Coordinator tip: Text the church contact that you are departing. They should be expecting you at 1:00 PM. If you are running behind, call now — not when you arrive late.*

---

**1:00 PM — Bridal Party Arrives at Church**
- Bridal party enters through the designated door (not through the front — guests should not see the bride before the processional)
- Coordinator confirms all bridal party members have arrived safely
- Bride is taken to the bridal room; groomsmen proceed to the sacristy
- Church coordinator or head usher briefed on final seating assignments and any changes

---

**1:00 PM — Parking and Guest Arrival Management**
- Church parking can be a significant logistical challenge, especially at churches in urban areas or on busy streets
- Confirm overflow parking location in advance and place directional signage if needed
- Ushers stationed outside to direct guests and offer escort to seats
- Reserved pews roped off or marked with flowers, ribbon, or a reserved sign
- Remind ushers: do not allow guests to sit in the reserved rows regardless of how insistently the guest asserts their own seating preference

---

**1:15 PM — Prelude Music Begins**
- Church organist, musician, or sound system operator begins prelude music
- Guests are seated as they arrive
- Coordinator inside managing seating flow and communicating with the bridal party in the holding room
- Confirm with officiant: they are in position and ready; script is accessible

---

**1:30 PM — Processional Staging in Narthex**
- All members of the processional are gathered in the church narthex or designated entry hallway
- Coordinator confirms processional order with all participants
- Coordinate any church-specific cues with the church coordinator or sound operator
- Flower girl and ring bearer given their final instructions, props, and encouragement
- Bridesmaids confirm bouquet grip and posture

---

**1:45 PM — Seating of Honored Guests**
- Grandparents escorted to seats by ushers
- Special guests with reserved seating escorted
- Groom's mother escorted by a designated usher (traditionally accompanied by her husband)
- Bride's mother is escorted last — her seating signals the ceremony is about to begin
- Sanctuary or chapel doors are closed after the bride's mother is seated

*Coordinator tip: Confirm in advance exactly who will be escorted and in what order. If there are divorced parents, step-parents, or complex family dynamics, this seating sequence can be a source of conflict. Get the instructions directly from the couple and confirm them in writing.*

---

**1:55 PM — Groom and Officiant in Position**
- Groom and best man enter from the side door and take their position at the altar
- Officiant confirmed in position
- Coordinator gives the church musician or sound operator the signal: processional music begins
- All participants in the narthex confirm readiness with the coordinator

---

**2:00 PM — Ceremony Begins**
- Groomsmen process in (if walking down the aisle)
- Bridesmaids process in, one at a time or in pairs
- Flower girl and ring bearer
- All guests rise for the bride's entrance
- Bride and escort process down the aisle

---

**2:05 PM — Ceremony in Progress (45–75 Minutes)**
- Traditional church ceremony runs 45–75 minutes depending on the denomination, number of readings, and whether Communion is included
- Coordinator steps away from the sanctuary once the processional is complete
- Use this time: confirm the reception venue is on schedule, confirm guest transportation (shuttle or valet) is in place, confirm cocktail hour setup is complete
- Note the ceremony start time — if it runs longer than expected, adjust the reception timeline accordingly and communicate this to the venue
- Flash photography is restricted in many churches — confirm this with the photographer well in advance, not on the day-of

*Coordinator tip: A 75-minute ceremony that was planned as a 45-minute ceremony eats 30 minutes of portrait time. Have a compressed portrait plan ready in case the ceremony runs long.*

---

**3:00 PM (Estimated) — Recessional**
- Couple exits together down the aisle on recessional music
- Wedding party follows in pairs
- Guests exit by rows — ushers assist and guide the flow
- Confetti, bubbles, or flower petals outside the church if permitted — confirm the policy well in advance; distribute items to a designated person before the ceremony so they are ready

---

**3:10 PM — Receiving Line (If Applicable)**
- Couple greets guests outside the church or in the narthex if a receiving line is planned
- Allot 20–30 minutes for a receiving line depending on guest count (approximately 1 minute per guest)
- If no receiving line: couple and family move to the portrait area immediately
- Coordinator manages guest flow through the receiving line and keeps it moving

*Coordinator tip: A receiving line at the church is beautiful but takes significant time. For a guest count over 100, a receiving line at the cocktail hour (where guests are already comfortable and have drinks in hand) is often a more relaxed alternative.*

---

**3:15 PM — Post-Ceremony Portraits**
- Church exterior portraits if lighting is favorable and the church permits photography on the grounds
- Family formals — church steps are often the perfect backdrop for these; coordinate with the photographer in advance on preferred locations
- Couple portraits at the church exterior or at a nearby location arranged in advance
- Wedding party portraits at church or en route to the reception venue
- Target: depart church by 4:00 PM for reception venue

*Coordinator tip: Do not use church interior for portraits after guests have been dismissed unless you have explicit permission from the church. Some churches close the sanctuary immediately after the ceremony. Confirm this in advance.*

---

**4:00 PM — Transportation to Reception Venue**
- Couple and wedding party depart for the reception venue
- Coordinator confirms all personal items are loaded: bouquets stored properly, personal bags, any items signed at the church (marriage license)
- Estimated travel time confirmed; communicate ETA to reception venue coordinator
- Reception venue should be contacted now if the couple will be more than 10 minutes off schedule

---

**4:15 PM — Arrival at Reception Venue**
- Couple and wedding party arrive at the reception venue
- If guests have already arrived, couple and wedding party use the service entrance or a private entry to avoid being seen before the grand entrance
- Coordinator confirms with the reception venue: all setup complete, cocktail hour in progress, DJ ready

---

**5:00 PM — Cocktail Hour Midpoint Check**
- Guests have been enjoying cocktail hour for approximately 45–60 minutes
- Coordinator confirms passed appetizers have been consistent and guests are satisfied
- Confirm with DJ: ready for the grand entrance when the coordinator gives the signal
- Begin staging the wedding party in entrance order

---

**5:30 PM — Grand Entrance**
- DJ announces wedding party members individually or in pairs
- Couple announced last to their chosen entrance song
- First dance follows immediately

---

**5:35 PM — First Dance and Parent Dances**
- Couple's first dance (3–4 minutes)
- Father-daughter dance immediately following
- Mother-son dance immediately following
- Transition directly into welcome toast and dinner service

---

**5:55 PM — Toasts**
- Welcome toast by best man and/or maid of honor
- Maximum 2–3 toasts before dinner service; additional toasts can be held between courses

---

**6:10 PM — Dinner Service Begins**
- Catering begins dinner service
- DJ transitions to dinner background music

---

**7:30 PM — Open Dancing Begins**
- Cake cutting at 7:20 PM
- DJ transitions to dance floor music
- Full evening of dancing and celebration

## SECTION 3: INDOOR VENUE — CEREMONY & RECEPTION (8 HOURS)

### Full Wedding Day — Getting Ready Through Reception End

**Format:** Ceremony and reception at a single indoor venue. The most common full-day wedding format.
**Sample Event Window:** Noon venue access through midnight
**Sample Ceremony Start Time:** 5:00 PM
**Sample Reception End Time:** Midnight

---

**12:00 PM — Venue Access, Vendor Load-In Begins**
- Coordinator arrives first and confirms load-in door access with venue staff
- Venue staff completes any room preparation or flips from daytime events (confirm this is fully finished before florals arrive)
- Coordinator walks both the ceremony and reception spaces with the floor plan in hand, noting anything that needs to be addressed
- Confirm all rented items from the venue are in place: tables in correct positions, dance floor installed, bar structure in place

*Coordinator tip: Your first 30 minutes at the venue should be a silent walkthrough with your floor plan and a pen. Mark off every element you see in place. Circle everything that is missing, wrong, or unclear. Fix it before vendors arrive.*

---

**12:30 PM — Floral Load-In**
- Florist arrives with all florals: ceremony arrangements, altar pieces, aisle decor, centerpieces, personal flowers, welcome table arrangement, escort card table arrangement
- Coordinator walks with the lead florist through the ceremony space first, then the reception space
- Confirm centerpiece placement matches the approved seating chart layout
- Ceremony florals placed: altar arrangements, aisle decor, candle accents
- Personal flowers separated, labeled, and placed in a cool area

*Coordinator tip: Centerpiece placement must match the seating chart exactly. A table numbered 8 that is sitting where table 12 should be will cause confusion at dinner. Confirm table numbers against the floor plan before the florist begins placing centerpieces.*

---

**1:00 PM — Catering Setup and Rental Delivery**
- Catering team begins linens, place settings, and table setup in the reception room
- Rental company delivers any additional items: specialty glassware, chargers, lounge furniture, cake table linens
- Coordinator verifies setup against the approved floor plan
- Confirm table numbers are in place and match the seating chart
- Confirm bar setup is complete and fully stocked
- Confirm the wedding cake delivery window with the bakery — most cakes arrive 2–3 hours before the reception and need a dedicated table with a clean surface

---

**1:30 PM — DJ or Band Load-In**
- DJ or band arrives for setup and sound check in the reception room
- Coordinate timing so that sound check happens while the ceremony space is still being set up — sound bleeds between rooms if the venue shares walls
- Confirm with DJ: full reception timeline reviewed, all special song requests loaded, name pronunciations for grand entrance confirmed and written phonetically
- Confirm DJ understands the coordinator is the primary communication point for all cues — DJ takes signals from the coordinator, not from the couple directly

*Coordinator tip: Sit down with the DJ for 10 minutes at load-in. Go through every programmatic moment: grand entrance order, first dance song, parent dance songs, cake cutting song, last dance song, exit song. Do this now so there are no questions later.*

---

**2:00 PM — Photographer Arrives at Getting-Ready Suite**
- Photographer begins detail shots: rings, shoes, bouquet (if already delivered to suite), invitation suite, dress hanging shot, accessories
- These shots require no participation from the bridal party and can be completed while hair and makeup are wrapping up
- Coordinator briefs photographer on the full timeline, first look location and logistics, and family formal shot list

---

**2:00 PM — Officiant Arrives for Ceremony Walkthrough**
- Officiant arrives at the ceremony space
- Confirm sound system and microphone setup; test the mic
- Walk through the ceremony sequence: where does the officiant enter, where do they stand, what are the cues
- Confirm any unity ceremony elements are in place and accessible: candles with lighters, sand vase set, handfasting cord, or other elements
- Confirm the officiant has the signed marriage license or knows where it will be at the time of the ceremony

---

**2:45 PM — Bride into Dress (If First Look at 3:00 PM)**
- Bride steps into gown
- Coordinator or maid of honor assists with buttons, lacing, or zipper
- Makeup artist does a final touch-up after dress is on
- Veil secured; accessories on

---

**3:00 PM — First Look (Optional)**
- Couple meets at the designated first look location within the venue or in a private outdoor space
- Coordinator positions both partners, ensures privacy, and steps back
- Photographer captures the moment and the candid reactions
- Allow 15–20 minutes for first look and immediate couple portraits
- If no first look: skip to wedding party portraits at this time

*Coordinator tip: If the first look is happening indoors at the venue, select a location with good natural light and a clean background — a window in a neutral hallway, a courtyard, or a private lounge area. Scout this location on your venue walkthrough at noon.*

---

**3:20 PM — Couple Portraits Continue**
- Post-first-look couple portraits in selected locations throughout or near the venue
- Aim for 3–4 distinct portrait locations for visual variety
- Target: 20–25 minutes of couple portrait time before transitioning to wedding party

---

**3:45 PM — Wedding Party Portraits**
- Full bridal party assembled in portrait location
- Photographer works through: full wedding party group, bridesmaids alone with bride, groomsmen alone with groom, bride and maid of honor, groom and best man, any other requested subgroups
- Coordinator manages energy and keeps bridal party engaged
- Target: complete all wedding party portraits by 4:15 PM

---

**4:00 PM — Family Formals**
- Family members called to portrait area by coordinator
- Work through the shot list from largest group to smallest
- Standard order: all immediate family together → bride's family → groom's family → bride with parents → groom with parents → individual parent pairings → grandparents → any other requested combinations
- Target: all family formals complete by 4:25 PM
- Release family members to the guest arrival area

*Coordinator tip: Assign a bridesmaid or trusted family member to help you call names and wrangle people for family formals. You cannot call names and manage the photographer and keep track of the shot list simultaneously without help.*

---

**4:15 PM — Final Setup Verification (Both Rooms)**
- Coordinator does a complete final walkthrough of the ceremony room and the reception room
- **Ceremony room:** chairs straight and uniform, programs on seats or at entrance, aisle clear and correct width, sound confirmed with DJ, all ceremony props in place (unity elements, rings cushion, etc.), lighting confirmed
- **Reception room:** all place settings complete, centerpieces in final position, seating chart posted and readable, escort cards arranged alphabetically or by table, dance floor clear, bar stocked and ready
- **Lighting check:** confirm ceremony lighting setting and reception lighting ambiance setting with venue AV staff
- **Cocktail hour space:** high-top tables set, passed appetizer trays ready in the kitchen, cocktail hour florals complete
- **Bathrooms:** clean, stocked; if the couple has provided amenity baskets, confirm they are in place

*Coordinator tip: This walkthrough should take 20 minutes and be done with a printed checklist in your hand. If something is wrong, you have 15 minutes to fix it before guests arrive. This is your last chance.*

---

**4:30 PM — Guest Arrival Begins**
- Ushers greet guests at the venue entrance
- Ceremony space is open; cocktail hour space is not yet open
- Prelude music begins in the ceremony space
- Coordinator stationed at the entrance until 4:50 PM, then moves to bridal party staging area

---

**4:50 PM — Bridal Party Staged for Processional**
- All processional participants gathered in the staging area
- Coordinator confirms the exact processional order with everyone
- Final checks: bouquets in hand, boutonnieres intact, veil secure, dress arranged
- Flower girl and ring bearer given their final instructions

---

**4:58 PM — Seating of Honored Guests Complete**
- Grandparents and parents seated
- Groom's mother escorted and seated
- Bride's mother seated last — she is the final guest seated before the ceremony begins

---

**5:00 PM — Ceremony Begins**
- Coordinator gives DJ the signal to begin processional music
- Processional proceeds in order
- Ceremony led by officiant per the approved script
- Estimated ceremony length: 25–35 minutes for a non-religious ceremony with personal vows

*Coordinator tip: Once the processional is complete and the ceremony is in progress, step out quietly and confirm with the venue staff that the room flip is ready to begin the moment the recessional music plays. If there is no room flip, confirm that cocktail hour service is fully staged and ready to open.*

---

**5:30 PM — Ceremony Ends, Recessional**
- Coordinator cues DJ for recessional music
- Couple exits on recessional music
- Wedding party follows in pairs
- Coordinator opens the cocktail hour space as guests exit the ceremony room
- Cocktail hour appetizers begin service within 3 minutes
- Bar opens immediately

---

**5:35 PM — Cocktail Hour + Room Flip (If Applicable)**
- Guests transition to cocktail hour in a separate room or lobby
- If the ceremony and reception are in the same space: venue staff begins the room flip now
- Couple and wedding party take advantage of this time for golden hour portraits outdoors
- Coordinator manages the room flip timeline — this is often the most logistically sensitive moment of the entire day
- Target: room flip complete by 6:15 PM if cocktail hour ends at 6:30 PM
- Check in on flip progress at T-30, T-15, and T-5 minutes

*Coordinator tip: A room flip typically takes 45–60 minutes for a team of 4–6 venue staff. Know your venue's flip time and plan accordingly. If the flip cannot be completed in time, you will need to extend the cocktail hour — which means communicating to the catering team that passed appetizers need to continue. Build this communication into your plan.*

---

**6:30 PM — Guests Called to Reception Room**
- DJ makes an announcement inviting guests to make their way to the reception room and find their seats
- Background music plays as guests enter and settle
- Wedding party and couple are staged in the entrance hallway, ready for the grand entrance
- Coordinator confirms DJ has the full name list and pronunciations one final time
- Confirm all wedding party members are present and in the correct order

---

**6:40 PM — Grand Entrance**
- DJ announces wedding party members individually or in pairs — build the energy with each announcement
- Couple announced last with their chosen entrance song
- First dance follows immediately after the couple enters — the energy is at its absolute peak right now, do not lose it by having the couple stand at a sweetheart table while guests are seated

*Coordinator tip: Grand entrance energy is contagious when done well. Brief the wedding party in advance: walk confidently, take your time, acknowledge the crowd. Give each couple 10 full seconds before the next pair enters.*

---

**6:45 PM — First Dance**
- Couple's first dance (3–4 minutes)
- Transition immediately into parent dances if planned
- Father-daughter dance (3–4 minutes)
- Mother-son dance (3–4 minutes)
- A group parent dance (both sets of parents and couple together) is a lovely option if time is limited
- Back-to-back parent dances are strongly recommended before dinner — doing them after dinner risks lower energy and guests who have started to leave

---

**7:00 PM — Welcome Toast**
- Best man and/or maid of honor toasts — 3–5 minutes each
- Maximum 3 toasts before dinner begins; guests are hungry and need to eat
- Coordinator confirms mic handoff protocol with DJ in advance
- If a parent wants to say a few words: place it here, after the maid of honor, and confirm a 2–3 minute time limit was communicated

*Coordinator tip: Toast speakers are rarely given guidance on length. Brief every toast speaker at least one week before the wedding: 3–4 minutes is ideal, 5 minutes is the hard maximum. Practice readings with a timer. A 10-minute toast is a reception-killer regardless of how heartfelt it is.*

---

**7:10 PM — Dinner Service Begins**
- Catering begins service: buffet lines open or first course is plated and served
- DJ transitions to dinner background music — curated and conversational, not dance music
- Coordinator ensures the couple has a full plate and is sitting down eating by 7:15 PM at the latest
- Make it a personal mission to ensure the couple eats dinner — they often do not, and they always regret it
- If tables are released to the buffet, DJ or coordinator manages the release in table order to avoid crowding

*Coordinator tip: During dinner, do a full vendor check-in loop. Catering: on track? Any issues? Bar: fully stocked? Any unexpected demand? DJ: all good? Any changes? Photographer: needs anything? This 5-minute loop prevents small problems from becoming large ones.*

---

**8:15 PM — Cake Cutting**
- Alert photographer and videographer 5 minutes in advance
- Alert DJ to prepare the announcement
- Walk couple to the cake at the exact planned time
- DJ announces the cake cutting moment
- Photographer captures from multiple angles
- Catering team begins cutting and distributing cake to tables immediately after the ceremony cut
- Cake service to all tables should be complete within 15–20 minutes

*Coordinator tip: Confirm with the catering team in advance how they want to handle the top tier of the cake — confirm it will be boxed and labeled with the couple's name before the end of the night.*

---

**8:30 PM — Open Dancing Begins**
- DJ transitions to the first dance floor set — typically a high-energy opener designed to pull people onto the floor
- Cake service continues as guests begin dancing
- Coordinator steps back from active management; DJ is now leading the entertainment
- Monitor: Is the energy high? Is the floor populated? Communicate any concerns to the DJ discreetly and briefly

---

**10:30 PM — Late Night Snack (If Applicable)**
- Late night food station opens: mini sliders, mac and cheese, pizza bites, pretzels, nachos, or a personally meaningful food choice for the couple
- DJ announces the late night snack availability
- Late night snacks re-energize guests, keep them on-site for the remaining hour, and are universally beloved

---

**11:45 PM — End-of-Night Prep Begins**
- Coordinator begins collecting couple's personal items:
  - Card box and all cash envelopes or gift cards (hand directly to a designated trusted family member)
  - Top tier of wedding cake (confirm caterer has boxed and labeled it)
  - Personal decor items: frames, signs, candles, favors the couple wants to keep
  - Guestbook
  - Toss bouquet if the couple wants to keep it
  - Bride's personal bag or emergency kit
  - Any rented items the couple is personally responsible for returning
- Guest transportation: confirm the first shuttle run is departing or valet is actively retrieving cars

---

**11:50 PM — Exit Preparation**
- DJ plays the second-to-last song
- Coordinator distributes exit props to guests: sparklers (with individual lighters or a long lighter), bubbles, ribbon wands, dried flower petals
- Begin staging guests in the exit formation — this takes more time than expected; allow at least 10 minutes for sparkler exits
- Photographer and videographer confirm their positions for the exit
- Brief guests on the exit plan: line up on both sides, hold sparklers up high and angled outward, light only when the coordinator gives the signal

*Coordinator tip: A sparkler exit requires every guest to have a lighter that works, at the same moment, with the wind cooperating. Pre-light three sparklers from your own source, then have guests light from those. It is significantly faster than 60 people with individual lighters.*

---

**11:55 PM — Last Dance**
- DJ announces the last dance by name
- Couple takes the floor together; all guests are invited to join
- This is a memory — let the DJ choose a song that fills the room

---

**12:00 AM — Grand Exit**
- Couple exits through the line of guests with their chosen exit element
- Photographer captures from multiple angles
- Videographer leads the couple out, walking backward, to capture the full sequence
- The exit is complete when the couple reaches their transportation

---

**12:05 AM — Couple Departs, Venue Breakdown**
- Couple departs in their getaway vehicle
- Coordinator confirms couple has all personal items, marriage license, and card box before they leave
- Vendor breakdown begins
- Coordinator conducts a final sweep of all rooms: ceremony space, reception room, cocktail hour area, bridal suite, and restrooms
- All items belonging to the couple are accounted for
- Coordinator confirms with venue staff that there are no post-event issues before departing

---

## SECTION 4: STANDARD RECEPTION TIMELINE (4 HOURS)

### Reception Only — Grand Entrance Through Send-Off

**Format:** Ceremony took place elsewhere (at a church, courthouse, or separate outdoor location). Guests arrive at the reception venue where cocktail hour is already in progress. 4-hour reception window.
**Sample Cocktail Hour Start:** 6:00 PM
**Sample Reception End:** 10:00 PM

**Note on 4-hour receptions:** Four hours is a manageable but tight reception format. The biggest mistake coordinators make is running dinner too long, which leaves under an hour for open dancing. Keep toasts to three or fewer, move dinner service efficiently, and cut the cake no later than 8:15 PM. Four hours done well is a complete, satisfying celebration. Four hours done poorly feels rushed.

---

### Hour 1: Cocktail Hour (6:00 PM – 7:00 PM)

**6:00 PM — Cocktail Hour Opens**
- Guests arrive directly to the cocktail hour space
- Passed appetizers begin immediately — do not let guests stand in a bar line with nothing in their hands for more than 5 minutes
- Bar opens to full service
- Cocktail hour stations open if applicable: charcuterie board, raw bar station, cheese display
- Background music at a conversational volume — guests should be able to hear each other easily

**6:00–6:30 PM — Couple Completes Portrait Work**
- If the couple has not yet completed all portrait work (which may be the case if the ceremony was elsewhere and travel was required), the photographer uses this window for any remaining couple or wedding party portraits
- Couple typically misses 30–45 minutes of cocktail hour — this is expected and normal; communicate this to the couple in advance
- Coordinator manages the reception venue during the couple's absence

**6:45 PM — DJ Announcement: Guests Move to Reception Room**
- DJ begins transitioning guests toward the reception room
- Guests find their seats per the seating chart and escort cards
- Reception room fully operational: centerpieces, place settings, ambient lighting confirmed

**6:55 PM — Wedding Party Staged for Grand Entrance**
- Wedding party gathered in the entrance hallway in correct order
- Coordinator confirms name pronunciations with DJ one final time
- Couple in position and ready

---

### Hour 2: Entrance, First Dances, Toasts, Dinner Begins (7:00 PM – 8:00 PM)

**7:00 PM — Grand Entrance**
- DJ announces each wedding party member or pair with high energy
- Couple announced last to their entrance song
- Do not pause between the entrance and the first dance — go directly into the first dance while the energy is at its peak

**7:03 PM — First Dance**
- 3–4 minutes
- Guests remain standing, watching — do not seat them yet

**7:07 PM — Father-Daughter Dance**
- 3–4 minutes
- If the couple has chosen a medley or a shortened version, confirm the exact edit with the DJ in advance

**7:11 PM — Mother-Son Dance**
- 3–4 minutes
- Option: combine both parent dances into a group family dance (all parents join the couple) — this is increasingly common and takes only one 3-minute song

**7:15 PM — Welcome Toast (Best Man or Maid of Honor)**
- 3–5 minutes maximum
- DJ manages the microphone handoff

**7:20 PM — Second Toast (If Applicable)**
- 3–5 minutes maximum
- For a 4-hour reception, limit toasts to 2 before dinner; any additional toasts go between courses

**7:25 PM — Dinner Service Begins**
- Catering team opens buffet or begins plated first course
- DJ transitions to dinner background music
- Coordinator ensures the couple is seated and eating by 7:30 PM

*Coordinator tip: In a 4-hour reception, parent dances before dinner are strongly recommended. Moving them after dinner compresses dancing to under an hour. Done before dinner, everything flows in sequence and dancing begins earlier.*

---

### Hour 3: Dinner and Cake (8:00 PM – 9:00 PM)

**8:00 PM — Dinner Service Wrapping Up**
- All guests should have been served by this point
- Any additional toasts placed here (between 8:00 and 8:10 PM) if not completed before dinner
- Catering team clearing plates and transitioning

**8:05 PM — Additional Toast or Parent Remarks (Optional)**
- If a parent or additional speaker wants to say a few words, place it here — immediately before the cake cutting, so it feels like part of the celebration moment

**8:10 PM — Cake Cutting**
- Coordinator walks couple to the cake
- DJ announces the cake cutting
- Photographer in position
- Catering team distributes cake to tables immediately following

**8:15 PM — Cake Service Complete; Open Dancing Begins**
- DJ transitions to first dance floor set
- Open dancing begins
- This gives the reception approximately 1 hour and 30 minutes of open dancing before the exit — sufficient, but only if everything before it ran on time

*Coordinator tip: For a 4-hour reception, the open dancing window is precious. If dinner service ran 20 minutes long, you have just 70 minutes of dancing. Communicate this to the DJ so they prioritize high-energy sets and do not waste the floor with slow songs until the very end.*

---

### Hour 4: Open Dancing and Send-Off (9:00 PM – 10:00 PM)

**9:00 PM — Open Dancing in Full Swing**
- DJ reading the room and managing energy
- Photo booth if applicable — confirm attendant is present
- Bar continues full service through 9:30 PM at minimum (confirm last call time with venue)

**9:30 PM — Last Call at Bar**
- If venue has a hard last call, DJ announces it now
- Coordinator confirms with catering: all service has been completed and kitchen is wrapping up

**9:45 PM — Last Dance Warning**
- DJ announces the next-to-last song
- Coordinator distributes exit props to guests
- Exit formation begins outside

**9:50 PM — Exit Staged**
- Guests lined up in two rows for the exit
- Exit props distributed and ready
- Photographer and videographer in position
- Coordinator gives signal when all guests are in place

**9:52 PM — Last Dance**
- DJ plays the final song by name
- Couple on the floor with guests

**9:55 PM — Exit**
- Couple exits through guest lineup
- Exit is complete when couple reaches transportation

**10:00 PM — Couple Departs, Venue Breakdown Begins**
- Coordinator collects couple's personal items before they depart
- Vendor breakdown begins at the stroke of the venue end time
- Final sweep of all rooms

*Common timing mistakes in a 4-hour reception:*
1. *Toasts that run long — brief every speaker that 3–4 minutes is ideal and 5 minutes is the absolute maximum*
2. *Plated dinner service that takes too long — buffet is typically faster; if plated, confirm the number of courses and the pace with the catering manager*
3. *Cake cutting delayed past 8:15 PM — there is no room for this in a 4-hour reception*
4. *Open dancing beginning at 8:45 PM or later — you now have just over an hour of dancing, and the first 20 minutes of any dance floor require warm-up time from guests*

---

## SECTION 5: EXTENDED RECEPTION TIMELINE (5–6 HOURS)

### Extended Reception — Cocktail Hour Through Late-Night Send-Off

**Format:** Full extended reception including late-night surprises, specialty stations, extended dancing, and a deliberate send-off.
**Sample Cocktail Hour Start:** 5:30 PM
**Sample Reception End:** 11:30 PM (6 hours total)

An extended reception offers everything a 4-hour reception offers, plus genuine breathing room: a relaxed dinner pace, programming additions during the dinner hour, a proper block of open dancing with real energy buildup, and a memorable late-night moment before the send-off.

---

### 5:30 PM – 6:30 PM — Cocktail Hour (60 Minutes)

**5:30 PM — Cocktail Hour Opens**
- Bar opens to full service
- Passed appetizers begin immediately
- If cocktail hour stations are planned, they open now: charcuterie and cheese display, raw bar, specialty cocktail station, artisan bread station, or any custom option the couple has chosen
- Live musician (acoustic guitar, string duo, jazz trio) or DJ cocktail playlist at a conversational volume

**5:30–6:10 PM — Couple Completes Portrait Work**
- Couple and wedding party use the cocktail hour window for any remaining portrait work
- Golden hour portraits (typically 30–45 minutes before sunset) are the highest-value portrait window of the day
- Coordinator manages the reception venue during the couple's absence
- Confirm couple will be back and ready for the grand entrance by 6:30 PM

**6:10 PM — Couple Arrives at Venue (If Off-Site Portraits)**
- Couple returns from off-site portrait location
- Touch-up if needed: lip color, hair, any wrinkles in the suit
- Stage with wedding party for grand entrance

**6:20 PM — Coordinator Begins Transitioning Guests**
- DJ announces that guests should make their way to the reception room and find their seats
- Guests settle; ambient music plays
- Wedding party staged in entrance hallway

---

### 6:30 PM – 7:15 PM — Grand Entrance, First Dances, and Toasts

**6:30 PM — Cocktail Hour Closes; Guest Transition Complete**

**6:35 PM — Grand Entrance**
- DJ builds anticipation with a brief announcement before calling the wedding party
- Each wedding party member or pair announced with energy
- Couple announced last

**6:40 PM — First Dance**
- 3–4 minutes
- Guests remain standing

**6:44 PM — Father-Daughter Dance**
- 3–4 minutes

**6:48 PM — Mother-Son Dance**
- 3–4 minutes

**6:52 PM — Welcome Remarks (Optional)**
- Brief welcome from the couple (1–2 minutes) — a simple, heartfelt thank-you to guests for being there
- This is optional but becomes a signature memory when the couple takes the mic personally before sitting down to dinner

**6:55 PM — Best Man Toast**
- 4–5 minutes

**7:00 PM — Maid of Honor Toast**
- 4–5 minutes

**7:05 PM — Third Toast (Optional — Parent, Close Friend)**
- 3–4 minutes

*Coordinator tip: For an extended reception, you can allow up to three toasts before dinner and still maintain a comfortable pace. Total toast time should not exceed 15 minutes. Brief every speaker on their timing in advance.*

---

### 7:10 PM – 8:20 PM — Dinner Service (70 Minutes)

**7:10 PM — Dinner Service Begins**
- Catering opens the buffet or begins first plated course
- DJ transitions to dinner music — curated, conversational, appropriate for background listening while guests connect with each other
- Tables released to the buffet in order if applicable

**7:15 PM — Coordinator Ensures Couple Is Eating**
- Couple should be seated and have a full plate by 7:15 PM
- A sweetheart table or private table for two allows the couple a moment to breathe together; a head table puts them in a more social environment — confirm which they prefer and set it up accordingly

**7:30 PM — Additional Programming During Dinner Opens**
- Photo booth opens: guests can visit throughout dinner and the first hour of dancing
- Cigar bar or whiskey tasting station opens (outdoor or designated space per venue policy)
- Lawn games if the venue has outdoor access and it is appropriate to the event
- These additions keep energy high during the dinner hour without requiring the DJ to change the music flow

**7:45 PM — Between-Courses Toast or Remarks (Optional)**
- A parent of the couple or another honored guest may wish to toast between courses
- Allot no more than 3–4 minutes; DJ manages the mic
- This is the moment for any couple remarks if they did not speak before dinner

**8:05 PM — Dessert Course (If Applicable)**
- If the catering package includes a dessert course separate from the wedding cake, it begins now
- Dessert stations: macaron tower, donut wall, candy buffet, or a curated assortment of individual desserts — these can be introduced now as a transition into the cake cutting

---

### 8:15 PM – 8:30 PM — Cake Cutting

**8:15 PM — Cake Cutting**
- Coordinator walks couple to the cake
- DJ announces the cake cutting moment
- Photographer in position; videographer captures from the opposite angle
- Catering team distributes slices to tables

**8:25 PM — Cake Service Underway; DJ Transitions to Dance Floor**
- DJ begins the first dance floor set while cake is being served
- The combination of cake in hand and the first high-energy song is the ideal way to open the dance floor

---

### 8:30 PM – 10:30 PM — Open Dancing (2 Hours)

**8:30 PM — Open Dancing**
- DJ reads the room and builds the set
- The first 20–30 minutes of any dance floor are warm-up time — the floor will build; do not panic if it is not packed immediately
- Photo booth active throughout; remind guests via DJ announcement once during this window

**9:15 PM — Mid-Dancing Check-In**
- Coordinator does a sweep: bar fully stocked? Photo booth working? Catering team has cleared dinner plates? Bathrooms clean?
- Check in with the couple: do they need anything? Water? A moment?

**9:45 PM — Late-Night Surprise Reveal (If Planned)**
- Some couples plan a late-night surprise for their guests: a performance announcement, a surprise musical guest, a specialty food moment, a memory montage video, or a fireworks or sparkler display (outdoor only, with permits)
- If a surprise is planned, coordinate with the DJ on the announcement script and timing
- The element of surprise requires no guest notification in advance — just a brief, energetic DJ announcement followed immediately by the experience

**9:55 PM — Late-Night Snack Station Opens**
- DJ announces late night snacks with enthusiasm
- Late night food station opens: classic options include sliders, waffle fries, mac and cheese bites, pizza by the slice, pretzels with mustard, or any food that is personally meaningful to the couple
- Late night snacks re-energize guests and keep them on-site for the final hour
- Catering team manages the station; replenish as needed

---

### 10:30 PM – 11:30 PM — Final Hour and Send-Off

**10:30 PM — Dessert Station (Optional)**
- If a secondary dessert station was not opened earlier, consider opening one now: cookies, brownies, cake pops, or a candy jar station for guests to take home
- This is especially effective if the couple is sending guests home with a favor from the dessert table

**10:45 PM — Last Dance Warning**
- DJ plays the second-to-last song
- Coordinator begins distributing exit props to guests
- Guests briefed on the exit plan: where to line up, how to hold sparklers or other props, what to do when the couple appears

**10:55 PM — Exit Staging**
- Guests lined up in two rows in the exit area
- If sparkler exit: all sparklers lit from a coordinator-held flame, not from individual lighters
- Photographer and videographer in final position
- Couple briefed: walk slowly, pause midway for the photographer, continue to the car

**11:00 PM — Last Dance**
- DJ announces the last dance by name — this should be a meaningful song chosen by the couple
- All guests invited to the floor
- Often a slow, emotional song that brings the evening to a close

**11:05 PM — Grand Exit**
- Couple walks through the guest lineup
- Guests cheer, sparklers burn, bubbles float
- Photographer captures from multiple positions
- Videographer leads from the front, backs toward the getaway vehicle

**11:10 PM — Couple Departs**
- Couple boards their transportation
- Coordinator confirms couple has: all personal items, marriage license, card box with designated family member, top tier of cake
- Guests begin dispersing naturally

**11:10 PM – 11:30 PM — Vendor Load-Out**
- All vendors begin breakdown at the venue's designated end time
- Coordinator does a final sweep of all spaces
- Confirm no items belonging to the couple have been left behind
- Sign off with venue staff; confirm any post-event requirements (vendor return for morning pickup, etc.)

---

## SECTION 6: BRIDAL PARTY GETTING-READY SCHEDULES

### Version A: Large Bridal Party (8+ People)

**Format:** 8-person bridal party (1 bride + 7 bridesmaids or equivalent), 4-person hair and makeup team (2 hair stylists + 2 makeup artists working simultaneously), targeting a 5:00 PM ceremony.

**Key principle:** With a large bridal party, getting-ready timing is the most vulnerable part of the entire wedding day. The schedule must be sent to the hair and makeup lead at least 2 weeks before the wedding, and each person must receive their individual appointment time directly. Do not assume information will be passed correctly through a group text chain.

---

**7:00 AM — Hair and Makeup Team Arrives**
- Lead hair stylist and lead makeup artist arrive and begin setting up both stations
- Confirm the suite has: adequate outlets for all tools, sufficient chair seating for the full party, mirrors at each station, and preferably access to natural light
- Confirm the water supply (for shampooing if applicable) and towels
- Set up a comfortable viewing area for anyone not currently in a chair

**7:15 AM — Breakfast Delivered**
- Coordinator or designated bridesmaid ensures a full breakfast is available
- Coffee, tea, juice, water
- Protein foods: eggs, cheese, nuts, yogurt — not just pastries and mimosas
- Food should remain available throughout the morning; people cycle through the chairs at different times and eat when they can
- *This is not optional. Bridesmaids who have not eaten by noon in an emotional environment will have low blood sugar, elevated anxiety, and difficulty. This is preventable.*

**7:30 AM — First Two: Hair and Makeup (Simultaneously)**
- Bridesmaid 1 begins hair: 7:30 AM – 8:20 AM (50 minutes)
- Bridesmaid 2 begins makeup: 7:30 AM – 8:20 AM (50 minutes)

**8:20 AM — Second Round**
- Bridesmaid 3 begins hair: 8:20 AM – 9:10 AM
- Bridesmaid 4 begins makeup: 8:20 AM – 9:10 AM

**9:10 AM — Third Round**
- Bridesmaid 5 begins hair: 9:10 AM – 10:00 AM
- Bridesmaid 6 begins makeup: 9:10 AM – 10:00 AM

**10:00 AM — Fourth Round (Maid of Honor + One More)**
- Bridesmaid 7 begins hair: 10:00 AM – 10:50 AM
- Maid of Honor begins makeup: 10:00 AM – 10:50 AM
- *The maid of honor should be fully done with hair and makeup no later than 11:00 AM so she can be present to assist the bride during the rest of the morning.*

**10:50 AM — Bride: Makeup Begins**
- Bride's makeup: 10:50 AM – 12:00 PM (70 minutes)
- The bride's makeup appointment is always allocated the most time — never compress it
- If airbrush makeup is being used, allot a minimum of 75 minutes
- Lead makeup artist works exclusively on the bride during this window

**10:50 AM — Remaining Bridesmaids: Touch-Ups**
- Any bridesmaid who needs a second pass on hair or a minor touch-up
- Second hair stylist handles these while lead makeup artist is on the bride

**12:00 PM — Bride: Hair Begins**
- Bride's hair: 12:00 PM – 1:30 PM (90 minutes)
- Complex styles (full updo, intricate braiding, extensions blended with natural hair) require 90 minutes minimum
- Confirm this time allocation with the lead stylist at the booking consultation — not the week of
- Lead hair stylist works exclusively on the bride

**12:00 PM — Photographer Arrives**
- Photographer begins detail shots and getting-ready documentation
- Detail shots (rings, shoes, dress, bouquet, invitation) require no input from the bridal party
- Begin capturing candid getting-ready moments: bridesmaids helping each other, conversations, the emotional atmosphere of the morning
- All bridesmaids should be fully ready by the time the photographer arrives so they can be photographed looking their best

**1:15 PM — All Bridesmaids Into Dresses**
- All bridesmaids fully dressed
- Coordinator and/or maid of honor assists with zippers, buttons, and any fitting issues
- Shoes on, jewelry on, accessories in place
- Bridesmaids confirm they have their personal items (phone, lipstick for touch-ups, any items they need for the day)

**1:30 PM — Bride: Hair Complete**
- Full reveal with veil
- Photographer captures this moment — it is one of the most significant of the getting-ready sequence
- Bride steps away from the chair for the last time and prepares to put on the gown

**1:30 PM — Makeup Artist Final Touch-Up for Bride (If Needed)**
- Any final powder, setting spray, or color adjustments
- 5–10 minutes at most

**1:40 PM — Bride into Dress**
- Bride steps into the gown
- Coordinator or maid of honor manages the lacing, buttons, or zipper
- Photographer captures the full process — this is a primary photo sequence
- Veil placement: final pins secured
- Accessories: earrings, necklace, bracelet applied in order
- Something old, new, borrowed, blue if applicable

*Coordinator tip: The "bride into dress" moment is when the full emotional weight of the day typically arrives for everyone in the room. Give it space. Do not rush. Do not have phone conversations in the background. This is one of the most important 15 minutes of the day.*

**1:55 PM — Bride Fully Dressed, Final Check**
- Makeup artist: lip color touched up, setting spray applied
- Hair stylist: final pins confirmed, hairspray set

- All bridesmaids have: their bouquet, any personal items, their dress secured at the hem if long
- Bride has her bouquet (coordinator brings it from the floral cooler now)
- All personal items from the suite are collected and assigned to a designated person
- Coordinator does a final sweep of the suite before everyone exits
- Transportation is confirmed and ready

- Full bridal party loads into transportation
- Coordinator confirms headcount
- Drive to venue or ceremony location

### Version B: Small Bridal Party (4 or Fewer People)

**Format:** 4-person bridal party total (1 bride + 1–3 attendants), 2-person hair and makeup team (1 hair stylist + 1 makeup artist), targeting a 5:00 PM ceremony.

**Key principle:** A small bridal party has a more relaxed morning with less logistical pressure, but it still requires a strict start time. The common mistake is starting late because "it's just a few people" — the bride's hair and makeup still take the same amount of time regardless of party size.

## SECTION 9: TIMELINE CUSTOMIZATION GUIDE

### How to Adapt These Templates for Every Couple

No two weddings use a timeline in exactly the same way. These templates are your framework — the underlying architecture is sound, but every element needs to be calibrated to the specific couple, venue, and vendor team in front of you. This section walks you through the key questions to ask, the factors that affect timing most significantly, and the most common mistakes to avoid.

---

### Questions to Ask Every Couple During the Planning Process

The answers to these questions will directly determine how you build the timeline:

**About the ceremony:**
- What time is your ceremony contract for? Is there any flexibility on start time?
- How long does your officiant typically run for a ceremony? Have you attended one of their ceremonies before?
- Are you doing a first look, or are you keeping the ceremony as your first reveal?
- What is the processional order? Who is walking down the aisle and in what sequence?
- Are there any special elements in the ceremony — unity candle, sand ceremony, handfasting, ring warming, reading, live music performance?
- Are children involved in the ceremony as ring bearers or flower girls? What are their ages?

**About getting ready:**
- How many people are in your hair and makeup chair total, including yourself?
- How many hair and makeup artists are coming?
- What time does your hair and makeup team begin their first appointment?
- Have you done a hair and makeup trial? What was the timing at the trial?
- Where are you getting ready? Is it at the venue, a hotel, a home, or a salon?
- How far is the getting-ready location from the ceremony venue?

**About the reception:**
- How many people are giving toasts, and in what order? Have you spoken to them about length?
- Are you doing a first dance? Both parent dances? Or a group parent dance?
- Do you want the parent dances before or after dinner?
- How many courses is the caterer serving?
- Is it a buffet, plated, or family-style dinner service?
- Do you have any additional programming: photo booth, photobooth, cigar bar, lounge area, live entertainment during cocktail hour, live painter, lawn games, late night snack?
- What time does the venue contract end, and what are the penalties for running over?
- Do you want a grand exit? What kind — sparklers, bubbles, ribbon wands, flower petals?
- Do you have guest transportation (shuttle, trolley, valet)? What is the schedule?

**About family and logistics:**
- How many combinations are on the family formal shot list?
- Are there any divorced or separated parents requiring specific positioning or separate groupings?
- Are there any guests or family members with mobility limitations who need extra time or special logistics?
- Is there a receiving line planned? At the church, at the venue, or during cocktail hour?
- How far is the drive between the ceremony and reception venues (if different)?

---

### Factors That Most Significantly Affect Timeline Timing

Understanding these variables allows you to calibrate the template rather than guess:

**Venue distance between ceremony and reception:** Every 10 minutes of travel time requires 15 minutes of buffer in the timeline to account for loading, departure, and arrival logistics. A 30-minute drive between venues adds 45 minutes to the post-ceremony sequence.

**Number of people in hair and makeup:** Each additional person in the chair adds 45–60 minutes to the total getting-ready window. A 4-person bridal party with a 2-person artist team finishes in approximately 4 hours. An 8-person party with the same team takes approximately 8 hours. Always do this math at the outset and communicate it clearly to the couple.

**Family formal combinations:** Each grouping takes 3–5 minutes when managed efficiently by a coordinator who is calling names and staging groups. 10 combinations = approximately 30–35 minutes. 20 combinations = 60–70 minutes. Limit the list to what is truly meaningful.

**Number of dinner courses and service style:** A plated 3-course dinner typically takes 70–90 minutes from first course to dessert, depending on the catering team's efficiency and the size of the guest count. A buffet with 150 guests can be served in 45–60 minutes. Cocktail-style dining with no formal seated dinner compresses the dinner window entirely. Know the service format before you build the timeline.

**Ceremony length by ceremony type:** A civil ceremony with personal vows: 20–30 minutes. A non-denominational ceremony with readings: 30–40 minutes. A Catholic Mass with Communion: 60–90 minutes. A Jewish ceremony with full traditional elements: 45–60 minutes. A Hindu ceremony: 2–4 hours. Religious ceremonies require direct confirmation of expected length from the officiant — do not assume.

**Number of guests for the exit:** A sparkler exit with 50 guests takes 8 minutes to stage. With 150 guests, it takes 20 minutes or more to distribute sparklers, light them, and create the formation. Build staging time in proportion to guest count.

**First look versus no first look:** A first look allows all portrait work (couple, wedding party, family formals) to be completed before the ceremony. This means the couple goes directly to cocktail hour after the ceremony rather than spending cocktail hour doing portraits. It compresses the pre-ceremony schedule but dramatically expands the post-ceremony freedom. Without a first look, all portraits happen after the ceremony — which means the couple misses 60–90 minutes of cocktail hour. Neither choice is wrong; both have timeline implications that must be built in explicitly.

### Common Timeline Mistakes to Avoid

**1. Not communicating individual arrival times to bridal party members.**
Sending a group message that says "hair and makeup starts at 8 AM" does not tell any individual person what time their chair is. Every person in the getting-ready party should receive their specific appointment time directly. Put it in writing.

**2. Failing to account for transitions.**
A photographer does not teleport from the bridal suite to the ceremony venue. A florist does not place 20 centerpieces in 10 minutes. Every task takes longer than the task itself because there is setup, teardown, and transit on either side of it. Build 10–15 minutes of transition time around every major shift.

**3. Building a timeline where everything must go perfectly.**
If the timeline only works when every vendor arrives on time, every task runs exactly as planned, and no one needs an extra moment, it is going to fail. Every professional wedding timeline has buffers — built-in time that exists not because you expect to use it, but because you know you might.

**4. Starting hair and makeup too late.**
This is the single most common source of ceremony delays. Work backward from the ceremony start time, add up the total hair and makeup time for every person in the chair, then add 30 minutes. That is your latest possible start time. Most planners recommend starting 30–60 minutes earlier than that.

**5. Not confirming toast length with speakers in advance.**
Toast speakers almost universally underestimate how long they will speak. A person who says "I'll keep it to two minutes" almost never keeps it to two minutes. Brief every speaker with a specific maximum time, tell them you will give them a subtle signal if they go over, and have that signal agreed upon in advance.

**6. Planning the cake cutting too late.**
In a 4-hour reception, cutting the cake at 9:00 PM for a 10:00 PM end leaves only one hour of open dancing. Cake cutting should happen no later than 60–75 minutes into the reception, immediately after dinner service, so that the remaining time is dedicated to the dance floor.

**7. Not sharing the timeline with vendors in advance.**
A timeline that lives only on your phone and in your head is not a timeline — it is a plan with no backup. Every vendor should have a copy of the timeline at least one week before the wedding. Surprises on the day-of are almost always the result of vendors who did not know what was expected of them.

**8. Over-programming the reception.**
A reception packed with activities — a slideshow during dinner, a money dance, a garter toss, a bouquet toss, a shoe game, a surprise performance, a photo booth raffle, a late night food reveal — rarely feels magical. It feels chaotic. Choose two or three meaningful moments and do them well. Every piece of programming eats into dancing time and requires a DJ announcement that disrupts the flow.

**9. Forgetting to plan for the couple's basic needs.**
On an 8-hour wedding day, the couple may not eat until 7:30 PM, may not sit down until dinner, and may not drink any water from 2:00 PM onward. It is the coordinator's job to ensure the couple has eaten, has had water, and has had at least a few quiet moments together. These are not luxuries — they are professional responsibilities. A couple who has eaten and is hydrated handles the unexpected with grace. A couple who is starving and dehydrated does not.

**10. Not building in time for the unexpected.**
Something will always go wrong on a wedding day. The question is not whether — it is what, and when, and how much it matters. The coordinator who has buffers built into the timeline can absorb a 15-minute delay without the couple ever knowing. The coordinator who has a perfectly packed schedule with no room to breathe will be scrambling visibly by noon.

## PRO TIPS

**On timelines in general:**
A timeline is a communication tool, not just a schedule. Every person who receives it — the DJ, the caterer, the photographer, the florist, the venue coordinator — uses it to understand their role in a larger sequence of events. Write your timelines so that any vendor can read their section and understand exactly what is expected of them, when, and in relation to what comes before and after.

**On the 30-minute buffer before ceremony:**
Always build 30 minutes of "cushion time" into the pre-ceremony schedule. In 90% of weddings, this time will be used for something: a zipper that sticks, a boutonniere that needs re-pinning, a groomsman who is in the wrong location, a florist who needs a moment of your attention. In the 10% of weddings where everything runs perfectly on time, you use those 30 minutes to take a breath, do a final walkthrough, and give the couple a quiet moment before the ceremony begins. There is no scenario in which this buffer is wasted.

**On the golden hour:**
If your couple's ceremony ends within 90 minutes of sunset, protect the golden hour at all costs. It is the best light of the entire day and produces the most stunning couple portraits. Build a 20–30 minute golden hour portrait window into the reception schedule, between cocktail hour and the grand entrance. Guests will not miss the couple for 20 minutes if they have drinks and appetizers. The photographs from those 20 minutes will be the ones framed and displayed for the next 40 years.

**On vendor relationships:**
A coordinator who treats vendors as partners rather than hired hands gets better results on the wedding day. Learn vendors' names. Thank them explicitly. When the DJ pulls off the perfect transition or the caterer executes a flawless service, acknowledge it. Vendors who feel respected and valued go above and beyond. The wedding day is a team effort, and the team performs better when the coordinator leads with respect.

**On the couple:**
Your job is not to execute a perfect timeline. Your job is to give the couple a perfect wedding day. Sometimes those are the same thing. Sometimes they require you to set aside the timeline and handle the unexpected with grace and creativity. The timeline is the means; the couple's experience is the end. Never lose sight of that.

**On your own energy:**
A wedding day is a 12–16 hour day on your feet in often extreme conditions. Eat a real meal before you arrive. Wear comfortable shoes. Bring water and snacks. Identify the moments in the day when you can sit for 5 minutes and take them. A coordinator who is depleted by 8:00 PM cannot serve the couple or the vendors at the level they deserve. Your physical preparation is part of your professional preparation.

**On handing off:**
If you are working with an assistant coordinator, the most important thing you can do is give them a specific job with specific authority. "You own the bridal suite and getting-ready schedule from 8 AM to 2 PM" is a clear assignment. "Help me out today" is not. A well-briefed assistant coordinator extends your capacity dramatically; an under-briefed one creates more work.

**On post-wedding follow-up:**
Within 48 hours of the wedding, send a brief email to every vendor thanking them for their work and noting one specific thing they did exceptionally well. This takes 20 minutes and builds vendor relationships that last for years. Those relationships become referrals, priority booking, and the professional network that sustains your business. The wedding day is the product; the relationship is the business.
