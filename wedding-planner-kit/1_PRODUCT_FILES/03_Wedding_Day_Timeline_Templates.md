# Wedding Day Timeline Templates

## How to Use These Templates

These timeline templates are designed to serve as your working foundation — not a rigid script. Every wedding is different, and the variables that affect timing are numerous: the distance between venues, the size of the bridal party, whether your couple is prone to running late, how efficient the catering staff is, and a dozen other factors specific to each event. Start with the framework that most closely matches your wedding format, then layer in the specific details: exact venue addresses and distances, vendor contracted arrival times, the number of people in hair and makeup, how many family formal combinations are planned, and any unique programming the couple has requested. Work backward from your ceremony time to build your getting-ready schedule, and work forward from ceremony end to build your reception flow. Share a copy with every vendor two weeks in advance and carry a printed copy on the wedding day — do not rely solely on your phone.

The single most important principle in building any wedding day timeline is this: build in buffers at every transition point, and do not apologize for them. A wedding day is not a factory floor where every task completes in exactly the allotted time. Hair runs fifteen minutes long because a bridesmaid arrived late. The florist needs an extra twenty minutes because the ceremony arch is more complex than anticipated. The photographer wants one more shot in the golden light. These are not failures — they are the nature of a day with this many moving parts and this much emotional weight. A timeline without buffers is a timeline that will fail by noon. A rule of thumb: add ten minutes to every task you think will take thirty minutes, add fifteen minutes to every vendor transition, and add a thirty-minute buffer before your ceremony start time. When everything runs smoothly, those buffers disappear silently. When something goes wrong, those buffers are the reason the couple never knows it happened.

## SECTION 1 — CEREMONY TIMELINE FRAMEWORKS

### Outdoor Ceremony (4-hour window) — Sample Timeline

The following timeline assumes a 4:00 PM ceremony start time, an outdoor venue (garden, estate, vineyard, or park), and a bridal party of six. Adjust all times proportionally if your ceremony starts earlier or later.

**12:00 PM — Venue Setup Begins**
- Coordinator arrives on-site and conducts full venue walkthrough
- Confirm ceremony space is clear and accessible for vendor load-in
- Verify restroom access, parking situation, and any site-specific restrictions
- Locate all electrical outlets and confirm power availability for DJ/sound equipment
- Identify and brief on-site venue contact; exchange cell phone numbers
- Responsible: Coordinator

**12:30 PM — Floral Delivery**
- Florist arrives with ceremony florals: arch arrangements, aisle markers, petal cones or programs, and any reserved seating flowers
- Coordinator walks with florist to confirm placement of all pieces
- Verify personal flowers are separated and labeled: bouquets, boutonnieres, corsages, flower girl baskets
- Personal flowers go into floral cooler or cool shaded area until needed
- Responsible: Florist, Coordinator

**1:00 PM — Ceremony Sound and AV Setup**
- DJ or band sound technician arrives and begins setup in ceremony area
- Run sound check for microphone (officiant), music playback, and any live instrument amplification
- Confirm processional song list and order with coordinator
- Confirm recessional song
- Test wireless lapel mic if officiant is using one
- Responsible: DJ/Sound Tech, Coordinator confirms sound check complete

**1:30 PM — Rental Company Final Delivery (if applicable)**
- Chair rentals, arch rentals, or any final furniture pieces arrive and are placed
- Coordinator verifies chair count against confirmed guest count
- Confirm aisle width is adequate for processional
- Responsible: Rental Company, Coordinator

**2:00 PM — Photographer Arrives at Bridal Suite**
- Photographer begins detail shots: rings, shoes, bouquet, invitation suite, dress hanging shot
- These shots require no participation from the bridal party — they can be captured while hair and makeup are still finishing
- Coordinator briefs photographer on timeline and first look location if applicable
- Responsible: Photographer, Coordinator

**2:15 PM — Hair and Makeup Completion Target**
- All bridesmaids should be fully ready by this time
- Bride begins final touch-ups if needed
- Getting into the dress begins: coordinator or maid of honor assists
- Bustling instructions confirmed with designated person if the dress has a bustle
- Responsible: Hair and Makeup Artists, Maid of Honor, Coordinator

**2:30 PM — Bride into Dress, Detail Portraits**
- Bride is fully dressed and ready
- Photographer captures bride getting into dress, lacing up shoes, receiving bouquet
- Bridesmaids dressed and holding bouquets for group getting-ready shots
- Father of the bride or parent first look if planned
- Responsible: Photographer, Coordinator (managing flow and timing)

**2:45 PM — First Look (if planned)**
- Transportation to first look location or designated spot at venue
- Coordinator positions both partners and steps away
- Photographer captures first look and immediate candid reactions
- Allot 15–20 minutes for first look and first spontaneous couple portraits
- Responsible: Photographer, Couple, Coordinator (traffic management)

**3:00 PM — Wedding Party Portraits**
- Full bridal party portraits: all together, bride with bridesmaids, groom with groomsmen
- Coordinator keeps bridal party on location and manages energy/enthusiasm
- Photographer works through shot list efficiently — coordinator should have a copy of the must-have list
- Target: complete all wedding party portraits by 3:30 PM
- Responsible: Photographer, Coordinator

**3:15 PM — Groomsmen Arrive at Ceremony Site**
- Groomsmen button up and arrive at ceremony area
- Ushers receive final briefing on seating protocol: who sits on which side, whether reserved rows are roped off, and how to escort guests
- Boutonniere check: all groomsmen and groom properly pinned
- Responsible: Best Man, Coordinator

**3:30 PM — Guest Arrival Begins**
- Ushers in position to greet and escort guests
- Ceremony music (prelude) begins softly
- Programs distributed at entrance or on seats
- Signage in place directing guests to ceremony space
- Parking attendants (if contracted) in position
- Coordinator at entrance managing flow, then moves to bridal suite at 3:45 PM
- Responsible: Ushers, Coordinator

**3:30 PM — Family Formals Begin**
- Immediately after wedding party photos, move into family formals
- Coordinator has called family members to staging area
- Work through list in a specific order: largest groups first, then smaller configurations
- Recommended order: full family photo with everyone, then bride's family, then groom's family, then individual parent combinations
- Target: complete all family formals by 3:50 PM
- Responsible: Photographer, Coordinator (calling names, managing groups)

**3:50 PM — Couple Released to Holding Area**
- Couple moves to private holding space — they should not be seen by guests before the processional
- Final touch-up: lip color, hair, any last adjustments
- Coordinator does final check: bouquets ready, rings with best man, officiant in position
- Groom and groomsmen move to staging position near ceremony entry point
- Responsible: Coordinator, Maid of Honor

**4:00 PM — Ceremony Begins (Hard Start)**
- Officiant takes position
- Coordinator gives DJ/musician the signal to transition from prelude to processional music
- Seating of grandparents (if planned): escorted by ushers
- Seating of mothers: groom's mother first, then bride's mother last to signal ceremony start
- Groomsmen process in
- Bridesmaids process in
- Flower girl and ring bearer
- Bride and escort
- Responsible: Coordinator (cue management), DJ/Musician

**4:05 PM — Ceremony in Progress**
- Officiant leads ceremony per the script approved by the couple
- Estimated ceremony length: 20–30 minutes for a non-religious ceremony
- Coordinator steps away to confirm cocktail hour space is ready
- Coordinator confirms caterer is prepared for cocktail hour service
- Responsible: Officiant, Couple

**4:30 PM — Recessional**
- DJ/musician receives cue from coordinator or officiant for recessional music
- Couple exits first, followed by wedding party pairs
- Guests invited to exit by rows (ushers assist)
- Responsible: DJ/Musician, Ushers, Coordinator

**4:35 PM — Cocktail Hour Begins**
- Guests directed to cocktail hour space
- Passed appetizers begin immediately
- Bar opens
- Couple pulled aside by photographer for golden hour portraits (if applicable) while guests enjoy cocktail hour — this is the optimal time for the best lighting and most relaxed couple portraits
- Responsible: Catering Staff, Bartenders, Photographer, Coordinator (directing guest flow)

### Church Ceremony (Traditional, 5–6 hour window) — Sample Timeline

The following timeline assumes a 2:00 PM church ceremony, a separate reception venue, and accounts for the logistical complexity of a traditional church setting. Church ceremonies require additional coordination with the church's own coordinator or designated church contact. Confirm all church-specific policies in advance: candle usage, aisle runner, confetti or petal restrictions, flash photography restrictions, and whether a sound check with the church's system is permitted.

**8:00 AM — Church Contact Briefing**
- Coordinator places a confirmation call to the church coordinator (or designated church contact)
- Confirm: doors open time for floral and vendor setup, sacristy access for groom's preparations, bridal room availability and its exact location, and any last-minute restrictions
- Confirm church sound system operator will be present and when they arrive
- Responsible: Coordinator

**9:00 AM — Church Setup Access Opens**
- Florist arrives and begins decorating: pew markers, altar arrangements, unity candle setup if applicable
- Coordinator on-site to receive florist and confirm placement per the approved floor plan
- Confirm kneeler placement if applicable
- Confirm program placement on seats or at entrance
- Responsible: Florist, Coordinator

**10:00 AM — Groom's Party Arrives at Church**
- Groom and groomsmen arrive at the church and access the sacristy or designated preparation room
- Boutonniere pinning (coordinator or best man)
- Final wardrobe check: ties straight, jacket buttons, pocket squares
- Coordinator confirms groom has rings (or confirms best man does)
- Groom signs marriage license if not already completed
- Responsible: Coordinator, Best Man

**10:30 AM — Photographer Arrives at Bridal Suite**
- If bridal suite is at or near the church, photographer begins detail and getting-ready shots
- If bridal suite is at a separate location, photographer is there while second shooter covers groom's prep at church
- Responsible: Photographer/Second Shooter, Coordinator or designated contact at each location

**11:00 AM — Hair and Makeup Final Hour**
- All bridesmaids should be in final stages of hair and/or makeup
- Bride in makeup chair for final application
- Breakfast or light snacks available — coordinator confirms food is present
- Responsible: Hair and Makeup Artists, Coordinator

**12:00 PM — Bride into Dress**
- All bridesmaids fully ready and dressed
- Bride steps into gown with assistance
- Photographer captures the process
- Veil placement and final bouquet delivery
- Responsible: Maid of Honor, Coordinator, Photographer

**12:15 PM — Family Formals at Getting Ready Location**
- If first look is not planned and family formals at the church will be limited, complete family portraits now at the getting-ready location
- If first look IS planned, proceed to first look before or after family formals depending on logistics
- Responsible: Photographer, Coordinator

**12:45 PM — Transportation Departs for Church**
- Confirm all members of the bridal party and immediate family are loaded into transportation
- Coordinator does a final headcount before vehicles depart
- Dress and veil are secured in the vehicle — designated person holds bouquet
- Responsible: Coordinator, Transportation Driver

**1:00 PM — Arrival at Church**
- Bridal party enters through designated door (not through the front — guests should not see the bride)
- Coordinator confirms all bridal party members have arrived safely
- Church coordinator or usher briefed on seating assignments
- Ushers in position at church entrance
- Responsible: Coordinator, Church Contact, Ushers

**1:00 PM — Parking and Guest Arrival Management**
- Church parking can be a significant logistical challenge
- Confirm overflow parking location in advance and place signage if needed
- Ushers stationed outside to direct guests and offer escort to seats
- Reserved pews roped or marked with flowers or ribbon
- Responsible: Ushers, Parking Attendants (if contracted)

**1:15 PM — Prelude Music Begins**
- Church organist, musician, or DJ begins prelude music
- Guests are seated as they arrive
- Coordinator inside managing seating flow and communicating with bridal party in holding area
- Responsible: Church Musician, Ushers, Coordinator

**1:30 PM — Doors Open, Procession Staging**
- All members of the processional are staged in the church narthex or designated hallway
- Coordinator confirms processional order with all participants
- Coordinator hands off to church coordinator for any church-specific cues
- Flower girl and ring bearer given final instructions and any props
- Responsible: Coordinator

**1:45 PM — Seating of Honored Guests**
- Grandparents escorted to seats by ushers
- Special guests with reserved seating escorted
- Groom's mother escorted by usher (often accompanied by her husband)
- Bride's mother escorted last — her seating signals the ceremony is beginning
- Doors to the sanctuary are closed after bride's mother is seated
- Responsible: Ushers, Coordinator

**1:55 PM — Groom and Officiant in Position**
- Groom and best man enter from side door and take position at altar
- Officiant confirmed in position
- Coordinator gives musician the signal: processional music begins
- Responsible: Coordinator, Church Contact

**2:00 PM — Ceremony Begins**
- Groomsmen process in (if walking down the aisle)
- Bridesmaids process in
- Flower girl and ring bearer
- All guests rise for bride's entrance
- Bride and escort process down the aisle
- Responsible: Coordinator (cues), Church Musician

**2:05 PM — Ceremony in Progress**
- Traditional church ceremony: 45 minutes to 75 minutes in length
- Coordinator steps away from the sanctuary once processional is complete
- Coordinator uses this time to: confirm reception venue is on schedule, confirm guest transportation (shuttle or valet) is in place, confirm cocktail hour setup is complete
- No photography with flash in many churches — confirm this with photographer in advance
- Responsible: Officiant, Church Sound Operator, Coordinator (off-site logistics)

**3:00 PM (estimated) — Recessional**
- Couple exits together down the aisle
- Wedding party follows in pairs
- Guests exit by rows — ushers assist
- Confetti, bubbles, or petals outside if permitted by church — confirm policy in advance, designate someone to distribute
- Responsible: Church Ushers, Coordinator

**3:10 PM — Receiving Line (if applicable)**
- Couple greets guests outside the church if a receiving line is planned
- Allot 20–30 minutes for a receiving line depending on guest count
- If no receiving line: couple and family move to portrait area immediately
- Responsible: Coordinator (managing guest flow and keeping line moving)

**3:15 PM — Post-Ceremony Portraits**
- Church exterior portraits if lighting is favorable and church permits
- Family formals — church steps are often an ideal backdrop
- Couple portraits at church or nearby location
- Target: depart church by 4:00 PM for reception venue
- Responsible: Photographer, Coordinator

**4:00 PM — Transportation to Reception Venue**
- Couple and wedding party depart for reception
- Coordinator confirms all personal items (bouquets, suits, any church items) are loaded
- Responsible: Coordinator, Transportation Driver

### Indoor Venue Ceremony & Reception (8-hour window) — Sample Timeline

The following timeline covers the most common wedding format: ceremony and reception at a single indoor venue, with an 8-hour event window. This assumes a 5:00 PM ceremony and a 1:00 AM venue end time. Vendor access typically begins at noon for an 8-hour event window.

**12:00 PM — Venue Access, Vendor Load-In Begins**
- Coordinator arrives first and confirms load-in door access with venue staff
- Venue staff begins room flip from any daytime events (confirm this is complete before florals arrive)
- Coordinator walks both ceremony and reception spaces with floor plan in hand
- Responsible: Coordinator, Venue Staff

**12:30 PM — Floral Load-In**
- Florist arrives with all florals: ceremony arrangements, centerpieces, personal flowers
- Coordinator walks with lead florist and confirms centerpiece placement
- Ceremony flowers placed: altar arrangements, aisle decor, welcome table
- Responsible: Florist, Coordinator

**1:00 PM — Catering Setup and Rental Delivery**
- Catering team begins linens, place settings, and table setup in reception room
- Rental company delivers any additional items: specialty glassware, chargers, lounge furniture
- Coordinator verifies setup against the approved floor plan
- Confirm table numbers are in place and match the seating chart
- Confirm bar setup is complete and stocked
- Responsible: Catering/Venue Team, Rental Company, Coordinator

**1:30 PM — DJ or Band Load-In**
- DJ or band arrives for setup and sound check in reception room
- Coordinate so sound check happens before ceremony space is finalized (sound bleeds)
- Confirm DJ has: full reception timeline, all song requests, pronunciation of names for grand entrance
- Responsible: DJ/Band, Coordinator

**2:00 PM — Photographer Arrives at Getting Ready Suite**
- Photographer begins detail shots and getting-ready documentation
- Venue often has a bridal suite — confirm access time with venue coordinator
- Responsible: Photographer

**2:00 PM — Officiant Arrives for Ceremony Run-Through**
- Officiant arrives at ceremony space
- Confirm sound system, mic setup, and ceremony script
- Confirm any unity ceremony elements are in place: candles, sand, handfasting cord, etc.
- Responsible: Officiant, Coordinator, Venue AV Staff

**3:00 PM — Bridal Party Ready, Portraits Begin**
- All bridesmaids and groomsmen fully ready
- Bride into dress begins by 2:45 PM if first look is at 3:00 PM
- Photographer works through: first look, couple portraits, wedding party portraits
- All portrait work should be complete before guest arrival at 4:30 PM
- Responsible: Photographer, Coordinator

**3:00 PM — Family Formals**
- Family formal photos in ceremony space or designated portrait area
- Coordinator has all family members staged and called by name
- Complete all family formals by 4:00 PM
- Responsible: Photographer, Coordinator

**4:00 PM — Final Setup Verification**
- Coordinator does full walkthrough of both rooms
- Ceremony: chairs straight, programs on seats or at entrance, aisle clear, sound confirmed
- Reception: place settings complete, centerpieces final, seating chart posted, escort cards in order
- Lighting check: confirm ceremony lighting and reception lighting ambiance settings
- Check all bathrooms: stocked with amenities if couple has provided a basket
- Check cocktail hour space: tables set, high-tops in place, cocktail hour florals complete
- Responsible: Coordinator

**4:30 PM — Guest Arrival, Ushers in Position**
- Ushers greet guests at venue entrance
- Cocktail hour space is NOT open yet — guests directed to ceremony waiting area or lobby
- Prelude music begins in ceremony space
- Coordinator at entrance until 4:50 PM, then moves to bridal party staging area
- Responsible: Ushers, Coordinator, DJ

**4:50 PM — Bridal Party Staged for Processional**
- All members of the processional gathered in staging area (typically a back hallway or foyer)
- Coordinator confirms order with all participants
- Final checks: bouquets, boutonnieres, veil, dress
- Responsible: Coordinator

**5:00 PM — Ceremony Begins**
- Seating of grandparents and parents complete by 4:58 PM
- Coordinator cues DJ for processional music
- Ceremony proceeds per script
- Responsible: Coordinator (cues), DJ, Officiant

**5:30 PM — Ceremony Ends, Recessional**
- Estimated 25–30 minute ceremony
- Couple exits on recessional music
- Coordinator opens cocktail hour space as guests exit ceremony room
- Cocktail hour appetizers begin service immediately
- Bar opens
- Responsible: Coordinator, Catering Staff, Bartenders

**5:35 PM — Cocktail Hour + Room Flip**
- Guests transition to cocktail hour in a separate room or lobby space
- Venue staff begins flipping ceremony room to reception room (if same space)
- Couple and wedding party take advantage of this time for golden hour portraits outside
- Coordinator manages room flip timeline — this is often the most logistically sensitive moment of the day
- Target: room flip complete by 6:15 PM if cocktail hour ends at 6:30 PM
- Responsible: Venue Staff, Coordinator, Photographer

**6:30 PM — Guests Called to Reception Room**
- Cocktail hour closes
- Guests directed to reception room and find their seats
- DJ plays background music as guests are seated
- Wedding party and couple staged for grand entrance
- Responsible: DJ (announcement), Coordinator

**6:40 PM — Grand Entrance**
- DJ announces wedding party members individually or in pairs
- Couple announced last
- First dance follows immediately after entrance (recommended — energy is highest right now)
- Responsible: DJ, Coordinator (staging order backstage)

**6:45 PM — First Dance**
- Couple's first dance
- Immediately followed by parent dances if planned (father-daughter, mother-son)
- Back-to-back parent dances keep the energy flowing before dinner
- Responsible: DJ, Couple

**7:00 PM — Welcome Toast**
- Best man and/or maid of honor toasts
- Keep to 2–3 toasts maximum before dinner — guests are hungry
- Coordinator confirms with DJ on mic handoff protocol
- Responsible: DJ (mic management), Coordinator

**7:10 PM — Dinner Service Begins**
- Catering begins dinner service: buffet opens or first course plated
- DJ transitions to dinner background music
- Couple eats dinner — coordinator ensures couple has a full plate and time to eat
- Remaining toasts (if any) happen between courses
- Responsible: Catering Team, DJ

**8:15 PM — Cake Cutting**
- Coordinator brings couple to the cake
- DJ announces cake cutting
- Cake cutting photos taken
- Catering team begins cutting and serving cake immediately after ceremony
- Responsible: Coordinator, Photographer, Catering Team

**8:30 PM — Open Dancing Begins**
- DJ transitions to dance floor music
- Cake service continues as guests begin dancing
- Coordinator checks in with all vendors: catering on track, bar stocked, any issues
- Responsible: DJ

**10:30 PM — Late Night Snack (if applicable)**
- Late night snacks served: mini sliders, fries, pizza, pretzels, or couple's choice
- DJ announces late night snack availability
- Responsible: Catering Team, DJ, Coordinator

**11:45 PM — Last Song Warning**
- DJ plays second-to-last song
- Coordinator begins gathering couple's personal items: top tier of cake, card box, gifts or gift cards, any rentals or personal items brought in by couple
- Guest transportation (shuttle) should be confirmed for first run
- Responsible: Coordinator

**11:55 PM — Last Dance**
- DJ announces last dance
- Couple takes the floor; guests join
- Responsible: DJ

**12:00 AM — Exit**
- Guests line up for couple's exit: sparklers, bubbles, ribbon wands, or flower petals
- Coordinator distributes exit items to guests and manages staging of the exit line
- Photographer and videographer in position
- Couple makes their grand exit
- Responsible: Coordinator, Photographer, Videographer

**12:05 AM — Venue Breakdown, Couple Departs**
- Couple departs with their transportation
- Coordinator oversees load-out and ensures couple's personal items go with them or with designated family member
- Vendor breakdown begins
- Responsible: Coordinator, All Vendors

## SECTION 2 — RECEPTION TIMELINE FRAMEWORKS

### Standard Reception Timeline (4 hours)

A four-hour reception is one of the most common formats, particularly for venues with strict end times or packages. Four hours is enough time for a full reception, but it requires disciplined timing. The biggest mistake coordinators make with a four-hour reception is running dinner too long, which compresses open dancing to less than an hour. The fix is to keep toasts to a maximum of three and move efficiently from one segment to the next.

**Hour 1: Cocktail Hour (60 minutes)**
- 6:00 PM — Cocktail hour opens; passed appetizers begin; bar opens
- 6:00–6:50 PM — Guests mingle; couple finishes portraits (couple typically misses 30–45 minutes of cocktail hour — this is normal and expected)
- 6:50 PM — DJ or coordinator begins calling guests into reception room
- Note: Do not let cocktail hour run over. If the venue is serving a second pass of appetizers at 6:45, confirm with catering that service wraps at 6:50 so guests are hungry for dinner.

**Hour 2: Entrance, First Dances, Toasts, Dinner Begins (60 minutes)**
- 7:00 PM — Grand entrance of wedding party and couple
- 7:03 PM — First dance (3–4 minutes)
- 7:07 PM — Father-daughter dance (3–4 minutes)
- 7:11 PM — Mother-son dance (3–4 minutes, or combine into a group parent dance)
- 7:15 PM — Welcome toast by best man or maid of honor (3–5 minutes)
- 7:20 PM — Second toast if applicable (3–5 minutes)
- 7:25 PM — Dinner service begins
- Note: Parent dances before dinner are strongly recommended for a four-hour reception. Moving them after dinner eats into dancing time significantly.

**Hour 3: Dinner and Cake (60 minutes)**
- 7:25–8:10 PM — Dinner service in progress; DJ plays background music; coordinator ensures couple is eating
- 8:10 PM — Cake cutting
- 8:15 PM — Cake service distributed to tables; DJ transitions to first dance floor set
- 8:20 PM — Open dancing begins
- Note: If any additional toasts are planned (parents toasting the couple, couple toasting their guests), place them at 8:05 PM, immediately before the cake cutting, so they feel like part of the celebration moment rather than interrupting dinner.

**Hour 4: Open Dancing and Exit (60 minutes)**
- 8:20–9:45 PM — Open dancing
- 9:45 PM — Last dance announcement; coordinator distributes exit items to guests
- 9:50 PM — Exit
- 9:55 PM — Venue breakdown begins; coordinator collects couple's personal items
- Note: Four hours does not leave room for a photo booth, cigar station, or additional programming. If any of these are requested, push back on the four-hour format or add additional time. A photo booth competes directly with dancing and typically costs the couple 30–40 minutes of floor time.

**Common timing mistakes in a 4-hour reception:**
1. Toasts that run too long — brief the speakers in advance that 3–4 minutes is ideal and 5 minutes is the hard maximum
2. Plated dinner service that takes too long — if the venue is doing plated service, confirm with the catering manager how many courses are planned and how long between courses; buffet service is often faster
3. Cake cutting delayed until 9:00 PM — this is too late for a reception ending at 10:00 PM; cut the cake by 8:15 PM at the latest
4. Open dancing started too late — if the first song doesn't play until 8:45 PM, you have barely one hour of dancing; most guests need 20–30 minutes to warm up, which means real dancing doesn't start until 9:15 PM — by which time guests are already thinking about leaving

### Extended Reception (5-6 hours)

An extended reception offers significantly more flexibility for additional programming, a more relaxed dinner service, and a full evening of dancing. Five to six hours is the sweet spot for most receptions: enough time for everything without guests becoming exhausted. The following timeline assumes a 5:30 PM cocktail hour start and a 11:30 PM end time (6 hours total).

**5:30–6:30 PM — Cocktail Hour (60 minutes)**
- Bar opens; passed appetizers in service
- Cocktail hour stations if applicable: charcuterie, raw bar, specialty drinks
- DJ or live musician plays cocktail music
- Couple completes any remaining portrait work

**6:30–6:40 PM — Guest Transition to Reception Room**
- DJ makes announcement; guests find seats
- Couple and wedding party staged for entrance

**6:40 PM — Grand Entrance and First Dances**
- Wedding party announced individually
- Couple's grand entrance
- First dance: 3–4 minutes
- Father-daughter dance: 3–4 minutes
- Mother-son dance: 3–4 minutes

**6:55 PM — Welcome Remarks and Toasts**
- Couple or officiant makes brief welcome remarks (optional but warm)
- Best man toast: 4–5 minutes
- Maid of honor toast: 4–5 minutes
- One additional toast (parent, friend) if applicable: 3–4 minutes
- Total toast time: 12–15 minutes maximum

**7:10 PM — Dinner Service Begins**
- Catering opens buffet or begins first plated course
- DJ transitions to dinner music — a curated background playlist, not dance music
- Coordinator ensures couple has a plate and is seated and eating by 7:15 PM at the latest
- Tables may be called to buffet in order — coordinator or DJ manages this

**7:30 PM — Additional Programming During Dinner (if applicable)**
- Photo booth opens: guests can visit during dinner and between courses
- Cigar bar or whiskey station opens (outdoor or designated indoor area)
- Lawn games available during extended cocktail/dinner period if venue allows
- These activities keep energy high during the dinner hour without requiring DJ participation

**8:15 PM — Cake Cutting**
- Coordinator brings couple to cake for cutting
- Photographer in position
- DJ announces cake cutting moment

**8:20 PM — Cake Service + Open Dancing Begins**
- Cake served to tables
- DJ transitions to first dance floor set — typically an upbeat crowd-pleaser to open the floor
- Early dancing begins; not all guests will dance immediately — this is normal

**8:20–10:30 PM — Open Dancing (2 hours 10 minutes)**
- DJ manages energy: builds up, reads the room, paces the set
- Photo booth remains open through dancing hour
- Coordinator checks in with bar — restock if needed
- Coordinator checks in with catering — confirm late night snack timing

**10:00 PM — Late Night Snacks Served**
- Late night food stations open: classic options include sliders, mac and cheese, pizza bites, pretzels, or a snack that is personal to the couple
- DJ announces late night snacks
- Late night snacks re-energize guests and keep them on site for the final hour of dancing
- Responsible: Catering Team

**11:00 PM — Last Dance Countdown**
- DJ plays the penultimate song
- Coordinator distributes exit props to guests: sparklers (with lighters), bubbles, ribbon wands, dried flowers, or whatever exit the couple has planned
- Coordinator stages guests in exit formation outside the venue if a sparkler exit is planned — this takes more time than expected; begin at 10:55 PM

**11:10 PM — Last Dance**
- DJ announces final song — often a slow song chosen by the couple
- All guests invited to the floor

**11:15 PM — Grand Exit**
- Couple exits through the line of guests with their chosen exit element
- Photographer captures exit from multiple angles
- Videographer gets establishing shot and then runs alongside or ahead

**11:20 PM — Couple Departs**
- Couple gets into their transportation (getaway car, limo, vintage vehicle)
- Coordinator confirms couple has: wedding license (if signed at reception), cards and cash envelope, personal items, top tier of wedding cake wrapped by catering
- Vendor breakdown begins
- Responsible: Coordinator

**11:20–12:00 AM — Load-Out**
- All vendors pack and remove equipment
- Coordinator ensures nothing belonging to the couple is left behind
- Final walkthrough of all rooms
- Responsible: Coordinator, All Vendors

Each person should be communicated their individual appointment time directly.

## SECTION 4 — DAY-OF COORDINATOR CHECKLIST

### Morning of Wedding Checklist (Coordinator Tasks)

These are the tasks the coordinator handles in the background on the morning and early afternoon of the wedding day. The couple should never be aware of most of these tasks — when they are handled well, the day simply feels smooth. When they are not handled, problems surface in front of the couple and in photographs.

**Coordinator Arrival at Venue:**
- Arrive a minimum of 30 minutes before the first vendor is scheduled to arrive
- Bring printed copies of: full timeline, vendor contact sheet, floor plan, seating chart, ceremony order of events, processional order, song list, and any venue-specific notes
- Bring emergency kit (see below)
- Conduct full venue walkthrough before any vendors arrive: check all spaces, note anything out of place, identify any issues that need to be addressed with venue staff immediately

**Venue Walkthrough Checklist:**
- Ceremony space is clean and clear of any items from previous events
- All chairs or pews are in place and correctly configured per the floor plan
- Aisle is clear and correct width
- Sound system is accessible and operational
- Any candle holders, pedestals, or ceremony structures are in place (if venue-provided)
- Reception room is cleared and ready for vendor setup
- Cocktail hour space is accessible
- Bridal suite is clean, has adequate seating, mirrors, and outlets
- Restrooms are clean and stocked (notify venue staff if not)
- Parking area is accessible and any signage is in place
- Load-in access door is unlocked

**Emergency Kit — Confirm Contents Before Leaving for Venue:**
- Safety pins (assorted sizes, minimum 20)
- Clear elastic hair ties (10+)
- Bobby pins (50+)
- Fashion tape (2 rolls)
- Stain remover pen or Tide-to-Go stick
- Pain reliever (ibuprofen and acetaminophen)
- Antacid tablets
- Blister bandages (at least 6)
- Regular bandages (assorted)
- Sewing kit with white, ivory, and black thread
- Small scissors
- Hem tape (iron-on and no-iron)
- Clear nail polish (for stocking runs)
- Breath mints
- Straws (for the bride to drink without ruining lipstick)
- Static spray or dryer sheets (for static cling)
- Phone charger and portable power bank
- Scissors
- Pen and Sharpie marker
- Floral tape (if florals may need adjustment)
- Superglue (for shoe emergencies and decor fixes)
- Ribbon or twine
- Extra programs
- Lighter (for candles)
- Hand sanitizer
- Tissues

**Vendor Check-In Protocol (as each vendor arrives):**
- Greet vendor by name
- Confirm their contracted arrival time was met or note if they were late (for your records)
- Walk vendor to their setup area if they are new to the venue
- Confirm vendor contact cell phone number for the day
- Reiterate: you are the primary point of contact for all day-of questions — vendors should not interrupt the couple
- Review any specific logistics relevant to that vendor (DJ: confirm pronunciation list; florist: confirm where personal flowers go; caterer: confirm dietary restriction table assignments)

**Floral Delivery Check-In:**
- Count all personal flowers against the order: bridal bouquet, bridesmaid bouquets, boutonnieres, corsages, flower girl baskets
- Confirm each piece matches what was ordered (color, style)
- Place personal flowers in a cool shaded area or floral cooler
- Confirm ceremony florals are placed correctly per the floor plan
- Confirm centerpieces are placed correctly and match the approved layout

**Catering and Bar Check-In:**
- Confirm all catering staff are on site and setup is on schedule
- Confirm dietary restriction accommodations are in place: which tables have which accommodations, how staff will identify these guests
- Confirm bar is fully stocked and ready for service at the correct time
- Confirm water is available for the ceremony (for the officiant especially, and for guests if it is warm)
- Confirm timing for each service: cocktail hour start, dinner service start, late night snacks if applicable

**Timeline Distribution:**
- Provide printed copies of the day's timeline to: head caterer/catering manager, DJ or band leader, lead photographer, videographer, officiant, and venue coordinator
- Confirm each vendor has reviewed it and has no conflicts
- Note any last-minute adjustments to the timeline and communicate verbally

**30 Minutes Before Ceremony Start — Final Checklist:**
- All vendors confirmed on site and in position
- Officiant confirmed in position or staging area
- Processional order confirmed with all participants
- DJ or musician confirmed ready to begin and has received the signal protocol
- Ushers in position at entrance
- Guest seating is underway
- Coordinator has confirmed with best man: rings are with him
- Bride has her bouquet
- All bridesmaids have their bouquets
- Groom is in staging position or with groomsmen in a private area
- Bride is in staging position (private, out of guest view)
- Coordinator confirms with bride: ready?
- Coordinator confirms with officiant: ready?
- Coordinator gives DJ the signal to begin

### Ceremony Checklist

**Coordinator Role During the Ceremony:**
The coordinator's job during the ceremony is to be invisible to guests but present for every cue. Stand at the back of the ceremony space where you have a clear sightline to the DJ or musician, the processional entry point, and the officiant. You will manage every cue; nothing should happen without your signal.

**Pre-Processional (T-5 minutes to ceremony start):**
- All guests are seated
- Bride's mother is in position to be escorted to her seat (she is always last)
- Groom and best man are in their staging position
- Officiant is at the altar
- DJ or musician is ready and watching you for the signal
- Flower girl and ring bearer are briefed and in position with their props

**Processional Order — Standard:**
1. Coordinator signals DJ: processional music begins
2. Grandparents of the groom escorted by usher (if planned)
3. Grandparents of the bride escorted by usher (if planned)
4. Groom's mother and escort (or groom's mother alone)
5. Bride's mother escorted by usher — her seating signals the ceremony is beginning; doors close behind her if applicable
6. Officiant takes position (if not already there)
7. Groom and best man enter (from side, or process down the aisle depending on preference)
8. Groomsmen (if processing)
9. Bridesmaids (individually or paired with groomsmen)
10. Maid of honor
11. Ring bearer
12. Flower girl(s)
13. Coordinator signals DJ to transition to bride's processional song
14. All guests rise
15. Bride and escort enter

**During Ceremony:**
- Coordinator stands at the back, out of sight but ready
- If unity ceremony involves lighting candles, confirm candle lighter is in position
- If ring bearer is young, have a backup plan: coordinator holds a second set of rings in their pocket
- Manage any unexpected guest issues quietly: a crying child, a late guest, a medical concern — handle without disrupting the ceremony
- Time the ceremony: note start time and watch for any significant deviations from the expected length (if officiant goes long, prepare to adjust the post-ceremony timeline)
- If a room flip is happening (ceremony to reception in same space), step out midway through the ceremony to confirm the flip crew is ready to begin the moment the recessional ends

**Recessional:**
- When the officiant announces the couple as married, prepare to signal the DJ
- Recessional music plays on the coordinator's cue, not automatically
- Couple exits
- Coordinator signals wedding party pairs to follow
- Ushers dismiss guests row by row — coordinator can assist by walking to the center aisle and gesturing to rows from the back

**Immediately After Recessional:**
- Direct guests to cocktail hour space — have ushers positioned at the path points
- Coordinator pulls couple aside if immediate family formals are happening before cocktail hour
- Confirm cocktail hour has begun (catering passing appetizers, bar open)
- Text photographer: ceremony complete, moving to post-ceremony portraits

---

### Reception Checklist

**Coordinator Role During Reception:**
The coordinator's role shifts during the reception from cue management to timeline management, vendor coordination, and problem prevention. The DJ is now managing the entertainment flow; the coordinator's job is to keep everything running behind the scenes.

**Cocktail Hour:**
- Confirm passed appetizers are in service within 5 minutes of cocktail hour opening
- Confirm bar is open and staffed adequately for the guest count
- Check in with couple: are they aware of when they need to transition to reception? Remind at T-15 minutes
- If room flip is happening, check progress at T-30 minutes and T-15 minutes
- Confirm reception room is fully ready before calling guests in

**Transition to Reception Room:**
- Confirm with DJ: they are ready to welcome guests and begin the entrance countdown
- Stage wedding party in entrance hallway in correct order
- Confirm DJ has correct name pronunciations one final time
- Give DJ the go-ahead signal when everyone is in position

**Grand Entrance and First Dances:**
- Coordinate with DJ on timing for each segment
- First dance: confirm couple knows when to take the floor (DJ will announce but give the couple a heads-up)
- Parent dances: confirm parents are present and briefed on what to do — they should come to the dance floor on the DJ's announcement

**Toasts:**
- Confirm all toast-givers are present and know the order
- Coordinate with DJ on mic handoff between speakers
- If a speaker goes significantly over time (past 6 minutes), have a subtle signal agreed upon in advance to begin the wrap-up gesture

**Dinner Service:**
- Check in with head caterer when dinner begins
- Confirm couple has food and time to eat — the couple often does not eat at their own wedding; make it your personal mission to ensure they have at least 20 minutes to sit and eat dinner
- Monitor table service: are all guests being served? Is one table being overlooked?
- Confirm timing for next programming elements: cake cutting, toasts between courses

**Cake Cutting:**
- Alert photographer and videographer 5 minutes before cake cutting
- Alert DJ to prepare announcement
- Walk couple to the cake at the correct time
- Confirm catering team is ready to immediately begin cutting and distributing

**Open Dancing:**
- Monitor the floor: is the energy high? Communicate any issues to the DJ discreetly
- Monitor vendor needs: does the bar need restocking? Does the photo booth need attendant support?
- Check in with the couple periodically — do they need anything?

**End-of-Night Wrap-Up:**
- 30 minutes before venue end time: begin collecting couple's personal items
  - Card box and any cash gifts or gift cards (hand to designated family member or secure in your bag)
  - Top tier of wedding cake (confirm caterer has boxed it)
  - Any personal decor items the couple brought: signage, frames, candles, favors they want to keep
  - Guestbook
  - Toss bouquet (if couple wants to keep it)
  - Bride's emergency kit or personal bag
  - Any rentals the couple is personally responsible for returning

- Confirm exit is staged and guests know what to do
- Distribute exit props to guests (sparklers, bubbles, ribbon wands) — this takes longer than expected, start early
- Confirm photographer and videographer are in position for the exit
- After couple departs: do a final sweep of all rooms — ceremony space, reception room, cocktail hour space, bridal suite, restrooms — for any items left behind
- Confirm all vendors have everything they need for breakdown and have your contact information if any issues arise after you leave
- Complete your vendor sign-out: note departure times
- Do a final check that the venue staff is satisfied and there are no venue-side issues that need to be addressed before you leave
