# Wedding Contract Language & Pricing Guide

## How to Use This Guide

This guide is a professional resource designed to help you build a stronger, more legally sound contract and a more compelling pricing presentation for your wedding planning business. The contract clauses in Section 1 are model templates drafted to reflect standard industry practices and common legal protections used by professional wedding coordinators across the United States. They are written in plain, enforceable language that can serve as the backbone of your own client agreement. However, these templates are not a substitute for legal counsel. Every business is different, every state has different laws governing service contracts, and the specific facts of your client relationships may require customized language that only a licensed attorney can provide. Before you use any of these clauses with paying clients, have a qualified attorney in your jurisdiction review and approve your final contract. This is not an optional step — it is a foundational investment in your business that can save you thousands of dollars and enormous stress down the road.

The pricing and marketing guidance in Section 2 is designed to help you present your services with confidence, clarity, and professionalism. The package templates and value language examples are starting points, not finished products. You should customize every word to reflect your own voice, your market, your experience level, and the specific clients you serve. As you grow your business, revisit this guide regularly — your pricing, your packages, and your contract language should evolve as your experience deepens and your market position strengthens. Use what fits, revise what doesn't, and treat this as a living framework that supports you as your business changes over time.

Coordinator will respond to scope change requests in writing within five (5) business days, and no additional work will begin until a written amendment to this Agreement has been executed by both parties and any additional fees have been agreed upon in writing. Verbal agreements to expand scope are not binding under this Agreement.

### 2. Payment Terms

**PAYMENT SCHEDULE AND TERMS**

In consideration for the services described herein, Client agrees to pay Coordinator the total fee set forth in Exhibit A. Payment shall be made according to the following schedule:

(a) **Retainer/Deposit.** A non-refundable retainer in the amount of fifty percent (50%) of the total package fee is due upon execution of this Agreement. This retainer secures Client's event date on Coordinator's calendar and is non-refundable under all circumstances, including cancellation by Client, except as otherwise provided in Section 4 of this Agreement. This Agreement shall not be considered executed, and no services shall commence, until the retainer has been received and cleared.

(b) **Second Installment.** Twenty-five percent (25%) of the total package fee is due no later than six (6) months prior to the scheduled event date. If the event date is fewer than six (6) months from the date of this Agreement, the second installment shall be due thirty (30) days after the execution of this Agreement.

(c) **Final Payment.** The remaining twenty-five percent (25%) of the total package fee, plus any additional fees for approved scope changes, approved expenses, or add-on services, is due no later than fourteen (14) calendar days prior to the scheduled event date. Services on the day of the event may be withheld at Coordinator's sole discretion if final payment has not been received in full by the due date.

(d) **Accepted Payment Methods.** Coordinator accepts payment via bank transfer (ACH), personal check made payable to Coordinator's business name, credit card (subject to a processing fee of three percent (3%) added to the transaction), Venmo Business, or Zelle. Cash payments are accepted in person only and will be accompanied by a written receipt. Personal checks returned for insufficient funds will incur a returned check fee of fifty dollars ($50.00) and must be reissued in certified form within seventy-two (72) hours.

(e) **Late Payment.** Any payment not received within five (5) calendar days of its due date shall be considered late. Late payments shall accrue interest at the rate of one and one-half percent (1.5%) per month on the outstanding balance. Coordinator reserves the right to suspend all services, including attendance at any pre-wedding meetings, vendor communications, and day-of coordination, until the account is brought current. Coordinator is not responsible for any adverse outcomes resulting from service suspension due to non-payment.

### 3. Cancellation Policy (Client Cancellation)

**CLIENT CANCELLATION**

Client may cancel this Agreement at any time by providing written notice to Coordinator. The consequences of cancellation depend on the timing of the notice relative to the scheduled event date, as follows:

(a) **Cancellation 12 or More Months Prior to Event Date.** If Client cancels twelve (12) or more months before the scheduled event date, Client forfeits the non-refundable retainer paid at signing. All other payments made to Coordinator, if any, will be refunded to Client within thirty (30) days of the written cancellation notice. As an alternative to forfeiture of the retainer, Coordinator may, at its sole discretion and upon Client's written request, offer a rebooking credit equal to the retainer amount, valid for twelve (12) months from the date of cancellation, applicable toward any available date on Coordinator's calendar.

(b) **Cancellation Between 6 and 12 Months Prior to Event Date.** If Client cancels between six (6) and twelve (12) months before the scheduled event date, Client forfeits the non-refundable retainer and owes an additional cancellation fee equal to twenty-five percent (25%) of the total package fee. If Client has not yet made the second installment payment, the cancellation fee will be invoiced and due within thirty (30) days. Any payments made in excess of the total amount owed under this section will be refunded to Client within thirty (30) days.

(c) **Cancellation Between 3 and 6 Months Prior to Event Date.** If Client cancels between three (3) and six (6) months before the scheduled event date, Client is responsible for fifty percent (50%) of the total package fee. This amount will be retained from payments already made. If payments made to date are less than fifty percent (50%) of the total package fee, the balance owed shall be invoiced and due within thirty (30) days of cancellation.

(d) **Cancellation Fewer Than 3 Months Prior to Event Date.** If Client cancels fewer than three (3) months before the scheduled event date, Client is responsible for one hundred percent (100%) of the total package fee. No refunds will be issued. If the full amount has not yet been paid, the remaining balance is due within fourteen (14) days of the cancellation notice. The parties acknowledge that at this stage in the planning process, Coordinator has incurred substantial unreimbursable costs and lost the opportunity to book another event on that date.

(e) **Rebooking Credits.** Rebooking credits are offered at Coordinator's sole discretion. Credits are non-transferable, apply only to the original Client, and cannot be applied toward gratuities, vendor fees, or reimbursable expenses.

### 4. Planner Cancellation / Force Majeure

**COORDINATOR CANCELLATION AND FORCE MAJEURE**

(a) **Coordinator Cancellation.** Coordinator reserves the right to cancel this Agreement in the event of non-payment by Client as provided in Section 2, material breach of this Agreement by Client, or conduct by Client or Client's guests that Coordinator reasonably determines to be threatening, abusive, or unlawful. In such cases, Coordinator shall provide written notice to Client and shall retain all payments received to date as liquidated damages for lost revenue and administrative costs.

(b) **Force Majeure.** Neither party shall be in default under this Agreement if performance is rendered impossible or impractical due to a force majeure event, including but not limited to: acts of God; natural disasters including earthquake, flood, hurricane, tornado, or wildfire; epidemic or pandemic declared by a governmental authority; acts of war, terrorism, or civil unrest; government-mandated venue closure or event prohibition; widespread utility failure; or serious illness, incapacitating injury, or death of Coordinator or an immediate family member of Coordinator (defined as spouse, child, parent, or sibling).

(c) **Coordinator's Obligations Upon Force Majeure.** In the event Coordinator is unable to fulfill this Agreement due to a force majeure event, Coordinator shall: (i) notify Client in writing as soon as practicably possible; (ii) make every reasonable effort to identify and recommend a qualified substitute coordinator to serve Client at no additional placement or referral cost; (iii) transfer all planning documents, vendor contacts, timelines, and notes to the substitute coordinator or to Client; and (iv) refund any fees paid by Client that exceed the value of services actually rendered to the date of cancellation, less the non-refundable retainer.

(d) **Limitation.** Coordinator does not guarantee the availability, quality, or pricing of any substitute coordinator and shall not be liable for any increased costs or differences in service resulting from a third-party coordinator assuming Client's event.

### 5. Rescheduling Policy

**DATE CHANGES AND RESCHEDULING**

(a) **Client-Requested Date Changes.** Client may request a change to the scheduled event date by providing written notice to Coordinator. Date changes are subject to Coordinator's availability and are not guaranteed. A date change request does not constitute a cancellation, provided the new requested date is within eighteen (18) months of the original event date.

(b) **Date Change Fee.** A date change fee of three hundred fifty dollars ($350.00) will be assessed for each approved date change to cover Coordinator's administrative costs, vendor re-notification, and timeline revision. This fee is due within ten (10) days of written confirmation of the new date.

(c) **Availability.** All date changes are subject to Coordinator's calendar availability. Coordinator will make reasonable efforts to accommodate Client's preferred new date but cannot guarantee availability. In the event that Coordinator is not available on the Client's preferred new date or any mutually agreeable alternative date within ninety (90) days of the requested new date, the parties may treat the change as a cancellation under Section 3, with the applicable cancellation policy applied based on the original event date.

(d) **Fee Adjustment Upon Rescheduling.** If Client reschedules to a date that falls into a different pricing season (e.g., peak season versus off-peak), Coordinator reserves the right to adjust the total package fee to reflect the pricing in effect for the new date. Coordinator will notify Client of any fee adjustment within ten (10) business days of confirming the new date. Client may accept the adjustment or, if Client does not accept the revised fee, the parties may treat the matter as a cancellation under Section 3.

(e) **Vendor-Driven Date Changes.** If a date change is necessitated by a venue or vendor closing, cancellation, or force majeure event outside Client's control, Coordinator will waive the date change fee for the first rescheduling event only, provided Client notifies Coordinator in writing within five (5) business days of learning of the vendor or venue cancellation.

### 6. Travel and Expenses

**TRAVEL AND REIMBURSABLE EXPENSES**

(a) **Local Travel.** Travel within thirty (30) miles of Coordinator's primary business address is included in the package fee at no additional charge. Travel beyond thirty (30) miles will be billed at the current standard mileage rate established by the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) for the applicable calendar year, calculated from Coordinator's business address to the venue and return. The IRS standard mileage rate is updated annually; the rate in effect at the time of travel will apply.

(b) **Out-of-Area Events.** For events requiring Coordinator to travel more than one hundred (100) miles from Coordinator's primary business address, or events requiring overnight stays, Client agrees to reimburse Coordinator for: round-trip airfare or ground transportation at the lesser of the actual cost or the IRS mileage rate equivalent; lodging for the night before and the night of the event at a standard business hotel rate not to exceed two hundred dollars ($200.00) per night without prior Client approval; parking fees and tolls incurred in connection with Client's event; and reasonable meal expenses not to exceed seventy-five dollars ($75.00) per day during event-related travel.

(c) **Pre-Approval Threshold.** Any single reimbursable expense exceeding one hundred fifty dollars ($150.00) requires Client's written pre-approval before Coordinator incurs the expense. Expenses incurred without required pre-approval may, at Coordinator's risk, be denied reimbursement by Client. Coordinator will not charge reimbursable expenses that were reasonably avoidable or that Client would not have authorized.

(d) **Documentation.** Coordinator will provide Client with an itemized expense report accompanied by receipts for all reimbursable expenses within fourteen (14) days following the event. Client agrees to reimburse Coordinator within thirty (30) days of receiving the itemized report. Late reimbursement shall accrue interest at the rate of one and one-half percent (1.5%) per month.

(e) **Assistant Travel.** If Coordinator brings an assistant coordinator to Client's event and travel beyond the local threshold is required, Client agrees to reimburse reasonable travel and lodging expenses for the assistant at the same rates described above. Coordinator will disclose the anticipated assistant travel costs to Client in advance.

## SECTION 2 — PRICING PRESENTATION GUIDE

### How to Write Your Investment Guide

The single most important shift you can make in how you talk about your pricing is to stop calling it a "price" and start calling it an "investment." This is not a gimmick — it reflects a genuine truth about what professional wedding coordination does for a client. Prices are associated with transactions: you pay a set amount and you get a set thing. Investments carry the expectation of a return. When a couple invests in a professional wedding coordinator, they get back something that is genuinely difficult to quantify: a wedding day they can be fully present for, a reduction in stress for themselves and their families, the confidence that comes from knowing someone experienced is managing every detail, and protection against the vendor mistakes, timeline derailments, and logistical crises that derail weddings without professional oversight. When you lead with the word "investment," you are framing your services honestly and setting the right expectation before a number ever appears.

Before you list a single number in your pricing guide, you should spend time establishing the value of what you do. Think of your investment guide as a short story that moves from problem to solution. The problem is that planning a wedding is overwhelming — there are dozens of vendors to manage, thousands of decisions to make, and one chance to get it right. The solution is you. Walk clients through the key ways your services protect them: the vendor relationships you've built over years, the timelines you've refined across hundreds of events, your ability to catch problems before guests notice them. By the time a client reaches your package pricing, they should already feel the pull of wanting what you offer. The numbers should feel like a relief, not a shock.

Your investment guide should include a brief introduction that speaks directly to the couple, your approach to weddings and what makes you different, your three to four packages with clear descriptions and investment ranges, a short explanation of your process (what happens after they book), and a warm closing with a clear call to action — typically an invitation to schedule a consultation call. What you should leave out: a long biography with every certification you've ever earned, a bulleted list of every single task you perform, negative framing about what can go wrong without you, and pricing comparisons to competitors. Your guide should stand alone as a complete and compelling picture of what it's like to work with you.

How you deliver your investment guide matters enormously. A beautifully designed PDF sent after an inquiry — before the consultation call — does several things a plain email price list cannot. It signals professionalism from the very first impression. It allows the couple to review your services on their own time and return to it together before making a decision. It frames you as an established business, not a freelancer who typed up a quick list. It gives you a consistent, polished document to update seasonally rather than rewriting email content over and over. If you do not have a designer on your team, platforms like Canva, HoneyBook, and Dubsado offer templates that can be customized to match your brand. Invest the time to make it beautiful — your guide is a preview of how carefully you will manage their wedding.

The timing of how you deliver your guide also matters. Send it within twenty-four hours of receiving an inquiry, before the discovery call. This does three things: it pre-qualifies the client so you are not spending time on consultation calls with people whose budget does not match your offering; it gives the couple context so the consultation is a conversation rather than a pitch; and it demonstrates your responsiveness, which is itself a form of reassurance that you will be communicative throughout the planning process. After the consultation, follow up by referencing specific details from the conversation — "Based on what you shared about your venue and your timeline, Package B would be the right fit for you because..." That kind of personalization, layered on top of a polished guide, is what converts inquiries into signed contracts.

### 3 Package Presentation Templates

#### Package A — Day-Of Coordination

**Day-Of Coordination**
*For the couple who has planned their own wedding and wants a professional to bring it all together.*

You have put in the work. You have toured venues, booked your vendors, and built the vision for your wedding day. What you need now is someone you can hand the reins to — someone experienced enough to manage every moving part so that you can stop being the event planner and start being the bride and groom.

That is exactly what Day-Of Coordination is designed to do.

Starting six weeks before your wedding date, we will step in as your primary point of contact for all of your vendors. We will review every contract you have signed, identify any details that still need to be confirmed, and reach out to each vendor to introduce ourselves and verify logistics. We will build your comprehensive wedding-day timeline — a minute-by-minute document that every vendor, family member, and member of your wedding party will receive. This is the roadmap that keeps everything running smoothly, even when the unexpected happens.

In the weeks before your wedding, we will meet with you for up to three planning sessions to walk through the full day, answer every question on your mind, and make sure nothing has fallen through the cracks. On the day itself, your lead coordinator and an assistant coordinator will arrive two to three hours before the ceremony begins and will remain through the end of your reception — up to twelve hours of on-site management, problem-solving, and presence.

**What is included:**

- Three planning meetings (in-person or virtual), typically beginning six weeks prior to your event date
- Vendor outreach and confirmation beginning six weeks out — we contact every vendor to verify arrival times, logistics, and final details
- Full wedding-day timeline creation and distribution to all vendors and your wedding party
- Rehearsal direction (up to ninety minutes at your ceremony venue)
- Lead coordinator on-site for up to twelve hours on your wedding day
- One assistant coordinator included for events with one hundred or more guests
- Ceremony coordination including processional and recessional management
- Reception coordination including transitions, announcements, and vendor cues
- End-of-night logistics: vendor load-out, gift and card collection, and final walkthrough

**Investment: $1,800 – $2,800**
*Final investment is determined based on guest count, venue complexity, number of vendors, and event duration. Contact us for a custom quote.*

**This package is right for you if:** You are detail-oriented and enjoy the planning process, you have booked most or all of your vendors, and your primary need is a trusted professional to manage execution on the day itself. You want to be fully present at your wedding, not answering texts from your florist.

*For the couple who has started planning but needs an experienced guide to carry them the rest of the way.*

Maybe you have discovered that there are about three hundred more decisions ahead of you than you expected, and you would like someone in your corner who has done this before.

**Investment: $3,500 – $5,500**

**This package is right for you if:** You have started planning but feel like the to-do list keeps growing faster than you can manage it. ### Value Language Guide

The way you describe your services has a direct impact on whether prospective clients feel the pull of wanting to hire you — or walk away feeling like they could just manage it themselves. Task-based language describes what you do. Value language describes what the client receives. Below are ten examples showing the transformation from task-listing to benefit-selling.

---

**1.**
Weak: "I create a wedding-day timeline."
Strong: "You will never wonder what happens next on your wedding day. I create a minute-by-minute timeline that ensures every vendor, family member, and wedding party member knows exactly where to be and when — so the day flows without you having to manage a single detail."

---

**2.**
Weak: "I communicate with your vendors leading up to the wedding."
Strong: "Instead of spending your final weeks before the wedding writing emails and chasing confirmations, you hand that entirely to me. I become the single point of contact for every vendor, so your inbox is quiet and your head is clear."

---

**3.**
Weak: "I am present for up to twelve hours on the wedding day."
Strong: "From the moment we arrive until the last guest leaves, you have a professional dedicated entirely to making your wedding run exactly as planned — and beautifully recovering from anything that doesn't."

---

**4.**
Weak: "I help you find vendors."
Strong: "Instead of spending dozens of hours reading reviews and sending inquiry emails into the void, you receive a curated shortlist of vendors I know personally — people who do exceptional work, show up on time, and communicate professionally. You get the benefit of my years of relationships, delivered directly to you."

---

**5.**
Weak: "I track your budget."
Strong: "You will always know exactly where your money is going. I maintain a live budget that tracks every expense in real time, flags areas of risk before they become overspending, and helps you make intentional trade-offs so you invest in what matters most to you."

---

**6.**
Weak: "I direct the rehearsal."
Strong: "Your rehearsal is the last chance to catch anything before the real thing. I run it efficiently, answer every question from your family and wedding party, and make sure that everyone — from your flower girl to your grandmother — walks into the ceremony the next day knowing exactly what to do."

---

**7.**
Weak: "I have an assistant coordinator."
Strong: "While I am managing the ceremony, my assistant is with your wedding party and family. While I am overseeing dinner service, my assistant is coordinating with your band for the first dance. You never have two things happen at once without someone watching both."

---

**8.**
Weak: "I can help with your venue search."
Strong: "Finding the right venue is one of the most significant decisions of your planning process — and one of the most time-consuming. I narrow an overwhelming field down to the options that genuinely fit your vision, your guest count, and your budget, and I know the questions to ask that most couples do not think to ask until it is too late."

---

**9.**
Weak: "I manage end-of-night logistics."
Strong: "At the end of your wedding night, you leave with your new spouse — you do not stay behind to direct vendor load-out, locate the gift card box, or make sure the caterer has your leftover cake. I handle all of it. You walk out the door as a married couple."

---

**10.**
Weak: "I have been planning weddings for several years."
Strong: "Every wedding I have coordinated has taught me something. I have managed thunderstorms that moved ceremonies inside at the last minute, vendors who arrived late, and timelines that needed to be rebuilt on the fly. When something unexpected happens at your wedding — and something always does — you want someone next to you who has seen it before and knows exactly what to do."

### How and When to Raise Your Rates

Knowing when to raise your rates is one of the most important business decisions you will make as a wedding planner — and most planners wait far too long to do it. The clearest signal that it is time to raise your rates is demand that consistently exceeds your capacity. If you have been fully booked for two or more consecutive seasons, turning away qualified clients on a regular basis, or finding yourself waitlisted for months, the market is telling you that your pricing is below where it should be. Supply and demand applies to creative services just as it does to any other market. When more clients want to work with you than you can serve, you have earned the right — and the obligation — to raise your price until demand and capacity come back into balance.

A second signal is a significant gap between your rates and those of your peers. If you are consistently twenty to thirty percent below what planners with similar experience, similar client profiles, and similar markets are charging, you are undervaluing your work and potentially undermining how prospective clients perceive you. Pricing is a signal. Clients who are looking for premium service often associate higher prices with higher quality — they may actually pass over a lower-priced planner in favor of one who charges more, because the price itself communicates confidence and professionalism. Regularly reviewing what your local and regional competitors charge is not about copying them; it is about making sure your pricing reflects the real market value of what you offer.

When you do raise your rates, the increase should be meaningful but not jarring. A rate increase of ten to twenty-five percent is typical for a planner who has been in business for one to three years; increases of twenty to thirty percent are reasonable if several years have passed without an adjustment, if you have added significant experience or credentials, or if your market has shifted substantially. Avoid making very small increases — a five percent bump barely accounts for inflation and signals uncertainty rather than confidence. Decide on your new rate, set it in your investment guide, and hold it. If the number makes you a little nervous, that is usually a sign you have set it at the right level.

When it comes to announcing a rate increase, the most important thing to understand is this: you do not need to announce it to new clients. Simply update your investment guide and begin quoting the new rate. New clients have no reference point for your previous pricing, so there is nothing to explain. Treating a rate increase as a major announcement to the general public often creates unnecessary drama and invites the wrong conversation. Update your materials, send a refreshed guide, and let your new pricing speak for itself. The right clients — the ones you most want to work with — will not blink.

For existing clients who have already booked with you at a previous rate, you honor the price in their signed contract. A signed agreement is a commitment in both directions. If a past client is rebooking for a second event (perhaps a second child's wedding or a vow renewal), you have a choice: you can offer them your new standard rate with a brief acknowledgment — "I always appreciate working with families I have built a relationship with, and I want you to know my investment guide has been updated since we last worked together" — or you can choose to offer a loyalty acknowledgment. This might be a flat discount, a complimentary add-on, or simply the goodwill of a conversation. Whatever you decide, set a clear policy and apply it consistently. Referral partners and long-standing relationships deserve acknowledgment; the form that takes is up to you, and it need not be a financial discount to be meaningful.

Perhaps the most important thing to internalize about pricing is the psychological dynamic that premium pricing creates. Clients who invest more in their wedding coordination often become your most collaborative, most trusting, and most pleasant clients to work with. This is not always true, but it is a reliable pattern: clients who have carefully considered a meaningful investment tend to respect the expertise they are paying for, communicate professionally, follow through on their commitments, and show genuine appreciation. At lower price points, you may attract clients who are price-shopping, who may not fully understand the value of what you do, or who may treat you as a vendor rather than a trusted professional. Raising your rates is not just about earning more — it is about creating the conditions for the best possible working relationships and the most rewarding work. You have built something worth paying for. Price it accordingly.
