# Client Experience & Onboarding System

## How to Use This System

In the wedding industry, your most powerful marketing tool is not Instagram, not a website, and not even your portfolio — it is the experience you give every single client from the moment they sign their contract to the moment they wave goodbye on their wedding night. Couples who feel genuinely cared for, consistently informed, and emotionally supported throughout the planning process become your loudest advocates. They leave glowing Google reviews without being pushed. They text their newly engaged friends your name before the confetti has even been swept up. They tag you in anniversary posts a year later. Word-of-mouth referrals in the wedding industry carry a level of trust no paid advertisement can replicate, because a personal recommendation from a happy bride or groom carries the full weight of one of the most important days of someone's life. When your client experience is thoughtfully designed and consistently executed, your marketing cost drops and your booking rate climbs — because your past clients do the selling for you.

This system gives you every tool you need to create that extraordinary experience from day one. You will find a complete welcome packet content guide, a full onboarding email sequence, milestone emails for every major planning phase, post-wedding care emails, and word-for-word scripts for the difficult situations that inevitably arise. Use these templates as your starting point and personalize them with your voice, your brand, and the specific details of each couple's wedding. The goal is not to send generic emails — it is to make every client feel like they are your only client. That feeling, delivered consistently, is what separates booked-solid planners from those who are always chasing the next lead. Work through each section, adapt it to fit your brand, load the emails into your CRM or email platform, and then let the system run so you can stay focused on what you do best: planning extraordinary weddings.

## SECTION 1 — WELCOME PACKET CONTENT GUIDE

### What to Include in Your Welcome Packet

A welcome packet is the first major impression your clients receive after signing. It sets the tone for the entire planning relationship, signals your professionalism, and answers the questions couples are already asking themselves. Include every section below, written in your own voice, and format it beautifully — a polished PDF sent via email or a branded digital guide both work well.

- **Your Story and Planning Philosophy**
  This section is not a resume — it is a relationship-builder. Share why you became a wedding planner, what you believe great planning looks like, and what your clients mean to you. Keep it personal and specific; couples hired you because they connected with you, and this section deepens that connection. Two to three short paragraphs is enough — warm, honest, and human.

- **Your Process Overview**
  Walk clients through the full arc of working with you, from onboarding through the wedding day and beyond. Give them a bird's-eye view of the major phases: initial planning calls, vendor research and booking, design development, logistics coordination, and day-of execution. This section reduces anxiety by showing clients that there is a clear, proven structure behind everything you do. They do not need to know every detail — they just need to know you have a plan.

- **Client Responsibilities**
  Clearly and kindly outline what you need from your clients throughout the process. This might include attending scheduled planning calls, responding to emails within an agreed-upon timeframe, making decisions by set deadlines, and communicating changes to their vision promptly. Frame this as a partnership, not a list of rules — something like "Here's how you can help us do our best work for you." Setting expectations early prevents the frustration of chasing unresponsive clients or missing booking windows.

- **What You Need From Them and When**
  Be specific about the documents and information you will need to collect: venue contract, vendor contacts already booked, guest list with addresses, inspiration images, budget breakdown, and family dynamics notes. Include a rough timeline for when you will need each item. This prevents the scramble of trying to gather critical information weeks too late, and it helps clients feel organized and in control from the start.

- **Vendor Sourcing Approach**
  Explain how you find and vet vendors — whether you work from a preferred vendor list, research based on budget and style, or a combination of both. Share your vetting criteria: experience with their venue, communication standards, backup plans, contract review practices. Couples want to know their vendors are in good hands, and explaining your sourcing process builds enormous confidence in your expertise and thoroughness.

- **Communication Preferences and Response Times**
  Be transparent about how you prefer to communicate, what your typical response window is, and when clients can expect to hear from you. If you check email during business hours and respond within 24 hours on weekdays, say that clearly. If you use a client portal or project management tool, explain how to access it and what lives there. Setting communication expectations upfront eliminates the anxiety clients feel when they do not hear back immediately and prevents the 10pm text spiral.

- **What to Expect at Key Milestones**
  Preview the major milestones of the planning process — 12 months out, 9 months, 6 months, 3 months, 1 month, and the week before. For each milestone, give a one-sentence summary of what will be happening and what they will be hearing from you. This gives clients a map of the journey and makes the entire multi-month planning process feel manageable. When people know what to expect, they stop worrying and trust the process.

- **Emergency Contacts and Day-Of Protocols**
  Include your cell phone number, your assistant's contact information if applicable, and a clear outline of how the day-of communication will work. Let clients know who their point of contact is on the wedding day, how to reach the team if something comes up, and what your protocol is for vendor emergencies, weather issues, or timeline shifts. Including this information in the welcome packet — before it is ever needed — signals to clients that you are prepared for every scenario and they are in completely capable hands.

**Conflict Between Couple and Family (What to Say)**

When a couple's family — whether a parent, in-law, or well-meaning relative — begins influencing or overriding planning decisions, your job is to be the calm, steady professional who holds the couple's vision at the center without creating a family war. Here is how to handle it.

**In conversation with the couple, use language like:**

- "I want to make sure I am always working toward what the two of you actually want. If there are moments where family input feels like it is pulling us away from your vision, just say the word and I will take the point position on that conversation so you do not have to."
- "It is completely normal for families to have opinions and feelings about a wedding — especially when they are contributing financially. Let us talk about which areas feel non-negotiable for you, and I will help us create a clear structure for how those decisions get made."
- "My job is to make sure your wedding reflects the two of you. I can be the buffer, the bad guy, or the bearer of logistical constraints whenever you need me to be. That is part of what you hired me for."

**In conversation with the family member, use language like:**

- "I so appreciate how much you care about making this day perfect. I want to make sure we channel all of that love and energy in a way that also reflects [COUPLE NAME]'s vision — because ultimately the day needs to feel like them."
- "From a logistics perspective, the direction we have already committed to with this vendor means that [FAMILY REQUEST] would require a change that is [too late / too costly / outside the contract]. I want to be transparent with you about what is still possible."
- "I hear that this is really important to you, and I am going to make sure [COUPLE NAME] is aware of your feelings. The decision on this one is ultimately theirs, and my job is to support whatever they decide."

**The rule of thumb:** Never take sides out loud. Always redirect to the couple. Use logistics and deadlines as neutral, un-emotional reasons for why something can or cannot be changed. Your professionalism protects everyone, including the family relationships the couple will have long after the wedding is over.
