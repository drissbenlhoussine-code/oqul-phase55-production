# Client Inquiry & Consultation System

## How to Use This System

Every booking starts with a first impression — and the way you respond to an inquiry sets the tone for the entire client relationship. The templates in this system are designed to move couples from curious to committed by leading with warmth, demonstrating your expertise quickly, and guiding them naturally toward a consultation call. When you receive an inquiry, your goal is not simply to answer questions — it is to open a conversation. Use the email templates in Section 1 to respond within 24 hours, ideally within a few hours, because couples are typically reaching out to multiple planners at once. A fast, thoughtful, personalized response immediately differentiates you. Customize each template with specific details from their inquiry — mention their venue if they listed one, reference their date, acknowledge something unique about what they shared. Generic responses get ignored; personal ones get booked.

The consultation call is where inquiries become clients, and it works best when you treat it as a discovery conversation rather than a sales pitch. Your job on that call is to listen more than you speak, ask questions that surface their real priorities and anxieties, and then reflect their needs back to them in the context of what you offer. The scripts in Section 2 walk you through a complete call structure — from the opening to the close — so you feel confident and prepared regardless of the couple's situation. After the call, the follow-up templates in Sections 3 and 4 keep communication professional, warm, and clear so that nothing falls through the cracks between inquiry and wedding day.

## SECTION 2 — CONSULTATION CALL SCRIPT

---

### Pre-Call Checklist

Before joining the consultation call, review and confirm the following:

- Read the couple's original inquiry in full and note any specific details they mentioned — venue name, aesthetic preferences, concerns, or anything personal they shared about their relationship or vision
- Look up their venue if one was named — familiarize yourself with the layout, typical capacity, any logistical quirks, and whether you have existing vendor relationships there
- Note their wedding date and calculate how many months away it is — this shapes urgency and what planning milestones should already be in motion
- Check your calendar for your actual availability on their wedding date and the surrounding days before the call, so you can give an honest answer if they ask
- Review any social media or website they linked in their inquiry to get a sense of their aesthetic sensibility
- Note the names of both people in the couple and any details about how they refer to their relationship — make sure you use the right names and terms throughout the call
- Prepare your service menu or package descriptions so you can speak to them fluently without reading directly from a document
- Have your baseline pricing ready so you can speak to ranges confidently and without hesitation
- Identify two or three recent weddings in your portfolio that are stylistically or logistically similar to theirs — you may want to reference these during the call
- Set a timer reminder so the call doesn't run past 45 minutes without intentional extension
- Have a notepad or open document ready to capture responses to your discovery questions — these notes will inform the proposal you send afterward
- Quiet your environment and test your audio and video if the call is on video — you want to show up looking and sounding professional

---

### Call Opening

"Hi, and welcome — I'm so glad we could make this work! Before we dive in, I just want to say congratulations again on your engagement. This call is really just a chance for us to get to know each other a little, hear more about your day, and figure out together whether what I do is a great match for what you need. There's no agenda, no pressure — I just genuinely love hearing about people's weddings.

I thought we'd spend the first part of the call with me asking you some questions about your vision, your timeline, and where you are in the planning process — because the more I understand about you and your day, the better I can explain how I'd actually be useful to you. Great. So — tell me about your wedding. Where are you two at right now? How did the engagement happen? I'd love to hear the story."

[Let them talk. Listen actively. Take notes. This unscripted opening moment tells you more about their communication style, priorities, and emotional relationship to the planning process than any formal question will.]

---

### Discovery Questions

Use the following twelve questions to guide the conversation after the opening. You do not need to ask them in rigid order — let the conversation flow naturally and check them off as they come up organically. Each question is followed by a note on what the answer reveals.

**1. "What does your ideal wedding day feel like — not just look like, but feel like? If you close your eyes and picture it, what emotion are you hoping everyone walks away with?"**
This reveals whether the couple is aesthetics-driven or experience-driven, and how emotionally connected they are to the event versus the logistics. Couples who lead with feelings are often easier to work with creatively; couples who lead with details may need more structure and reassurance.

**2. "Have you set a working budget yet, and do you feel comfortable sharing a general range with me?"**
Budget is the single most important practical data point in the conversation. Asking it warmly and framing it as "working budget" rather than "final budget" lowers resistance. Their answer — or their discomfort answering — tells you how financially prepared they are and whether you'll need to do budget education during the planning process.

**3. "How did you hear about me, and what made you reach out?"**
This tells you where your marketing is working and, more importantly, what specifically resonated with them about you. If they mention a specific wedding they saw in your portfolio, or a recommendation from someone they trust, that gives you immediate common ground.

**4. "Have you worked with any vendors yet — venue, photographer, caterer? If so, who?"**
This immediately tells you how far into the planning process they actually are, regardless of what timeline they gave in their inquiry. It also lets you identify whether they've booked any vendors you have relationships with and what their taste level is based on who they've hired.

**5. "What does your guest list look like right now — and is the size something that's been easy to agree on, or is it a point of tension?"**
Guest count shapes every logistical and budget decision that follows. The second half of the question — asking whether it's easy or tense — surfaces family dynamics and potential stressors before they become surprises during planning.

**6. "Who is most involved in the planning on your end? Is it primarily the two of you, or do you have family members who are very engaged in the process?"**
This is one of the most important questions on the list. It tells you whether you'll be managing input from parents, in-laws, or other stakeholders — and it helps you gauge how much of your job will be interpersonal navigation versus pure logistics.

**7. "What's your biggest fear or worry about the planning process right now?"**
This opens the door to honest conversation and almost always surfaces the real reason they reached out. Whether their answer is "I'm afraid we'll go over budget," "I don't know how to find vendors we can trust," or "I just don't want to be stressed on my wedding day" — their fear tells you exactly what problem you need to solve for them to feel safe hiring you.

**8. "What does the planning process look like right now — are you feeling organized, overwhelmed, or somewhere in between?"**
Helps you gauge how much hand-holding and structure they need from you versus how much autonomy they'll want to maintain. Overwhelmed couples need reassurance and a clear plan; organized couples need respect for what they've already built.

**9. "Is there anything about your wedding that you know is non-negotiable — something that has to happen exactly a certain way, no matter what?"**
Non-negotiables reveal values. Whether it's a specific cultural tradition, a vendor they've already committed to emotionally, a detail that represents their relationship, or a logistical hard line — knowing these upfront prevents future friction and demonstrates that you care about what matters to them individually.

**10. "Have you looked at any other planners, and do you have a sense of what you're hoping to find that you haven't found yet?"**
This is a tactful way to understand where you are in their process and what their comparison criteria are. If they say "we've talked to a few people and felt like they were just trying to sell us," you know to stay conversational. If they say "we haven't talked to anyone else," you know they may need a little more time to evaluate.

**11. "When you picture the morning of your wedding and the moment you say 'I do' — what do you most want to feel in those moments?"**
This is an emotional anchor question, best used after you've established rapport. It's not about logistics — it's about helping them reconnect to why the wedding matters. Their answer becomes the emotional core of your proposal and is something you can reference back to throughout the planning process.

**12. "Is there anything else about your wedding, your family, your relationship, or your situation that you think is important for me to know as we think about whether we're a good fit?"**
This open invitation at the end of the discovery section catches everything you didn't think to ask. Couples frequently share critical context here — a health situation, a divorce in the family, a logistical constraint, or simply something that makes their wedding unique — that shapes how you'd approach their day.

### Presenting Your Services

After you've completed the discovery questions, transition into your services with this framing:

"Okay — I feel like I have a really good picture of where you are and what you're hoping for. Let me tell you about what I do and, based on what you've shared, what I think would actually serve you best.

There are three ways couples typically work with me. The first is full planning — that's when I'm your partner from right now through the end of your wedding night. We build the vision together, I source and manage all of your vendors, I build out your budget and keep us on track within it, I handle all the logistics and communication, and on your wedding day I'm there from the first vendor arrival to the last guest out the door. Based on what you've told me — especially the fact that you're feeling a bit overwhelmed and you have a lot of moving parts — this is the level of support that would give you the most peace of mind.

The second option is partial planning, which is a great fit for couples who have a strong start — maybe you've already booked your venue and photographer — but who need strategic guidance and execution support for the rest. I come in, take over vendor management, build a cohesive plan from wherever you are, and carry it through to the end.

The third option is day-of coordination. This is for couples who genuinely want to do all of the planning themselves and just need someone to execute it flawlessly on the actual day. I step in about four to six weeks before to do a thorough review and handoff, and then I run the day for you so no one in your family has to.

For you specifically, based on everything you've shared, I'd recommend [full planning / partial planning / day-of coordination] because [specific reason tied to what they told you in discovery]. Here's what that would look like and what it would cost..."

[Walk through the relevant package in detail, using their own words and priorities wherever possible to connect the service directly to their stated needs.]

### Handling Price Objections

**Script 1 — "That's more than we budgeted"**

"I really appreciate you being honest with me about that — I'd rather know now than have you feeling uncertain later. Can I ask: when you put together that budget number, did you include wedding planning in it, or was that figure for everything else and planning was going to be on top?

The reason I ask is that a lot of couples don't factor planning into their initial budget, and then when they realize they need it, it feels like an unexpected cost on top of everything else. But here's the thing I'd encourage you to think about: when I'm in your corner, I help you save money in other places — negotiating with vendors, identifying where to spend and where to pull back, keeping the budget from running away from you. For most of my couples, the planning fee pays for itself in the money we save together.

That said, I also offer [partial planning / a scaled version of full planning] that might fit more comfortably within your current thinking. Could we look at that option together and see if it covers the things that matter most to you?"

**Script 2 — "We're comparing a few planners"**

"That is completely the right thing to do, and I would encourage you to compare. Choosing a wedding planner is a real relationship — you're going to talk to this person hundreds of times over the next year, trust them with one of the most important days of your life, and need to feel like they genuinely get you. That should never be a decision made without comparing options.

What I'd encourage you to look at as you evaluate is not just price, but fit and process. Ask every planner how they handle vendor relationships, what happens if something goes wrong on your wedding day, and whether they've worked at your venue before. And trust how each conversation feels — because how a planner shows up in this call is how they'll show up for you throughout the entire process.

I'm confident in what I offer, and I'm not going to try to rush you. What I will do is send you a thorough proposal within 24 hours so you have everything in writing in front of you. And if you have questions after you've compared, I'm always happy to talk through them. What matters most to me is that you end up with the right planner for your wedding — whether that's me or someone else."

**Script 3 — "Can you do it for less?"**

"I hear you, and I want to be straightforward with you: I'm not able to discount the service itself, because the price reflects the actual time, expertise, and resources I put into every wedding I take on. What I don't want is to promise you something and then not be able to deliver it, so I keep my pricing tied directly to what it takes to do the job properly.

What I can do is look at whether a different service level — like partial planning instead of full planning — is actually the right fit for your situation. Sometimes couples come in thinking they need full planning but actually have more of it handled than they realize, and a lighter package genuinely serves them better and costs less. Or we can talk about what could be shifted into the 'you handle this, I handle that' category if there are pieces you truly want to manage yourself.

Would it be helpful for me to send you a proposal that outlines two or three different options at different investment levels, so you can see exactly what each includes and decide what feels right?"

---

### Closing the Call

"This has been such a great conversation — I feel like I really understand your wedding now, and honestly, I'm excited about it.

Here's what I'd like to do next: I'm going to put together a personalized proposal for you that outlines the service I'd recommend based on everything we talked about, with full pricing, what's included, and how we'd work together. I'll have that to you within 24 hours.

Once you've had a chance to look it over, I'd love to hear your thoughts. If you're ready to move forward, the next step is a signed contract and a deposit to hold your date — and I want to be upfront that I can only hold your date for a short period after the proposal is sent, because I have a limited number of weddings I take on per year and your date may be of interest to other couples I'm talking with.

If you have any questions after you receive the proposal — about anything at all — just reply to my email and I'll get back to you quickly.

Is there anything you want to ask me before we wrap up? Anything we didn't cover that's on your mind?

[Pause and let them speak.]

Perfect. Thank you so much for your time today — I genuinely loved hearing about your wedding, and I'm looking forward to sending this over. Talk soon!"
