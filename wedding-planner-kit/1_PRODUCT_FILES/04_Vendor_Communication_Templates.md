# Vendor Communication Templates

## How to Use These Templates

The vendors you work with are not just service providers — they are the people who will make or break every wedding you plan. A florist who knows how you operate, a photographer who trusts your timeline, a venue coordinator who calls you first when a date opens up: these relationships are career assets that compound over time. Professional, clear, and warm communication is the foundation of every strong vendor partnership. Use these templates as a starting point, but always personalize them. Mention something specific about the vendor's work, reference a past event you attended, or acknowledge a mutual connection. Vendors receive dozens of generic inquiry emails each week. The ones that get priority responses are the ones that feel like they came from a real person who did their homework.

Your communication with vendors also directly reflects on you in the eyes of your clients. When a vendor tells a couple, "Your planner is so organized and easy to work with," that is a referral in progress. Every email you send, every phone call you make, and every post-wedding thank you note you write is part of your brand reputation. Wedding vendors talk to each other constantly — at industry events, in Facebook groups, on styled shoot sets. Being known as a planner who communicates clearly, sends complete information on time, and treats vendors with genuine respect will fill your calendar more reliably than any advertising campaign. Treat every vendor interaction as a long-term investment, not a one-time transaction.

## SECTION 3 — VENDOR ISSUE HANDLING

### Vendor Running Late on Wedding Morning (Phone Script)

Use this script when a vendor has not arrived at their confirmed time and you are calling to get a status update. Stay calm, speak clearly, and do not telegraph panic — your tone sets the standard for how this conversation goes.

---

WHEN THEY PICK UP:

"Hi [Name], this is Sarah Mitchell — I'm the planner for the [Client Last Name] wedding today. I'm calling because your confirmed arrival was at [time] and we're now at [current time], so I want to check in and get an update on where you are."

IF THEY SAY THEY'RE ON THE WAY:

"Okay, great. Can you give me a realistic ETA? I need an honest number — not the best case, but what it looks like right now given where you are."

[Listen to their answer.]

"Okay. [Repeat the time back.] I'm going to hold you to that. Please call me the moment you pull onto the property — don't wait until you're parked, call when you turn onto the road. That way I can have [client / bridal party / room] ready and we won't lose any more time once you arrive."

IF THEY ARE MORE THAN 30 MINUTES OUT:

"Okay, I need you to understand that puts us [X] minutes behind where we need to be. Here is what I need from you: I need you to tell me right now if there is any chance you will not make it, because I would rather know that in the next two minutes than at [time]. Is there any scenario where you don't get here today?"

[If they say no, they're coming:]

"Then I'll take you at your word. I'm adjusting the timeline and notifying the team here. You need to move as fast as safely possible and call me the moment you are five minutes out. If anything changes at all — a delay, a detour, anything — you call me immediately. Are we clear?"

IF THEY ARE NOT COMING OR CANNOT MAKE IT:

"I understand. I need you to stay on the line for the next sixty seconds. Tell me everything you know that might help me find a replacement or a partial solution. Do you have a colleague who could step in? Do you have any materials or equipment I could retrieve myself or send someone for? What can you do right now to help me solve this?"

[Take notes. Do not raise your voice. Hang up and immediately move to your backup contact list.]

AFTER THE CALL — INTERNAL NOTE TO SELF:
Document the time of the call, what was said, and the vendor's ETA commitment. If they are more than 30 minutes late, text a follow-up message immediately so there is a written record: "Following up on our call at [time] — you confirmed arrival by [ETA]. I'm counting on that. Please call when you're 5 min out."
